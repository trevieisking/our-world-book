# OUR WORLD — MASTER PLAN

## Working title
**Our World**  
**Subtitle:** *The History We Know*  
**Descriptor:** *From the First Oceans to the Age of Satellites*

## Purpose

Create a serious, readable, illustrated history of Earth and humanity for one physical personal copy and a public digital/web edition.

The book is designed as an investigation rather than a catalogue of dates. Each major section should ask:
1. What happened?
2. What evidence survived?
3. How do we date or test it?
4. What does that evidence actually establish?
5. What remains uncertain?
6. What question carries us into the next section?

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ESTABLISHED** | Multiple independent lines of evidence converge; disagreement concerns details rather than the central claim. |
| **STRONGLY SUPPORTED** | Best-supported explanation with substantial converging evidence, but meaningful inferential steps remain. |
| **PROBABLE** | Evidence favours the claim, but credible alternatives remain. |
| **UNCERTAIN** | Present evidence cannot securely distinguish between serious alternatives. |
| **SPECULATIVE** | Physically/logically discussable but insufficiently evidenced. |
| **UNSUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED** | No reliable support located, or reliable evidence conflicts with the claim. |

The label always applies to the **claim**, never to a person, religion, culture or community.

## Visual truth rule

All finished artwork is original to this project. A generated image must not silently convert uncertainty into fact.

Before generating a visual, record:
- the exact page/spread;
- the purpose of the visual;
- facts that must be preserved;
- details that are uncertain;
- details that must not be invented;
- whether the result is a reconstruction, diagram, map/timeline, or artist's impression;
- the caption and evidence references.

## Chapter intent

### Front Matter — pages 001–016
Establish title, scope, contents, evidence key, uncertainty rule, reconstruction policy and reader promise.

### Prologue — pages 017–032
Open at the deep seafloor. Use darkness, pressure, hydrothermal systems and chemosynthetic ecosystems to pose the book's first question: how did life become possible? Reveal that surviving oceanic crust is comparatively young because plate tectonics continually recycles it, forcing the investigation into rocks, zircons, isotopes and fossils.

### Chapter 1 — pages 033–056
Hot early Universe, stars, chemical elements, stellar deaths, Solar System formation, meteorites and the raw materials from which Earth formed.

### Chapter 2 — pages 057–084
Accretion, differentiation, Moon formation, early crust, atmosphere, bombardment and the transition from a molten young planet toward a world capable of retaining liquid water.

### Chapter 3 — pages 085–112
Early water, ancient zircons, oceans, hydrothermal systems, tectonics and the limits of the surviving seafloor record.

### Chapter 4 — pages 113–152
The origin-of-life problem: prebiotic chemistry, RNA, metabolism-like networks, membranes, mineral surfaces, concentration and cycling environments, energy gradients and the still-missing complete bridge from non-life to open-ended Darwinian biology.

### Chapter 5 — pages 153–176
Earliest convincing biosignatures, stromatolites, microfossils, isotopes, microbial metabolism and disputed claims for even older life.

### Chapter 6 — pages 177–200
Photosynthesis, oxygen production, Great Oxidation Event, changing oceans, changing atmosphere and biological/geochemical feedbacks.

### Chapter 7 — pages 201–224
Eukaryotes, endosymbiosis, sexual reproduction, multicellularity, Snowball Earth episodes and Ediacaran organisms.

### Chapter 8 — pages 225–248
Cambrian diversification, ecological escalation, eyes, armour, predation, burrowing and complex food webs.

### Chapter 9 — pages 249–276
Plants, fungi, arthropods, forests, vertebrate limbs and the construction of terrestrial ecosystems.

### Chapter 10 — pages 277–304
Mass extinctions, dinosaurs, changing climates and continents, the end-Cretaceous impact and the rise/diversification of mammals.

### Chapter 11 — pages 305–348
Primates, hominins, bipedality, tool use, early Homo, Neanderthals, Denisovans, Homo sapiens, DNA evidence, migration and interbreeding.

### Chapter 12 — pages 349–388
Symbolic culture, agriculture, settlement, trade, cities, writing, early states and multiple independent pathways toward complex societies.

### Chapter 13 — pages 389–414
Oral memory, myth, sacred narrative, scripture, historical method, archaeology and the distinction between historical claims and theological claims.

### Chapter 14 — pages 415–440
Miracles, magic, witchcraft, ghosts, telepathy, precognition and time travel. Treat extraordinary claims respectfully and test claims according to the evidence they would predict.

### Chapter 15 — pages 441–464
Electricity, radio, medicine, relativity, nuclear physics, genetics, computers, neural decoding and technologies that would have appeared magical to earlier societies.

### Chapter 16 — pages 465–480
Rockets, satellites, communications, navigation, Earth observation, Moon exploration and humanity seeing the planet as one world.

### Chapter 17 — Modern Theories and Predictions — pages 481–504
**Working subtitle:** *What We Know, What We Suspect, and How We Could Find Out.*

Bring the chronological journey to the research frontier: modern observations, gravity, dark matter and dark energy, stellar collapse, planetary recycling, the conditions for habitable worlds, and predictions that can be tested. Connect these questions back to the book's earlier investigations of Earth formation and life's origins without replacing those chapters or presenting a speculative alternative as the accepted history of Earth.

Include a clearly attributed case study, **Trev's collapse–rotation–dark-matter hypothesis**, preserving both the author's original question about an implosion-associated dark-matter/dark-energy interaction and the narrower conventional-gravity version developed in discussion. Do not silently substitute one for the other. Credit the proposal to the author within this book, not as an established discovery or a claim of worldwide scientific priority.

Separate observations, established physical principles, conditional model results and the author's untested extension. A conceptual conjecture is not yet a quantitative predictive theory. Specify the additional equations, initial conditions, conservation checks, competing explanations, measurement precision and rejection criteria needed before a claimed new force could be tested. A disc, a fast-spinning asteroid or a planet around a stellar remnant is not by itself evidence for the proposed dark-sector interaction.

## Locked production loop — manuscript first

The preferred production order is now **write and evidence-lock the complete book first, then create artwork in a dedicated art pass**.

### PHASE A — MANUSCRIPT / RESEARCH PASS
For each target page or spread:
1. Read this Master Plan.
2. Read the current Page and Evidence Ledgers.
3. Select the next unchecked page/spread.
4. Research it from high-quality sources.
5. Record important claims in the Evidence Ledger.
6. Draft the page in final-quality book prose.
7. Keep artwork pending; the user requested a writing-only loop on 2026-10-02.
8. Defer new artwork briefs and generation until the whole manuscript is written; retain existing briefs for the later pass.
9. Fact-check every material claim.
10. Mark the page **TEXT LOCKED** when prose, evidence and fact-check agree.
11. Update the ledgers.
12. Commit and verify the completed page in GitHub.
13. Research the next page only after that checkpoint; normally complete about 7–10 pages per pass.

This phase continues through the entire manuscript before the main artwork pass begins.

### PHASE B — ARTWORK PASS
After the whole manuscript is text-locked:
1. Complete pending artwork briefs and work through the Image Ledger in page order.
2. Re-check each image brief against the final surrounding text.
3. If image generation requires explicit activation, ask: **"Trev/buddy, can you enable the image tool?"**
4. Generate only the approved, evidence-constrained visual.
5. Review it for scientific/historical accuracy.
6. Record the final caption and reconstruction/diagram/map label.
7. Mark the image approved and commit it.
8. Continue to the next image.

### PHASE C — FINAL BOOK PASS
After text and artwork are complete:
1. Assemble final page layouts.
2. Check page flow, typography, captions, cross-references and contents.
3. Re-verify frontier claims before publication.
4. Produce the digital/web edition and the print-ready personal edition.
5. Mark pages FINAL only when text, evidence, artwork and layout agree.

## Completion definition

A page is only **FINAL** when:
- manuscript text is complete;
- required sources are recorded;
- factual claims have been checked;
- any visual is generated and approved;
- caption accurately states what is known vs reconstructed;
- the page fits the narrative transition;
- Page, Evidence and Image Ledgers agree.
