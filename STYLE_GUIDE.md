# STYLE GUIDE — OUR WORLD

## Voice
Curious, serious, readable and evidence-first. The prose should feel like an investigation, not a textbook or a list of dates.

## Reader experience
Use the recurring rhythm:
**question -> surviving evidence -> how we know -> what it means -> what remains uncertain -> next mystery**

## Prose rules
- Explain technical terms on first use.
- Prefer concrete scenes and physical evidence over abstract summaries.
- Never fabricate dialogue, thoughts or sensory details for historical individuals.
- Sensory description of ancient environments is allowed only as clearly signposted reconstruction constrained by evidence.
- Avoid false certainty.
- Avoid false balance: if one explanation is much better supported, say so while describing genuine uncertainty accurately.
- Do not equate "unknown" with "anything is possible."
- Do not equate "not established" with "impossible."
- Treat religions, cultures and extraordinary claims respectfully while applying the same evidential standards to testable claims.

## Page rhythm
A standard text-heavy page should usually contain roughly 450–650 finished words depending on illustration area and layout.

A spread may be:
- Narrative
- Evidence
- How-we-know
- Map/timeline
- Contested evidence
- Full-visual chapter opening

## Dates
Use reader-friendly dates in prose and precise uncertainty ranges where scientifically meaningful. Explain:
- Ga = billion years ago
- Ma = million years ago
- ka = thousand years ago
only when specialist notation is useful.

## Names
Italicise genus/species names in final typesetting, e.g. *Homo sapiens*.

## Artwork
No third-party finished images planned. Generated reconstructions must be fact-constrained and labelled. Scientific diagrams should prioritise clarity over cinematic appearance.

## Citation workflow
Draft prose may use inline source notes during research. Final public-facing prose should use endnotes/notes rather than interrupting every paragraph with technical citations, while the repository Evidence Ledger retains claim-level provenance.

## Revision rule
If new reliable evidence changes a conclusion, update the digital edition and preserve the revision in Git history rather than hiding the change.
