# OUR WORLD — BOOK MANIFEST

**Book:** *Our World: The History We Know*  
**Purpose:** Preserve what this book is trying to do, including the author's questions about the future, without allowing prediction or imagination to overwrite evidence.  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-04  
**Authority:** Read with `MASTER_PLAN.md`, `CURRENT_TARGET.md` and the relevant chapter ledger. This file guides theme and evidence boundaries; it does not make a planned page complete.

## The promise

*Our World* follows a single long question: **how did this world become capable of producing observers who can investigate its past and deliberately change its future?**

The narrative therefore does not stop when it reaches the present. After reconstructing Earth's formation, life, evolution, humanity, civilisation and technology, the final chapter asks what may come next. Future claims must be labelled with the same discipline as claims about the past.

## Rules that do not change at the frontier

- Observation, interpretation, hypothesis, model, prediction and established theory are different things.
- An unexplained observation is not automatically evidence for an extraordinary explanation.
- A possible mechanism is not a demonstrated historical event.
- A scientific proposal grows stronger by stating what could prove it wrong.
- The book may preserve Trev's own ideas and predictions, but attribution must never be converted into scientific priority or confirmation.
- Dates, programme announcements and forecasts must be separated from completed achievements.
- Life, intelligent life, technology, a spacecraft and a living crew are separate claims.
- A world that can contain liquid water is not automatically habitable; a habitat is not automatically inhabited.
- Artificial intelligence is treated as an enabling technology, not a suspension of physics.

## Chapter 17 frontier questions to preserve

### 1. Collapse, rotation and unseen matter

Preserve Trev's question about catastrophic inward collapse, increasing rotation and a possible dark-matter/dark-energy interaction. Keep two versions distinct:

1. **Conventional comparison:** collapse or disruption redistributes ordinary matter and angular momentum while existing dark matter may respond gravitationally where relevant.
2. **Stronger author proposal:** an additional dark-sector interaction or restoring effect is triggered by collapse.

The second version remains SPECULATIVE until it has a mechanism, equations, parameter range, observable difference and rejection criterion. Similarity between an astronomical object and the idea is not confirmation.

### 2. Dimensions, hidden sectors and things we cannot see

Preserve the author's earlier thought experiment of apparently overlapping realities or sectors that might interact only weakly.

Do not merge three different meanings of “hidden”:
- ordinary light outside human sight or a detector's sensitivity;
- hypothetical particles/interactions in a hidden sector;
- additional spatial dimensions.

Rubin or another observatory seeing something previously missed does not by itself mean that it has looked into another dimension.

### 3. Alien life, technology and disappearing objects

The search for extraterrestrial life and technosignatures belongs in the frontier discussion, but the evidential ladder must remain explicit:

**unusual signal -> authenticated observation -> unresolved natural/instrumental alternatives -> evidence of technology -> evidence of extraterrestrial origin -> evidence of living occupants**

No step may be skipped.

An object disappearing from an image can mean that its detectable light disappeared, the geometry changed, the object moved, processing changed, or the source was transient. If an engineered craft were ever established, it could be autonomous, remotely controlled, inactive or crewed; a craft alone would not demonstrate biological occupants.

### 4. Machines before people

Preserve Trev's 2036–2046 prediction that increasingly capable construction robots and AI could substantially change terrestrial industry while preparing infrastructure beyond Earth.

The core sequence is:

**scout -> establish power/communications -> use local material -> construct useful infrastructure -> maintain/repair -> allow longer human operation -> expand**

Judge the prediction by repeatable operating capability, total support requirements, costs and adoption, not promotional renderings or wall-printing speed alone.

American leadership is a forecast to test, not a guaranteed outcome. China, Europe and other actors already have independent and collaborative lunar programmes. Cooperation, competition and shared standards may all occur.

“Conquer space” is treated as the author's informal phrase for increasing operational reach, not as evidence of ownership or an undisclosed military programme.

### 5. AI and autonomous exploration

AI could accelerate science, engineering, planning and local autonomy, especially where communication delays make continuous human control impractical. It can help machines respond to unfamiliar conditions and coordinate work.

AI does not remove energy requirements, travel distance, radiation, materials limits, maintenance or the laws of physics. Its historical significance should be assessed by what systems actually accomplish.

### 6. From outposts to engineered environments

Mars is an example, not the only possible destination. A future civilisation may choose worlds or moons that require less intervention.

A plausible progression to investigate is:

**robotic construction -> local resource processing -> protected ecosystems -> larger enclosed environments -> regional environmental engineering -> possible long-term terraforming**

For Mars specifically, plants and water are insufficient on their own. Pressure, temperature, atmospheric chemistry, radiation, nutrient cycles, liquid-water stability and atmospheric loss must be addressed. Microbes and plants could contribute only when conditions allow them to survive and reproduce.

Distinguish **paraterraforming**—large controlled or enclosed Earth-like environments—from altering an entire planet.

### 7. Biology as part of planetary engineering

Trev's idea of machines distributing water, nutrients, microorganisms and seeds is a legitimate future-engineering question once the physical environment is suitable.

The book should investigate whether a future system could use:
- local water and minerals;
- microbial processing of soil/regolith;
- engineered or selected organisms;
- controlled greenhouse warming;
- increasingly large ecological zones.

Do not describe present-day exposed Mars as ready for forests. Do not confuse atmospheric composition with atmospheric pressure.

Planetary protection comes first while a destination could contain indigenous life: deliberately releasing terrestrial organisms could destroy or confuse evidence before it is understood.

### 8. A 2150 forecast

Preserve Trev's conditional prediction:

> **If technological civilisation is still functioning in 2150 and has been given the grace of time, humans and their machines may already be deliberately engineering environments beyond Earth, perhaps beginning long-duration or planetary-scale transformation on worlds selected because they suit our needs better than Mars.**

This is a forecast, not a scheduled achievement. More defensible intermediate milestones include autonomous industry, resource extraction, large controlled ecosystems and environmental-engineering experiments.

### 9. Interstellar expansion is a different threshold

The logic “machines before people” could eventually extend to other stellar systems: robotic scouts and builders might arrive before biological settlers.

But present interstellar distances and propulsion remain an enormous barrier. A robot that can build a habitat has not thereby acquired a practical method of reaching another star.


### 10. The placed-humanity / reset thought experiment

Preserve Trev's thought experiment that an overcrowded precursor civilisation might deliberately place a very small number of memory-wiped humans on an otherwise empty Earth, producing a story superficially reminiscent of Adam and Eve. Treat this as an **extraordinary-claims test case**, not as an alternative history established by the book.

A recent founding from only two couples would predict an exceptionally severe genetic bottleneck, very high relatedness and rapid inbreeding unless additional genetic diversity were somehow introduced. Present human genomic diversity is not consistent with a recent origin of the entire species from four individuals. The biblical story, a literal recent genetic founding, and a hypothetical much older technological seeding event are therefore separate claims and must not be silently merged.

A more distant “humans or their ancestors were placed here” hypothesis also requires positive evidence that distinguishes it from ordinary evolution on Earth. Compatibility with a story, an unexplained gap or the mere possibility of advanced extraterrestrial technology is not sufficient. Appropriate tests would ask what genomic, palaeontological, archaeological or geochemical signature deliberate placement would leave that standard evolutionary history would not.

If discussed in Chapters 13–14, present the religious narrative respectfully while testing the empirical population-history claim by the evidence it predicts. No conclusion about theology follows from accepting or rejecting a literal genetic bottleneck model.


### 11. Galaxies we cannot see

Preserve Trev's question: **what if there are galaxies we cannot see?** This has scientifically real versions, but they must remain distinct from extra dimensions.

1. **Too faint or too diffuse for a particular survey:** low-surface-brightness galaxies can contain sparse starlight spread over a large area and be extremely difficult to detect against the sky/background. In 2026 NASA reported CDG-2, a very faint galaxy candidate/measurement in the Perseus cluster whose underlying diffuse light was revealed after a globular-cluster overdensity drew attention to the location.
2. **Known mainly through indirect tracers:** a galaxy or mass concentration may be constrained through gas, star clusters, motions or gravitational effects even when its ordinary starlight is exceptionally weak. “Dark” in this astronomical usage does not mean a portal, another spatial dimension or complete absence of ordinary matter.
3. **Outside our observable horizon:** cosmology permits a universe larger than the region from which signals have had time or causal opportunity to reach us. Such regions are different from a faint galaxy inside the observable Universe; better telescope sensitivity cannot retrieve information that has not reached us.

Do not turn observational incompleteness into evidence for hidden civilisations or inhabited extra dimensions. The scientifically productive question is: **what interaction or signal would make the unseen object detectable, and what ordinary explanation could produce the same evidence?**

## The thematic ending

The final chapter should retain this progression without presenting it as destiny:

**The Universe made stars.  
Stars made the elements.  
Those elements made Earth.  
Earth produced life.  
Life changed Earth.  
One branch of that life produced machines.  
Those machines may someday help life reach—and perhaps carefully reshape—another world.**

The ending is conditional. Time can permit cumulative technological change, but survival, choices, resources, ethics and physical limits decide which possibilities become history.
