# CHAPTER EIGHT — THE CAMBRIAN WORLD
## Page 226 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE BOUNDARY IS A BURROW

Geological periods need boundaries.

Nature rarely provides a perfect one.

The formal base of the Cambrian is defined at Fortune Head in Newfoundland, Canada.

The traditional biological marker is the first appearance of the trace-fossil species **Treptichnus pedum**.

The fossil is not the body of an animal.

It is a branching burrow system.

An animal moved through sediment.

It changed direction repeatedly.

It probed fresh sediment.

Its behaviour was organised enough to leave a distinctive three-dimensional pattern.

That makes the beginning of the Cambrian formally a boundary defined by **behaviour**.

There is something fitting about that.

The coming revolution will transform the sediment itself.

But *Treptichnus* creates a chronological problem.

Trace fossils depend heavily on environment.

A behaviour cannot be preserved where the sediment is unsuitable.

The same animal can make different traces in different substrates.

Similar traces can be made by different animals.

Treptichnid burrows also occur in terminal Ediacaran successions.

So the first local appearance of one trace cannot be assumed to equal the evolutionary origin of the animal that made it.

The Fortune Head boundary is a **stratigraphic convention** designed to correlate rocks.

It is not a claim that animals crossed an evolutionary line on that day.

High-precision geochronology has continued refining the age.

Recent work brackets the Ediacaran–Cambrian transition close to **538.8 million years ago**, with uncertainties of a few hundred thousand years in some successions.

Different calibration models can shift correlations modestly.

The biological transition itself spreads across far more time.

This creates an important distinction between three dates.

**Origination.**

The lineage evolves.

**Ecological expansion.**

The lineage becomes abundant or enters new environments.

**First fossil appearance.**

The rock record finally preserves something we identify.

Those dates need not match.

Imagine a small soft-bodied animal living in low abundance.

Its lineage can exist for millions of years without leaving an identifiable body fossil.

If it later evolves a shell, its apparent fossil “origin” becomes abrupt.

If it begins making deep burrows, its behaviour can suddenly appear across sedimentary basins.

The rock record turns an ecological expansion into what looks like a taxonomic birth.

This does not make fossils unreliable.

It tells us what question they answer.

A fossil gives a **minimum age** for the trait or lineage identified.

A trace gives a minimum age for behaviour.

A molecular clock estimates deeper divergence under an evolutionary model.

Sedimentology tells us whether preservation was possible.

The strongest chronology combines all of them.

The Ediacaran–Cambrian boundary therefore marks the start of a named geological period.

The animal radiation ignores the line.

Its roots cross from one side to the other.

But after the boundary, one ecological transformation becomes increasingly obvious in trace fossils.

Animals stop merely walking across or lightly probing microbial-mat surfaces.

They begin entering sediment.

Repeatedly.

More deeply.

In more complicated ways.

The sea floor becomes habitat rather than just ground.

That transformation is called the **Cambrian Substrate Revolution**.

And unlike a body fossil, a disturbed sediment can record the cumulative work of an entire community.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Formal base Cambrian GSSP at Fortune Head traditionally tied to FAD of *Treptichnus pedum*.
- Boundary age is currently treated near ~538.8 Ma; recent high-resolution Namibia/Ediacaran correlations bracket the transition tightly but do not make biology synchronous.
- *Treptichnus* and treptichnid traces have environmental/facies limitations and terminal-Ediacaran antecedents.
- First fossil appearance is separated explicitly from evolutionary origination.

**Decision:** the boundary is treated as a correlation tool, not an evolutionary instant.
