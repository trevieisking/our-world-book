# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 101 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### CARBON: ABUNDANT, MOBILE, AND CHEMICALLY RESTLESS

Carbon was everywhere on the early Earth.

In the atmosphere.

In volcanic gases.

Dissolved in seawater.

Locked inside minerals.

Delivered by meteorites.

Produced and transformed by geological reactions.

But carbon does not exist in one useful form.

In water, inorganic carbon shifts among dissolved carbon dioxide, carbonic acid, bicarbonate and carbonate.

Which form dominates depends strongly on pH.

Lower pH favours dissolved CO₂ and bicarbonate.

Higher pH shifts more carbon toward carbonate.

That matters because carbonate can combine with calcium and other cations to precipitate minerals, while dissolved CO₂ participates directly in atmosphere–ocean exchange.

Early Earth probably contained much more atmospheric carbon dioxide than today, although the exact pressure remains debated.

A more CO₂-rich atmosphere would feed more carbon into the ocean.

At the same time, water–rock reactions and weathering could remove carbon from the atmosphere and transfer it into dissolved ions or carbonate-bearing minerals.

So carbon moved continuously among atmosphere, water, rock and later life.

The phrase **carbon cycle** can make this sound modern.

It is not.

A geological carbon cycle existed before biology.

Volcanism supplied carbon.

The ocean dissolved it.

Weathering redistributed it.

Minerals stored it.

Metamorphism and volcanism could return it.

This does not mean prebiotic organic chemistry was easy.

Carbon abundance is not the same as organic-molecule abundance.

Carbon dioxide is chemically stable.

Turning it into more reduced carbon compounds requires energy and suitable reaction pathways.

Lightning, ultraviolet radiation, hydrothermal reactions, impact chemistry and mineral-catalysed processes can all produce reduced carbon under some conditions.

Which pathways dominated on the real Hadean Earth is still unresolved.

So the first lesson of ocean chemistry is simple:

**having carbon is not the same as having usable carbon.**

The planet had plenty of the element.

Chemistry still had to activate it.
