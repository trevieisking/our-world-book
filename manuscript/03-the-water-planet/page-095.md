# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 095 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### SEA LEVEL WITHOUT A MODERN MAP

Sea level is not simply the amount of water on a planet.

It is the height of that water relative to the shape of the basins that contain it.

Change the volume of the ocean and sea level changes.

Change the depth of ocean basins and sea level changes.

Raise or lower continents and sea level relative to land changes again.

Early Earth changed all three.

The mantle was hotter.

Hotter mantle produces more melting.

More melting can produce thicker oceanic crust.

Several geodynamic studies therefore infer that Archean oceanic crust was commonly thicker than modern oceanic crust.

That matters because a thicker, more buoyant seafloor reduces the depth available for ocean water.

At the same time, the volume and elevation of continental crust were different from today's.

Some early landmasses may have been small, volcanic and short-lived.

Stable cratonic regions emerged later and expanded over time.

The result is a problem with several moving parts:

**ocean-water volume + basin depth + crustal buoyancy + land elevation = exposed land and sea level.**

We know each part mattered.

We do not know all of them precisely enough to draw one authoritative Hadean coastline.

A 2024 review of Archean continental emergence concluded that subaerial land existed during the Archean, but its spatial extent and longevity remain uncertain.

Early exposed terrain may often have consisted of volcanic crust or proto-continental fragments rather than broad, stable modern-style continents.

This helps explain why an ocean-dominated early Earth is plausible without requiring a literal waterworld.

Some land could emerge.

Much of it could disappear again.

Volcanic islands could rise rapidly.

Crust could founder.

Impact basins could alter regional topography.

Cooling could change seafloor elevation over time.

And as continents grew and oceanic lithosphere evolved, the geometry of the ocean basins changed with them.

So when we later illustrate the early Earth, the honest image is not:

**ONE KNOWN GLOBAL COASTLINE.**

It is:

**an ocean-dominated planet with uncertain, changing emergent land.**

The missing map is part of the evidence.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Foley, Buhre & Jacob (2003), *Nature*, hotter Archean mantle and thicker oceanic crust.
- Zhu et al. (2021), *Reviews of Geophysics*, early crustal growth and thicker Archean oceanic crust.
- Cawood and colleagues (2024), *Annual Review of Earth and Planetary Sciences*, subaerial emergence of continents on Archean Earth.
- Kirkland et al. (2025), *Nature Reviews Earth & Environment*, evolution of early continental crust.

**Decision:** ocean-dominated early Earth is plausible; no precise Hadean global sea level, land fraction or coastline is assigned.
