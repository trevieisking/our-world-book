# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 086 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### WHEN ROCK PROVES IT WAS UNDERWATER

A zircon can imply water.

A pillow lava can show it.

When basalt erupts underwater, the outside of a lava flow chills rapidly.

The cooling surface forms a skin.

Hot lava continues pushing from inside, inflating rounded lobes.

The result is a pile of bulbous structures known as **pillow lavas**.

They are common in modern submarine volcanic settings.

Ancient examples therefore provide direct field evidence that lava erupted into water.

In the Isua Supracrustal Belt of southwest Greenland, volcanic and sedimentary rocks around **3.8 to 3.7 billion years old** preserve some of the oldest known subaqueous environments on Earth.

Pillow basalts occur there.

So do chemical sediments and layered rocks formed from material deposited in water.

This is a crucial change in our evidence.

For Hadean oceans, much of the case is carried by mineral chemistry and model-dependent inference.

At Isua, we can point to rocks that were physically formed in aquatic settings.

That does not make every interpretation easy.

The rocks have been metamorphosed.

They have been folded.

They have been tectonically rearranged.

Some original minerals are gone.

Some textures have been modified.

But enough primary structures survive locally to establish that these were not simply dry volcanic terrains later soaked in water.

They belonged to marine or subaqueous systems.

One study of Isua pillow basalts argued that the preserved volcanic pile implies water deep enough to place those eruptions beneath a substantial water column.

That is useful evidence for local conditions.

It is not permission to calculate the exact global depth of the Archean ocean from one Greenland outcrop.

Local geology is local geology.

The importance of Isua is more fundamental:

**by around 3.8–3.7 billion years ago, liquid water was not merely touching isolated minerals. It was shaping landscapes, volcanoes and sediments.**

We have crossed from inferred hydrosphere to preserved aquatic geology.

The water planet is now visible in rock.
