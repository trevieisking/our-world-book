# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 094 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### STRONGER GRAVITY DOES NOT MEAN THE SAME TIDE EVERYWHERE

A closer Moon does not mean every ancient coastline experienced a wall of water twice a day.

Tides are more complicated than that.

The Moon and Sun provide the astronomical forcing.

The ocean decides how that forcing is expressed.

Tidal amplitude depends on the shape and depth of ocean basins, the arrangement of land, the roughness of coastlines, the speed of Earth's rotation and whether the natural oscillation period of a basin happens to approach the tidal forcing period.

That last effect is **resonance**.

The Bay of Fundy has enormous tides today not because the Moon pulls harder on Canada than on the rest of Earth, but because basin geometry amplifies the incoming tidal motion.

Other coastlines experience much smaller tides under the same Moon.

Deep-time tides must therefore be reconstructed with geography as well as astronomy.

A 2021 long-term Earth–Moon modelling study emphasised that tidal amplitudes and dissipation depend strongly on basin geometry, bathymetry and continental configuration.

A 2023 modelling study of Hadean and Archean-style oceans reached a particularly useful conclusion for this book.

Even with a closer Moon and larger forcing, its simulated early oceans did **not** automatically produce global "supertides".

Large, ocean-dominated basins could be poorly tuned for resonance.

The simulations allowed substantially greater tidal energy dissipation than today in some cases — potentially several times modern rates — without requiring every shoreline to experience extreme tidal ranges.

That distinction is important.

The early Moon supplied stronger potential forcing.

Local tides still depended on local geography.

A narrow inlet could amplify the tide.

A broad open coast might not.

A huge global ocean could dissipate energy differently from today's partitioned basins.

And because Hadean geography is almost entirely lost, exact tide heights at a specific 4.2-billion-year-old shoreline are not recoverable.

So our reconstruction will show **stronger tidal cycling**, not a Hollywood ocean repeatedly swallowing continents.

The physics is interesting enough without adding a tsunami every six hours.
