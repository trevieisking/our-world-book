# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 110 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### EARTH COULD MAKE ORGANICS TOO

Meteorites were not Earth's only source of organic molecules.

Energy was everywhere.

Ultraviolet radiation reached the surface far more strongly before an ozone layer existed.

Lightning crossed volcanic and atmospheric environments.

Impacts generated shock waves and high temperatures.

Hydrothermal systems supplied heat, reduced compounds and catalytic mineral surfaces.

Chemical reactions driven by those energy sources can produce organic molecules from simpler starting materials.

The famous Miller–Urey experiment demonstrated that amino acids can form abiotically under a particular reducing gas mixture exposed to electrical sparks.

That experiment remains historically important.

It should not be mistaken for a perfect recreation of the Hadean atmosphere.

Modern geochemical work generally favours a less strongly reducing global atmosphere than the original Miller–Urey mixture.

But local reducing environments and transient reduced gases could still exist.

And prebiotic synthesis does not depend on one experiment.

Laboratory studies have produced amino acids, nucleobase precursors, sugars, lipids and other biologically relevant molecules through many different reaction networks.

Impact experiments have produced amino acids and other organics under simulated collision conditions.

Hydrothermal experiments generate reduced carbon compounds under some water–rock conditions.

Ultraviolet-driven chemistry can connect simple atmospheric or aqueous precursors into more complex products.

This demonstrates **chemical possibility**.

It does not reconstruct one historical sequence.

Laboratory chemists choose concentrations.

They purify reagents.

They control temperature.

They decide when one product is transferred into the next reaction.

Early Earth had no technician.

That gap between a plausible individual reaction and a complete natural pathway is one of the central problems in origin-of-life research.

So we can say with confidence:

**early Earth had multiple plausible abiotic sources of organic molecules.**

We cannot yet say which combination supplied the decisive pathway to life.
