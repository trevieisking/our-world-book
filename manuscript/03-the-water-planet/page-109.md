# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 109 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### SPACE DELIVERED ORGANIC MOLECULES

Not every organic molecule on early Earth had to be manufactured on Earth.

Carbonaceous meteorites contain complex organic material.

The famous Murchison meteorite contains amino acids and other carbon-bearing compounds that are demonstrably extraterrestrial in origin.

Later analyses identified sugar-related compounds and other biologically relevant molecules in carbonaceous meteorites.

Returned samples from primitive asteroids such as Ryugu and Bennu have expanded that inventory further.

This does **not** mean life arrived from space.

An amino acid is not an organism.

A nucleobase is not a genome.

A sugar is not a cell.

What meteorites prove is more modest and more useful:

**organic chemistry is not unique to living planets.**

Molecular clouds, protoplanetary disks, asteroids and comets can all participate in organic synthesis.

Some of that material survived incorporation into small Solar-System bodies.

Some of those bodies later struck Earth.

During the early bombardment, the flux of extraterrestrial material was far larger than it is today.

Meteorites and interplanetary dust therefore delivered carbon compounds continuously.

How much survived each impact depended on impact speed, size, atmosphere and chemistry.

Large energetic impacts can destroy organics.

Smaller bodies and dust can deliver them more gently.

Some impacts can even generate new organic compounds through shock chemistry.

So extraterrestrial material supplied both ingredients and experiments.

This adds another source to the early-Earth inventory:

not only atmosphere,

not only ocean,

not only rock,

but material manufactured before it arrived here.

The boundary between cosmic chemistry and planetary chemistry was porous.

Earth inherited molecules as well as atoms.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Kvenvolden et al. (1970), *Nature*, extraterrestrial amino acids in Murchison.
- Cooper et al. (2001), *Nature*, sugar-related compounds in carbonaceous meteorites.
- 2026 *Nature Astronomy* discussion of organic inventories in primitive asteroid samples.
- Chyba & Sagan (1992), *Nature*, endogenous, exogenous and impact-shock organic sources.

**Decision:** extraterrestrial organics are established; their delivery does not constitute evidence for extraterrestrial life or panspermia.
