### THE OCEAN FLOWS THROUGH THE PLANET

An ocean does not end at the seafloor.

Water enters cracks.

It reacts with rock.

It comes back changed.

Modern oceanic crust demonstrates this dramatically at hydrothermal systems, but the same broad physical principle must have operated wherever early Earth's hot crust was fractured and permeable.

The young planet had more internal heat than Earth does today.

Its crust was more volcanically active.

Water–rock reactions were therefore likely major controls on seawater chemistry.

Cold seawater entering hot crust changes both.

The water can lose magnesium.

It can gain calcium, silica, iron and other dissolved components.

Sulfur changes chemical form.

The rock becomes hydrated and altered.

At sufficiently high temperatures, hydrothermal fluids can return to the seafloor carrying reduced chemicals that react vigorously when mixed with cooler ocean water.

This exchange matters for more than vents.

If enough seawater circulates through enough crust, hydrothermal alteration can influence the chemistry of the **whole ocean** over geological time.

Oxygen isotopes are one example.

Scientists have argued for decades over whether the oxygen-isotope composition of seawater has remained nearly constant or evolved substantially through time.

Part of that debate depends on the balance between high-temperature and low-temperature interaction between water and crust.

That is why an isotope measured in a tiny mineral can end up forcing scientists to model an entire global ocean.

The early seafloor itself is mostly gone.

So the exact geometry of Hadean hydrothermal circulation cannot be observed directly.

Nor should we casually paste a modern mid-ocean-ridge diagram onto a planet whose tectonic regime may have been different.

But the underlying thermodynamics is unavoidable.

Hot rock plus liquid water produces reaction.

Reaction changes water.

Reaction changes rock.

And those reactions create chemical gradients.

Later, when we investigate life's origins, those gradients will become important.

For now they tell us something simpler.

The ocean was not sitting on top of Earth.

It was circulating through Earth.
