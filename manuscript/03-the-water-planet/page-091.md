# CHAPTER THREE — THE WATER PLANET
## Page 091 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### RAIN NEEDS SOMETHING TO FALL ON

An ocean alone does not make a full hydrological cycle.

Water evaporates.

Clouds form.

Rain falls.

But if every square kilometre of the planet were deep ocean, that rain would simply return to seawater.

To weather continental rock, build rivers and carry sediment from land, some crust must emerge above sea level.

How early did that happen?

Again, the answer arrives in fragments.

The 2024 Jack Hills zircon study showing interaction with **meteoric water** by about 4.0 billion years ago or earlier implies that precipitation reached crustal material involved in later magmatic recycling.

That suggests exposed land or at least subaerial volcanic surfaces existed somewhere.

By about 3.7 billion years ago, Isua contains detrital sediments derived from volcanic and differentiated crustal sources.

Some studies interpret the sedimentary system as involving material eroded from nearby emergent terrain and transported into marine basins.

This does not mean Earth already looked like a modern atlas.

The fraction of exposed land could have been small.

Islands may have been important.

Volcanic plateaus and proto-continental fragments may have appeared and disappeared.

Sea level depends on both water volume and basin capacity, and both changed as the mantle cooled and crust evolved.

But once land met rain, a new conveyor system existed.

Weathering released ions.

Rivers transported dissolved and solid material.

Sediments accumulated in basins.

Nutrients moved from rock into water.

Fresh surfaces were continually exposed.

The ocean became chemically connected to the atmosphere **and** the land.

This connection is easy to underestimate because a river looks small beside an ocean.

Over millions of years, rivers are patient.

They dismantle mountains one grain at a time.

The ancient Earth did not need the Amazon to begin the process.

It needed rain, exposed rock and time.

It had all three.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Johnson et al. (2024), *Nature Geoscience*, meteoric-water interaction by ~4.0 Ga or earlier.
- Isua sedimentary-system studies and 2024–2026 work on 3.7-Ga detrital marine sediments.

**Decision:** evidence for subaerial weathering/exposed crust is local and inferential; no global land fraction is assigned.
