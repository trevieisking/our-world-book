### THE STAGE IS READY

We started this chapter with water.

We end it with chemistry.

The ocean is now persistent.

The atmosphere exchanges gases with it.

Tides move shorelines.

Rain weathers rock.

Rivers carry dissolved ions and sediment.

Hydrothermal systems drive water through hot crust.

Carbon cycles between gas, water and minerals.

Nitrogen is abundant but difficult to activate.

Phosphorus is chemically available in some environments and restricted in others.

Sulfur shifts between oxidation states.

Iron remains soluble in anoxic water until chemistry removes it.

Silica fills the sea at concentrations unlike modern oceans.

Meteorites deliver organic molecules.

Earth makes others.

Mineral surfaces concentrate some compounds.

Evaporation concentrates more.

Gradients supply energy.

Cycles repeatedly change conditions.

Everything needed for **interesting chemistry** is present.

That is not the same as everything needed for life.

The scientific problem now changes.

We no longer need to ask whether amino acids can form.

They can.

We no longer need to ask whether organic molecules exist in meteorites.

They do.

We no longer need to ask whether membranes can self-assemble under some conditions.

They can.

The difficult question is integration:

**How can a non-living chemical system become a self-maintaining, heritable, evolvable system?**

No single experiment has yet demonstrated the complete natural transition from plausible prebiotic geochemistry to an autonomous, open-ended Darwinian biosphere.

That is not evidence that the transition requires anything supernatural.

It means the mechanism remains incomplete.

Researchers are testing RNA-first models.

Metabolism-first models.

Protocell models.

Hydrothermal models.

Wet–dry cycling.

Mineral-surface chemistry.

Hybrid scenarios that combine several environments.

Recent work continues to add new pathways and constrain old ones.

Some ideas will fail.

Some will merge.

Some may turn out to describe different stages of the same process.

Our job is not to choose a winner because it sounds elegant.

Our job is to follow what has been demonstrated.

The planet is ready.

The chemistry is active.

The evidence now reaches the hardest boundary in Earth's history.
