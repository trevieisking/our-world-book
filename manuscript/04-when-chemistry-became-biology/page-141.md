# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 141 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### CO₂ CAN BECOME METABOLIC CARBON WITHOUT ENZYMES

Carbon dioxide is everywhere in origin-of-life discussions.

That does not make it easy to use.

CO₂ is already strongly oxidised.

Turning it into the carbon skeletons used by biology requires electrons and suitable reaction pathways.

Modern organisms solve the problem with enzymes.

Some of the oldest forms of microbial carbon fixation use the **acetyl-CoA pathway**, also called the Wood–Ljungdahl pathway.

In living cells, that pathway reduces carbon dioxide through a sequence of reactions involving elaborate metal-containing enzymes and organic cofactors.

Its simplicity relative to other biological carbon-fixation pathways has made it central to metabolism-first models.

But the historical question is dangerous.

A modern pathway can look ancient without being a direct chemical fossil.

The relevant test is experimental:

**Can non-biological catalysts make the same kinds of carbon compounds from CO₂?**

In 2018, Sreejith Varma, Kamila Muchowska and colleagues tested native transition metals.

They reacted carbon dioxide with metallic iron, nickel and cobalt in water.

The metals reduced CO₂.

Among the products were **acetate** and **pyruvate**.

Those molecules are not arbitrary organics.

Acetate lies at the end of the modern acetyl-CoA carbon-fixation route.

Pyruvate is one of the central junctions of metabolism, connecting carbon fixation to amino-acid and biosynthetic chemistry.

The reactions produced these compounds at concentrations approaching the millimolar range under some tested conditions.

They worked across temperatures from roughly 30 to 100 degrees Celsius and carbon-dioxide pressures from 1 to 40 bar.

The experiments therefore established something that had previously been mostly conceptual.

Protein enzymes are not required for every chemically important step connecting inorganic carbon to central metabolic molecules.

Metal surfaces can perform part of the job.

That is evidence for **geochemical carbon fixation**.

It is not evidence for a complete primordial acetyl-CoA pathway.

Several gaps remain.

Native metallic iron is highly reactive and its abundance in the relevant early-Earth environments is uncertain.

Meteorites can supply reduced metals, and unusual geological settings may expose them, but a laboratory metal powder is not automatically a realistic Hadean catalyst bed.

The experiment supplied elevated CO₂ pressures for many conditions.

Products were measured after hours or days under controlled laboratory conditions.

Modern acetyl-CoA metabolism also contains cofactors, sulfur chemistry and energy-coupling steps that the simple metal system did not reproduce.

Most importantly, acetate and pyruvate do not constitute a self-sustaining network.

They are starting points for more chemistry.

Still, the result weakens an important objection to metabolism-first ideas.

If central carbon compounds required complex enzymes before they could form at all, metabolism could not precede proteins.

But if metal-rich geochemistry can produce some of those compounds directly, biological pathways may have recruited chemistry that was already physically accessible.

That possibility makes a prediction.

Other parts of central metabolism should also have non-enzymatic analogues under plausible conditions.

Researchers have tested exactly that.

And the result is not one perfect primordial cycle.

It is something messier.

Fragments.

Overlapping networks.

Alternative routes.

Chemistry that resembles the map of metabolism without yet behaving like a cell.

That mess may be a weakness.

Or it may be what evolution had to start with.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Varma et al. (2018), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: native Fe⁰, Ni⁰ and Co⁰ reduce CO₂ in water to products including acetate and pyruvate, in near-millimolar concentrations under some tested conditions, over ~30–100 °C and 1–40 bar CO₂.
- The products overlap with the acetyl-CoA pathway and central metabolism, but the experiment does not reproduce the complete biological pathway.
- Availability and persistence of native reduced metals in specific Hadean environments remain geochemical constraints.

**Decision:** non-enzymatic CO₂ reduction to central metabolic products is experimentally established. Direct continuity with the modern acetyl-CoA pathway remains a hypothesis.
