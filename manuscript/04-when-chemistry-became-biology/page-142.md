# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 142 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### PIECES OF A METABOLIC NETWORK

Making one metabolic molecule is chemistry.

Making connected sequences begins to look more interesting.

The reverse tricarboxylic-acid cycle — the reverse TCA or reverse Krebs cycle — is used by some modern microorganisms to build biomass from carbon dioxide.

It contains eleven core transformations.

Like the acetyl-CoA pathway, it has been proposed as a possible descendant of older geochemical chemistry.

In 2017, Kamila Muchowska and colleagues tested whether metal catalysts could promote parts of the cycle without enzymes.

Zinc ions, chromium ions and metallic iron drove **six of the eleven** reverse-TCA reactions.

More importantly, the team linked reactions together.

Two separate sequences of three consecutive reactions proceeded under a common set of acidic aqueous conditions.

The chemistry also produced amino acids from ketoacids through reductive amination.

This was not a full cycle.

But it demonstrated that non-enzymatic metabolic chemistry need not consist only of isolated one-step reactions performed under mutually incompatible conditions.

In 2019, the same research programme found another network.

Ferrous iron promoted reactions between pyruvate and glyoxylate.

The resulting chemistry generated **nine of the eleven intermediates** found in the modern TCA cycle.

It included all five molecules described by the authors as universal metabolic precursors — compounds sitting at major junctions from which cells build amino acids, lipids, sugars and nucleotides.

The system did something else that makes it chemically interesting.

Products were both made and broken down.

That creates a network with simultaneous constructive and destructive flux rather than a one-way synthetic recipe.

Add hydroxylamine and metallic iron, and four biological amino acids appeared through chemistry paralleling parts of biosynthesis.

Again, the resemblance is striking.

Again, it is not a cell.

The starting materials pyruvate and glyoxylate had to be provided.

The network was not shown to continuously regenerate every reactant.

No membrane retained useful products.

No genetic system selected catalysts.

No energy-coupling mechanism regulated the flow.

Further work has pushed the chemistry into different conditions.

A 2024 study used hydrogen gas with catalytic nickel — or ground meteorite material containing catalytic metals — at about room temperature and pH 8.

Hydrogen drove reductive amination of six biological ketoacids and glyoxylate to their corresponding amino acids.

Under the same conditions, carbon–carbon bond-forming reactions generated additional ketoacids.

The authors described the result as a continuous metabolism-like reaction network connecting hydrogen-driven carbon chemistry with amino-acid synthesis.

That matters because H₂ is not an exotic laboratory reductant invented solely for the experiment.

It is a geologically plausible product of water–rock reactions.

Yet even this system began with selected organic feedstocks and supplied ammonia.

It did not start with nothing but rock, water, CO₂ and H₂ and then bootstrap itself into a complete metabolic cycle.

The emerging pattern is therefore neither trivial nor complete.

Metabolic maps contain reactions that can occur without enzymes.

Metal-rich chemistry can connect several of them.

Common conditions can support multiple transformations.

Hydrogen can power network-like chemistry.

And amino-acid synthesis can become coupled to carbon chemistry.

These experiments make it increasingly plausible that biology did not invent every core reaction from scratch.

But a metabolic **map** and a self-sustaining metabolic **system** are different things.

The next page is about that difference.

Because one of the easiest mistakes in origins research is to see half a cycle and mentally draw the missing arrows.

Nature does not get those arrows for free.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Muchowska et al. (2017), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: metals promoted 6/11 rTCA reactions; two three-step sequences occurred under common conditions; reductive amination yielded amino acids.
- Muchowska, Varma & Moran (2019), *Nature*: Fe²⁺-promoted pyruvate/glyoxylate network generated 9/11 TCA intermediates, including five universal metabolic precursors; additional reagents yielded four biological amino acids.
- 2024 *Chem*: H₂ with catalytic Ni or ground meteorites drove reductive amination and a connected metabolism-like ketoacid network at pH ~8 and 22 °C.
- None of these experiments demonstrates a self-sustaining closed prebiotic metabolic cycle.

**Decision:** experimentally connected protometabolic networks are distinguished explicitly from an autonomous metabolism capable of maintaining and reproducing itself.
