# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 128 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE RNA WORLD IS A BRIDGE, NOT A FINISHED ROAD

We can now ask what the RNA-world framework actually explains.

It explains why one early polymer is attractive.

RNA can carry sequence information.

RNA can fold.

Folded RNA can catalyse reactions.

RNA templates can guide complementary copying.

Laboratory-evolved ribozymes can polymerise RNA.

RNA-catalysed copying can support selection and evolution when fidelity is high enough.

Physical cycling can help solve strand separation.

And a polymerase ribozyme only 45 nucleotides long can perform the two core copying reactions needed for reproduction of its own sequence.

That is a much stronger experimental foundation than existed when the RNA-world idea was first proposed.

The hypothesis has moved from a clever answer to a molecular chicken-and-egg problem toward a programme with many testable pieces.

But the road is still incomplete.

We do not know the historical route that supplied the first compatible nucleotide pool.

We do not yet have one continuous natural sequence from simple geochemical feedstocks to an autonomously evolving RNA population.

Activated building blocks have to be replenished.

Polymerisation has to compete with hydrolysis and unwanted side reactions.

Useful RNAs have to become concentrated rather than lost in a planetary ocean.

Copying must remain accurate enough.

Strands must repeatedly separate.

Catalysts and their products must stay near enough to benefit one another.

Parasites — sequences that exploit replicative machinery without contributing useful function — can emerge.

And an RNA population still needs access to energy, matter and a local physical identity.

There is also no requirement that the earliest biology was **pure RNA**.

RNA may have shared the stage with peptides from early on.

Short peptides can stabilise RNA structures, bind metals and contribute catalytic chemistry.

Prebiotic reaction networks can generate precursors relevant to nucleotides, amino acids and lipids from related chemical systems.

RNA–peptide coevolution may therefore have begun before anything resembling modern translation existed.

The modern ribosome itself is a partnership.

Its catalytic heart is RNA, but today's machine is surrounded by proteins and linked to transfer RNAs, messenger RNAs, energy chemistry and a genetic code.

That architecture may preserve stages of an ancient handover in which RNA gradually recruited peptides and proteins as more versatile catalysts.

DNA could then become the more stable long-term archive.

That sequence is plausible.

Parts of it are strongly supported by comparative biochemistry and experiments.

The exact historical route is not preserved.

So we should resist turning “RNA world” into a picture of a warm pond full of modern-looking RNA cells.

It is better understood as a family of hypotheses about an evolutionary phase in which RNA carried much more of the combined burden of heredity and catalysis than it does today.

The strongest version of the evidence says:

**RNA can solve several problems that otherwise have to be solved simultaneously.**

It does not say:

**RNA by itself has solved the entire origin of life.**

One missing piece now becomes impossible to ignore.

A useful catalyst that diffuses away from the molecules it helps cannot build a stable lineage.

A replicator surrounded by an unlimited public pool of shared chemistry gives cheaters an advantage.

For evolution to act on cooperating molecular systems, chemistry needs an **inside** and an **outside**.

It needs compartments.

The next step is not another molecule.

It is the beginning of a cell.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Higgs & Lehman (2015), *Nature Reviews Genetics*: RNA-world models increasingly involve molecular cooperation rather than one isolated self-sufficient molecule.
- Patel et al. (2015), *Nature Chemistry*: common prebiotic chemistry can feed precursors relevant to RNA, amino acids and lipids, supporting hybrid/co-emergence possibilities.
- Seelig & Chen (2025), *Nature Chemistry*, and 2025–2026 RNA-world literature: early biochemical systems likely assembled through interacting functions and transitions rather than one fully formed modern pathway.
- Gianni et al. (2026), *Science*: QT45 strongly narrows the RNA-replicase bootstrap problem but does not complete an autonomous prebiotic replication-to-life sequence.

**Decision:** the RNA world is presented as a well-supported framework for an RNA-rich evolutionary phase, not as a uniquely established location, date or complete RNA-only historical sequence. The next target is compartmentalisation and protocells.
