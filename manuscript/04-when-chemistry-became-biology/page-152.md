# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 152 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE BOUNDARY WE HAVE NOT CROSSED

We began this chapter with a definition problem.

What counts as life?

We end with a historical problem.

How did Earth cross from chemistry into biology?

We do not know the complete mechanism.

That answer should neither be softened nor exaggerated.

It does not mean we know nothing.

We know that organic chemistry occurs without life.

We know that Earth and meteorites supplied relevant molecules.

We know that nucleotides can form through plausible abiotic chemistry.

We know that RNA can carry information and catalyse reactions.

We know that RNA replication can support heredity and selection in laboratory systems.

We know that surprisingly small RNA catalysts can perform sophisticated copying chemistry.

We know that membranes can self-assemble.

We know that simple compartments can grow, divide, compete and couple internal chemistry to external fitness.

We know that rocks, metals, light, drying, freezing and redox gradients can drive reactions that later became central to biology.

We know that parts of core metabolism have non-enzymatic chemical analogues.

We know that modern synthetic systems can integrate many of these functions.

And we know from the rocks that life existed very early in Earth's history.

What we do **not** know is the continuous historical sequence.

We do not know whether RNA came before substantial protometabolism or emerged within it.

We do not know whether the decisive compartment was a fatty-acid vesicle, a mineral pore, a droplet, an ice channel or something no longer represented by modern biology.

We do not know whether the critical chemistry occurred at an alkaline vent, in a volcanic pool, on a drying mineral surface, in ice, across several connected settings or in an environment we have not yet reconstructed correctly.

We do not know how the first genetic code formed.

We do not know exactly when protein synthesis became linked to nucleic-acid heredity.

We do not know how primitive energy coupling became the chemiosmotic machinery shared by modern cells.

We do not know the first organism's chemistry.

We do not know the date of abiogenesis.

And we have not recreated an autonomous prebiotic system that starts with plausible simple geochemical inputs and then indefinitely builds, maintains and reproduces its own evolvable organisation.

Those are real unknowns.

They are not blank permission slips.

“Unknown” does not mean every proposed explanation has equal evidence.

A mechanism that violates chemistry does not become plausible because the history is incomplete.

A hypothesis gains weight by making predictions, surviving experiments and connecting to geology.

A gap in the mechanism is not positive evidence for whatever answer we personally prefer.

It is a research target.

This chapter has also shown why the phrase **“just chemicals”** can be misleading.

Chemistry can organise.

Molecules self-assemble.

Networks create feedback.

Compartments create individuality.

Replication creates memory.

Variation creates alternatives.

Selection preserves some alternatives over others.

None of those processes requires foresight.

Together, they provide a physical route by which matter can acquire history.

Where that route first became self-sustaining on Earth remains unresolved.

The exact crossing is hidden because the planet recycled most of its oldest crust and because the earliest living systems were microscopic, fragile and chemically simple.

But once biology became widespread enough to alter sediments, build communities and fractionate elements, it began leaving evidence we can interrogate.

The question therefore changes again.

We stop asking:

**How might chemistry have become life?**

And begin asking:

**What was the earliest biosphere actually doing?**

The trail moves out of the laboratory.

Back into stone.

Into layered rocks.

Carbon isotopes.

Sulfur.

Microscopic structures.

Ancient shorelines.

Hydrothermal veins.

Stromatolites.

And microbial worlds that ruled Earth for billions of years before any animal existed.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Chapter synthesis based on the evidence registers for pages 113–152.
- Geological evidence establishes early life but does not identify the abiogenesis mechanism or date.
- 2026 literature continues to report major component advances without a demonstrated complete autonomous transition from simple prebiotic geochemistry to open-ended cellular Darwinian evolution.
- The chapter deliberately distinguishes unresolved scientific mechanism from evidence for any non-scientific alternative.

**Decision:** Chapter 4 closes with the strongest defensible 2026 boundary: abiogenesis remains mechanistically incomplete, but many formerly speculative component steps now have experimental mechanisms and constraints. The next chapter follows the surviving biosphere record rather than repeating origin scenarios.
