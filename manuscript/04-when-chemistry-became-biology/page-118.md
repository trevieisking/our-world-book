# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 118 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### CAN WE PUSH LIFE BACK TO 3.7 BILLION YEARS?

Older rocks exist.

Their evidence is harder to read.

The Isua Supracrustal Belt in southwest Greenland contains sedimentary and volcanic rocks more than 3.7 billion years old.

For decades, researchers have searched them for traces of an extremely early biosphere.

The results show why no single headline should stand in for the evidence.

In 2016, researchers reported structures in Isua rocks that they interpreted as 3.7-billion-year-old stromatolites.

If correct, they would have pushed clear morphological evidence for microbial communities far deeper into time.

Two years later, a detailed three-dimensional reassessment challenged that interpretation.

The supposed stromatolites were more plausibly explained as deformation structures produced after burial and metamorphism.

That claim weakened.

But it did **not** erase every line of evidence for life at Isua.

Other Isua rocks contain carbonaceous material in metasediments.

Some of that carbon is depleted in the heavier carbon isotope, carbon-13, in a pattern compatible with biological carbon fixation.

The difficulty is proving that the carbon is both biological and genuinely ancient.

Metamorphism has heated and recrystallised these rocks.

Later fluids can deposit graphite.

Abiotic reactions can also fractionate carbon isotopes.

So researchers must distinguish graphite tied to original sediment from graphite introduced in later veins.

Recent work has strengthened that distinction.

A 2025 study measured carbon isotopes in graphite preserved in more than 3.7-billion-year-old Isua metasediments and secondary veins.

The sediment-associated graphite showed a narrower isotopic distribution and was interpreted as detrital biogenic organic matter, while the vein graphite was consistent with abiotic fluid precipitation.

A 2026 study then reported carbon and sulfur isotope patterns in Isua marine sediments that the authors interpret as evidence for multiple anaerobic microbial metabolisms, including iron- and sulfur-linked respiration.

Those results are important.

They make the case for a 3.7-billion-year-old biosphere stronger than the rejected stromatolite morphology alone ever could.

But ancient metamorphic rocks demand caution.

Not every researcher will assign the same confidence to every proxy, and further work can still alter the interpretation.

For this book, the correct statement is therefore layered.

**Life by about 3.48 billion years ago is supported by especially convincing, convergent geological evidence.**

**Evidence consistent with life at or before 3.7 billion years ago is substantial and has recently strengthened, but it comes from a more altered and difficult record.**

That is not indecision.

It is what the rocks justify.

The oldest pages of Earth's history are damaged.

We should expect confidence to fade gradually as we read farther back.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Allwood et al. (2018), *Nature*: the proposed 3.7 Ga Isua stromatolites are more plausibly non-biological deformation structures.
- Hassenkam et al. (2017), *Nature*: carbonaceous inclusions in >3.7 Ga Isua metasediments preserve elements compatible with ancient biological material.
- 2025 *Communications Earth & Environment*: preserved carbon-isotope compositions distinguish sediment-associated Isua graphite interpreted as biogenic from more variable vein graphite.
- 2026 *Communications Earth & Environment*: carbon and sulfur isotope data are interpreted as evidence for diverse anaerobic metabolisms in 3.7 Ga Isua sediments.

**Decision:** Isua is treated as a strong but more difficult older biosignature record. The rejected stromatolite morphology is separated from independent carbonaceous and isotopic evidence.
