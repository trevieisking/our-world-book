# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 137 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### METABOLISM BEFORE METABOLISM

A cell is not powered by having molecules.

It is powered by **reactions that keep happening**.

Modern metabolism is the enormous network of chemical transformations through which organisms acquire energy, build components, recycle material and maintain themselves far from chemical equilibrium.

That network contains thousands of enzyme-catalysed reactions.

It uses ATP and related molecules as transferable chemical energy.

It moves electrons through specialised cofactors.

It stores ion gradients across membranes.

It regulates which pathways run, when they run and how quickly.

The first evolving chemical systems could not begin with that machinery.

But they still faced the same physical law.

Useful chemistry costs free energy.

A polymer does not copy itself simply because copying would be biologically useful.

A membrane does not keep growing indefinitely without fresh material.

Carbon dioxide does not automatically become a cell's carbon skeletons.

Reactions proceed according to thermodynamics and kinetics, not according to what an organism needs.

So before asking where the first metabolism came from, we need to strip the word down to its function.

A primitive metabolic system need not resemble a complete modern pathway.

It needs a **flow of matter and energy** capable of driving useful reactions faster than those products are lost.

The crucial starting point is chemical disequilibrium.

Two substances can coexist with the potential to react.

One may contain electrons at relatively high chemical potential.

Another can accept them.

A pH difference can store free energy because protons are distributed unevenly.

A temperature gradient can drive transport and reaction.

Light can excite electrons.

Drying can concentrate compounds and change which reactions become favourable.

The environment can therefore contain energy without containing life.

Life's achievement is to capture part of that energy and connect it to chemistry that preserves the system doing the capture.

Modern organisms internalise this control.

They build enzymes.

They regenerate ATP.

They pump ions.

They couple an energetically favourable reaction to an unfavourable one.

At the origin of life, some of the coupling may instead have been supplied by the environment.

A metal surface can lower an activation barrier.

A mineral can hold reactants close together.

Hydrogen produced by rock–water reactions can donate electrons.

A natural pH gradient can provide a difference in proton concentration.

Repeated environmental cycles can push chemistry through changing conditions.

This suggests an important distinction.

**Energy source** is not the same thing as **metabolism**.

A hydrothermal vent may produce hydrogen.

Sunlight may produce reactive molecules.

A mineral may catalyse carbon reduction.

None of those facts alone creates a self-sustaining chemical network.

For something metabolism-like to emerge, reactions must become connected.

Products from one step have to feed another.

Useful intermediates must persist.

Side reactions must not consume everything.

Catalysts have to remain available.

The overall network must continue while matter flows through it.

And eventually that network has to become coupled to heredity and compartments.

The origin-of-life debate sometimes contrasts “metabolism first” with “RNA first.”

That can be misleading.

RNA replication itself requires activated chemistry and energy.

A metabolic network without heredity cannot accumulate adaptive innovations.

The two problems meet.

The question is not simply which came first.

It is how **energy flow and information flow became dependent on one another**.

To investigate that, we return to one of the most powerful energy sources on the young Earth.

Rock reacting with water.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Zimmermann et al. (2024), *Accounts of Chemical Research*: metabolism-first models seek geochemically driven reaction networks that dissipate environmental free energy and can become progressively catalysed/organised.
- Harrison et al. (2023), *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*: origin models increasingly integrate disequilibrium, geochemistry, protocells and information rather than requiring one isolated subsystem to precede all others.
- Modern metabolic machinery is not projected backward as a primitive requirement.

**Decision:** “protometabolism” is used functionally for connected energy- and matter-transforming chemistry before modern enzymes, ATP-based regulation or genetically encoded pathways. No complete prebiotic metabolism is assumed.
