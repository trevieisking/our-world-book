# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 120 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### “LIFE EXISTED BY” IS NOT “LIFE BEGAN AT”

We can now draw the most important line in this chapter.

The rock record gives us **minimum ages for life's existence**.

It does not directly give us the date of life's origin.

By about 3.48 billion years ago, the evidence for microbial life is convincing.

Evidence from Isua suggests that a biosphere may already have existed at or before 3.7 billion years ago.

Still older claims exist, but their ages, biological interpretations or both are less secure.

Now compare those dates with what we learned about the planet itself.

Liquid water and water-altered crust existed much earlier.

Organic chemistry had multiple sources.

Mineral surfaces, hydrothermal systems, shorelines and chemical gradients supplied environments in which prebiotic reactions could occur.

So the origin of life must fall somewhere after Earth became capable of sustaining the relevant chemistry and before the earliest life whose traces we can confidently recognise.

That sounds like a window.

It is not a narrow one.

Early Earth's rock record has been heavily destroyed.

An origin event could leave no identifiable fossil.

The earliest organisms may have been too small, too fragile or too rare to preserve.

Entire early environments have been subducted, metamorphosed or eroded away.

The first successful lineage may also have existed long before it became abundant enough to leave a global geochemical signal.

Molecular biology offers another kind of clock.

All known cellular life shares deep features: the genetic code, ribosomes, ATP-based energy transfer and many core biochemical systems.

Those shared features point to a **last universal common ancestor**, or LUCA.

LUCA was not the first life.

It was the population ancestral to all cellular life that survives today.

There could have been earlier lineages that left no descendants.

A 2024 molecular-clock analysis estimated LUCA at roughly 4.2 billion years ago, with modelled uncertainty extending from about 4.09 to 4.33 billion years ago.

That is an extraordinary result.

It is also indirect.

Molecular clocks depend on evolutionary models, assumptions about rate change, gene histories and geological calibrations.

They are not radiometric dates measured on a fossilised cell.

Other analyses can produce different estimates.

So we will treat molecular clocks as constraints to compare with geology, not as a stopwatch recording abiogenesis.

The honest conclusion is powerful enough.

**Life was established very early in Earth's history.**

How early remains unresolved.

The first successful transition from non-living chemistry to evolvable biology may have occurred relatively soon after persistent habitable environments appeared.

Or there may have been a long interval of chemical experimentation before a lineage became durable.

There may even have been multiple partial or failed transitions before one evolutionary system persisted.

The surviving evidence cannot yet distinguish those histories.

That gives us the next problem.

If geology cannot show us the first living system directly, chemistry must tell us which pathways are physically plausible.

We now turn from the rocks back to the molecules.

The first major candidate is unusual because one class of molecule can do two jobs that modern cells mostly divide between different systems.

It can carry information.

And it can catalyse reactions.

That molecule is RNA.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Dresser Formation evidence anchors convincing life by ~3.48 Ga.
- Isua evidence may extend the biosphere to ≥3.7 Ga but carries greater alteration and interpretive uncertainty.
- Moody et al. (2024), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: molecular-clock analysis estimated LUCA at ~4.2 Ga (modelled interval ~4.09–4.33 Ga) and reconstructed it as part of an established ecosystem.
- LUCA is explicitly distinguished from the first life and from the abiogenesis event.
- Molecular-clock dates are model-dependent and must not be presented as direct fossil ages.

**Decision:** the book will use “life existed by” for geological/molecular constraints and reserve “life originated at” for a date that is currently unknown. Page 120 transitions into RNA-world evidence without declaring RNA-first proven.
