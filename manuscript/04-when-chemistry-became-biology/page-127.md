# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 127 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### FORTY-FIVE NUCLEOTIDES

In February 2026, the RNA replication problem changed again.

Edoardo Gianni and colleagues reported a polymerase ribozyme called **QT45**.

The name reflects its size.

Forty-five nucleotides.

Earlier general RNA polymerase ribozymes were typically more than 150 nucleotides long and structurally complex.

That created an obvious origin problem.

If a polymerase is too long to be produced by primitive chemistry and too long for existing primitive copying to reproduce accurately, it cannot easily bootstrap the system that needs it.

QT45 is much smaller.

It was discovered by searching and selecting from random RNA sequence pools.

Despite its compact size, it can catalyse general template-directed RNA synthesis using activated trinucleotide triphosphates — three-nucleotide RNA building blocks.

Most importantly, the researchers tested the two molecular reactions required for an RNA to replicate its sequence.

First:

use the ribozyme sequence as a template to make its complementary strand.

Second:

use the complementary sequence as a template to make a new copy of the ribozyme.

QT45 could catalyse both reactions.

When copying its complementary strand from a random pool of triplet substrates, the reported per-nucleotide fidelity was about **94.1 percent**.

Using defined substrates, it could also produce a copy of its own sequence.

Both reactions reached yields of roughly **0.2 percent after 72 days** in the reported experiments.

The chemistry operated in mildly alkaline eutectic ice — concentrated liquid channels within a frozen system.

This is a major proof of principle.

A surprisingly small RNA sequence can contain enough structure to perform the core chemistry required to copy both sides of its own genetic information.

The result also suggests that useful polymerase motifs may be more common in RNA sequence space than previously feared.

But QT45 has not crossed every boundary.

The two component reactions were demonstrated under controlled laboratory conditions.

The yields were low.

The experiment supplied activated RNA triplets.

For self-copying, defined substrate sets were used.

The ribozyme itself had been identified through a modern experimental selection process.

The study did not show one QT45 molecule entering an uninterrupted natural cycle, producing its complement, producing multiple functional descendants, maintaining its substrate supply, and then evolving autonomously without laboratory support.

It therefore would be inaccurate to say:

**“Scientists created the first self-replicating life.”**

They did not.

A more accurate statement is both narrower and scientifically more exciting:

**a 45-nucleotide RNA catalyst can perform the two template-copying reactions required for its own sequence replication.**

That removes part of a long-standing bootstrap objection.

The hypothetical first RNA replicase no longer has to be imagined only as an enormous, intricate molecule appearing all at once.

A much smaller catalytic core can do surprisingly advanced work.

And the environmental conditions are interesting too.

The 2025 freeze–thaw work showed how physical cycling can keep copied strands available.

The 2026 QT45 work shows that small RNA catalysts can exploit related concentrated icy conditions.

These experiments do not prove that life began in ice.

They show something more general:

**before cells evolved molecular machines for every task, the environment itself may have supplied part of the machinery.**

## Research / fact-check notes
- Gianni et al. (2026), *Science*, “A small polymerase ribozyme that can synthesize itself and its complementary strand.”
- QT45 is 45 nt long and uses trinucleotide triphosphate substrates in mildly alkaline eutectic ice.
- Reported complementary-strand copying fidelity: 94.1% per nucleotide using a random triplet pool.
- Reported synthesis of both the complementary sequence and a copy of itself: ~0.2% yield over 72 days under the tested conditions.
- The paper demonstrates both component reactions needed for self-replication; it does not demonstrate a fully autonomous, sustained life cycle arising from simple prebiotic feedstocks.

**Decision:** QT45 is treated as a major 2026 advance in plausibility, with its low yield, activated substrates, controlled conditions and lack of an autonomous complete cycle kept explicit.
