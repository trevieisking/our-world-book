# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 148 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### ICE IS NOT CHEMICALLY DEAD

Freeze a glass of salty water.

The ice does not become chemically uniform.

Growing ice crystals largely exclude dissolved salts and organic molecules.

Those solutes are squeezed into microscopic pockets and channels of remaining liquid.

The bulk system freezes.

The liquid fraction becomes concentrated.

This is the **eutectic phase**.

For prebiotic chemistry, that concentration can be extremely useful.

Molecules that were too dilute to interact frequently in open water can become crowded together.

Cold temperatures also slow some degradation reactions.

RNA can survive longer.

Ice can create microscopic compartments without a lipid membrane.

And changing temperature naturally produces cycles of concentration, melting and redistribution.

The idea is not new.

Experiments have shown that ice can promote non-enzymatic nucleotide polymerisation and enhance RNA-catalysed RNA copying.

In 2010, James Attwater and colleagues demonstrated that eutectic ice could increase the activity and stability of an RNA polymerase ribozyme.

The concentrated liquid microchannels reduced the otherwise demanding substrate concentrations and provided a form of physical compartmentalisation.

Fifteen years later, the role of freezing became more powerful.

The 2025 experiments we encountered earlier combined activated RNA trinucleotides with cycles of pH, freezing and thawing.

When RNA duplexes were separated, triplet substrates helped keep the strands from immediately reannealing.

During the frozen phase, solutes became concentrated.

The polymerase ribozyme copied templates.

Across repeated cycles, both complementary strands could be amplified.

Random RNA sequence pools changed under replication and selection.

The experiment is one of the clearest demonstrations that an environmental cycle can solve a molecular problem that otherwise appears to require biological machinery.

The freezer, in effect, performs part of the job later taken over by evolved replication systems.

But ice is not a miracle solvent.

Cold slows many chemical reactions.

Some reactions need liquid water over long periods.

Freezing can concentrate salts to damaging levels.

Different compounds partition unevenly between ice and liquid.

Repeated freezing can disrupt membranes.

The 2025 RNA system also relied on an evolved polymerase ribozyme, activated triplet substrates and laboratory-imposed pH changes and replenishment.

It did not show a frozen pond spontaneously developing life.

And there is no evidence that the first evolving RNA population actually lived in ice.

The important principle is environmental again.

Freezing is not simply the absence of chemistry.

It changes chemistry.

It concentrates.

It protects.

It compartmentalises.

It creates gradients.

Thawing then releases products into a different reaction environment.

On a young planet, cold and warm chemistry need not be rival origin theories.

A molecule formed in a warm environment can later encounter ice.

A cold region can preserve products made elsewhere.

Seasonal, daily or geothermal temperature changes can move chemistry between states.

Even within an icy environment, thin liquid channels coexist with solid water.

This is another warning against asking one condition to do everything.

Wet–dry cycles exploit loss of water.

Ice exploits exclusion by solid water.

Hydrothermal pores exploit continuous flow.

Sunlight supplies photons.

Minerals supply surfaces and metals.

Each creates concentration and energy in a different way.

The first life needed neither a perfect environment nor every environment.

It needed a sequence of physical conditions in which useful chemistry could persist long enough to become evolvable.

The remaining candidates now fit on the same map.

And the map has no marked birthplace.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Attwater et al. (2010), *Nature Communications*: eutectic ice promotes RNA polymerase-ribozyme activity, protects RNA and creates concentrated microenvironments.
- Attwater et al. (2025), *Nature Chemistry*: trinucleotide substrates plus pH/freeze–thaw cycles enable exponential replication of complementary RNA strands by a polymerase ribozyme and selection in sequence pools.
- The 2025 system used laboratory-supplied ribozyme/substrates and controlled cycling; it is not autonomous abiogenesis.

**Decision:** ice is treated as a demonstrated concentration/protection/cycling medium with origin relevance, not as a proven site of life's origin.
