# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 123 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### BEFORE RNA, YOU NEED NUCLEOTIDES

An RNA world cannot begin with RNA already available.

Its building blocks need an explanation.

Each ordinary RNA nucleotide contains three linked components:

a nucleobase,

a ribose sugar,

and phosphate.

If those pieces are imagined forming separately and then being snapped together, the chemistry becomes difficult.

Ribose forms among mixtures of related sugars.

Some nucleobases are easier to produce than others.

Joining a preformed base to ribose in the correct orientation can be inefficient.

Phosphorylation and chemical activation create further problems.

For years, these difficulties were among the strongest objections to simple RNA-first stories.

The important advance was to stop insisting that nature had to assemble nucleotides in the same conceptual order used in a textbook diagram.

In 2009, Matthew Powner, Béatrice Gerland and John Sutherland demonstrated a route to activated pyrimidine ribonucleotides under conditions designed to be prebiotically plausible.

Instead of first making a finished ribose and a finished pyrimidine base and then forcing them together, the pathway built the sugar-and-base framework through shared intermediates.

The result did not manufacture an RNA world.

But it showed that a long-standing synthetic obstacle was not absolute.

Other work then broadened the chemistry.

Researchers found networks in which related feedstocks can yield precursors relevant not only to RNA, but also to amino acids and lipids.

In one influential 2015 system, hydrogen cyanide-derived chemistry driven by ultraviolet light and sulfur-containing reductants generated precursor chemistry feeding all three classes.

This matters because the first evolving systems may not have lived in chemically isolated categories.

RNA, peptide and membrane chemistry could have overlapped from early stages.

Yet laboratory success comes with warnings.

A reaction may be called “prebiotically plausible” because its starting materials and physical conditions could occur without biology.

That does not prove that all required reagents were present at the same place, concentration and time on Hadean Earth.

A multistep sequence may work because a chemist changes conditions between stages.

Side products may interfere in a messy natural mixture.

Useful compounds may hydrolyse or react away before they accumulate.

A pathway may require ultraviolet light at one stage and protection from it at another.

Geology has to supply the setting in which the chemistry remains connected.

There is another complication.

The four canonical bases used by modern RNA need not have been the only nucleobases available at the beginning.

Prebiotic chemistry can produce non-canonical nucleosides and nucleotides too.

Chemical evolution may have begun with a more diverse molecular alphabet and narrowed later.

So the question is no longer:

**Can any plausible chemistry make an RNA building block?**

The answer is yes.

The harder question is systems-level:

**Can a natural environment continually make, concentrate, activate and select enough compatible building blocks for copying chemistry to begin?**

That remains open.

The early Earth did not need to follow one elegant reaction scheme drawn on one page.

But every proposed pathway has to survive contact with the same planet.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Powner, Gerland & Sutherland (2009), *Nature*: activated pyrimidine ribonucleotide synthesis through a pathway that avoids simple direct attachment of preformed ribose and base.
- Patel et al. (2015), *Nature Chemistry*: common cyanosulfidic chemistry produced precursors relevant to ribonucleotides, amino acids and lipids.
- Yadav, Kumar & Krishnamurthy (2020), *Chemical Reviews*: abiotic nucleotide synthesis remains a continuing challenge; progress should not be equated with a solved complete origin pathway.
- Fialho, Roche & Hud (2020), *Chemical Reviews*: prebiotic chemistry can include non-canonical nucleosides/nucleotides.
- 2026 review literature emphasises realistic “messy” reaction networks and environmental filtering.

**Decision:** prebiotic nucleotide chemistry is presented as experimentally advanced but not as a complete, demonstrated Hadean production line.
