# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 138 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### ELECTRONS ARE FUEL

Life can run on light.

It can also run on electrons moving from one chemical compound to another.

These are **redox reactions**.

One substance loses electrons.

Another gains them.

When the overall transfer is energetically favourable, some of the released free energy can be captured to drive other chemistry.

Modern cells build elaborate electron-transfer chains to do this.

Early Earth supplied redox chemistry without cells.

One important source came from **serpentinization**.

When water reacts with certain iron-rich rocks from Earth's mantle, the minerals are altered into new hydrated phases.

Iron is oxidised.

Hydrogen gas can be produced.

The process also creates strongly alkaline fluids and a chemically reducing environment.

This still happens on Earth today.

The significance for life's origin is not that a modern serpentinizing system is a preserved Hadean birthplace.

It is that rock and water can continuously manufacture a powerful electron donor.

Hydrogen, H₂, matters because it can supply electrons for reducing carbon dioxide.

Modern methanogens and acetogens use H₂ and CO₂ in some of the most ancient-looking forms of carbon metabolism known.

Their pathways are now controlled by sophisticated enzymes and cofactors.

But the underlying chemical pair is simple:

reduced hydrogen,

oxidised carbon.

Put them together and there is free energy available — if chemistry can find a route across the kinetic barriers.

Hydrothermal environments can also create other disequilibria.

Hot reduced fluid mixes with cooler seawater.

Different oxidation states of sulfur and iron meet.

Minerals precipitate.

Temperature changes.

pH changes.

Reactants continuously arrive and products can be carried away.

A flow-through environment therefore differs from a closed flask.

It does not necessarily drift once toward equilibrium and stop.

Geology keeps supplying fresh imbalance.

That is attractive for origins research because life itself is a flow-through process.

Matter enters.

Matter leaves.

Energy is dissipated.

The organised system persists only while the flow continues.

A 2010 proposal by Michael Russell and colleagues argued that serpentinization could have supplied hydrogen, methane, reduced carbon compounds and natural redox and proton gradients to alkaline hydrothermal systems on early Earth.

The broad geochemistry is sound.

The stronger claim — that this particular setting powered the origin of life — is a hypothesis.

We can test pieces of it.

In 2025, Vanessa Helmbrecht and colleagues grew iron-sulfide “chemical gardens” under conditions designed to simulate aspects of early-Archaean hydrothermal geochemistry.

Formation of the iron-sulfide minerals mackinawite and greigite produced hydrogen abiotically.

The amount was sufficient to support growth of the modern hyperthermophilic methanogen *Methanocaldococcus jannaschii*.

Its carbon fixation and methane production increased in response to the geochemically generated H₂.

That experiment does **not** show life appearing from minerals.

The organism was already a fully evolved archaeon with enzymes, a genome and membranes.

What the experiment demonstrates is narrower:

an iron-sulfide geochemical system can produce enough usable chemical fuel to power an existing H₂-dependent metabolism.

The planet can supply the battery.

The unsolved step is how non-living chemistry first learned to connect that battery to self-maintenance and heredity.

And hydrogen is only half of the hydrothermal argument.

The other half involves protons.

A difference in pH across a boundary is itself stored energy.

Modern life uses such gradients everywhere.

Could geology have provided the first one for free?

## Research / fact-check notes
- Russell et al. (2010), *Geobiology*: serpentinization as a sustained source of H₂, reduced compounds and natural redox/proton disequilibria.
- Serpentinization and H₂ production are established geochemical processes; a specific role in life's origin remains hypothetical.
- Helmbrecht et al. (2025), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: mackinawite/greigite formation in simulated early-Archaean chemical gardens generated abiotic H₂ sufficient to support growth, CO₂ fixation and methanogenesis by *Methanocaldococcus jannaschii*.
- The 2025 experiment used a modern organism and is not evidence for spontaneous abiogenesis.

**Decision:** geological H₂ production is treated as an established possible energy source. Its conversion into a prebiotic metabolic network remains unresolved.
