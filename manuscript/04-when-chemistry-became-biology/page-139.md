# CHAPTER FOUR — WHEN CHEMISTRY BECAME BIOLOGY
## Page 139 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### A VENT IS A BATTERY — NOT A VERDICT

Every cell alive today uses differences across membranes.

A proton concentration can be higher on one side than the other.

That difference stores free energy.

Allow protons to flow back through molecular machinery and the flow can be coupled to chemical work.

In modern cells, the most famous example is ATP synthase.

It acts like a molecular rotary machine, using an ion gradient to help manufacture ATP.

Because this strategy is so deeply embedded in biology, some researchers have asked whether the first energy gradients existed **before** biological membranes and pumps.

Alkaline hydrothermal vents provide a possible setting.

Serpentinization can produce alkaline, hydrogen-rich fluids.

Those fluids can meet ocean water with a different pH and redox state.

Minerals precipitate where the waters mix.

Porous chimney structures can form.

In origin models developed by Michael Russell, William Martin, Nick Lane and others, microscopic mineral barriers inside such systems act as natural compartments.

On one side is alkaline vent fluid.

On the other is relatively more acidic ocean water.

The pH difference creates a natural proton gradient.

Iron–nickel sulfide minerals may provide catalytic surfaces.

Hydrogen supplies reducing power.

Carbon dioxide supplies carbon.

The appeal is obvious.

The environment would provide several functions later performed by cells:

compartments,

catalysts,

electron donors,

carbon supply,

and a persistent electrochemical gradient.

Modern systems such as the Lost City hydrothermal field demonstrate parts of the geochemical picture.

Lost City is driven by serpentinization rather than volcanic magma.

Its vent fluids are warm rather than black-smoker hot and can reach strongly alkaline pH.

Carbonate structures create porous mineral architectures.

But Lost City is not a fossil of life's birthplace.

It exists in today's oxygenated ocean.

Its rocks, seawater chemistry and biological communities differ from Hadean conditions.

It is an analogue for processes, not a preserved replica.

There is also a deeper problem.

A gradient is useful only if chemistry can **tap it**.

Modern cells use proteins embedded in thin organic membranes.

Before proteins and genetically encoded ion pumps, what molecular system converted a geological pH difference into carbon fixation or other useful work?

That step remains unsettled.

In 2016, J. Baz Jackson published a detailed critique of the natural-proton-gradient hypothesis.

He noted that there was no direct evidence for the thin, stable inorganic membranes with sharp pH gradients required by some models at modern alkaline vents such as Lost City.

He also argued that proposed pre-protein mechanisms for harnessing such a gradient remained chemically problematic.

Supporters of the vent model respond that modern chemiosmosis is so ancient and so universal that environmental gradients remain an attractive precursor, and laboratory systems continue to test ways in which mineral barriers and simple chemistry might couple proton differences to carbon reduction.

Both points can be true.

**Alkaline hydrothermal systems generate real disequilibria.**

And:

**we do not yet know that early chemistry could exploit those disequilibria in the way the origin model requires.**

So the correct conclusion is not that vents are proven cradles of life.

Nor is it that vents are irrelevant.

They are testable natural reactors with several ingredients an origin scenario needs.

The claim becomes scientific precisely because each part can be challenged.

Can the gradient persist?

Can the barrier be thin enough?

Can carbon chemistry be driven?

Can products accumulate?

Can protocells escape from mineral pores while retaining their chemistry?

Until those questions join into one working pathway, the vent remains a candidate environment.

A battery.

Not a verdict.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Sojo et al. (2016), *Astrobiology*: alkaline-vent origin model involving H₂-rich alkaline fluids, CO₂-rich ocean water, Fe(Ni)S micropores and natural proton gradients; authors explicitly note that pre-protein coupling of gradients to carbon reduction remains unresolved.
- Russell et al. (2010), *Geobiology*: serpentinization-driven proton/redox disequilibrium model.
- Jackson (2016), *Journal of Molecular Evolution*: critical review arguing that sharp natural pH gradients across suitable thin inorganic vent membranes and plausible pre-protein coupling mechanisms remain unsupported.
- Modern Lost City is used only as a process analogue, not as a direct reconstruction of the Hadean ocean.

**Decision:** natural proton-gradient models are treated as plausible and experimentally testable but contested; no unique hydrothermal birthplace is asserted.
