# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 167 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### A MILLIMETRE CAN HOLD A WORLD

A microbial mat can be thinner than your finger.

Chemically, it can contain several environments stacked on top of one another.

Light enters from above.

Its intensity falls with depth.

Different wavelengths penetrate different distances.

Gases diffuse.

Dissolved compounds rise from sediment.

Cells consume reactants and release products.

Within millimetres, pH and redox conditions can change sharply.

Modern mats show just how extreme this spatial compression can become.

One layer performs photosynthesis.

Nearby organisms consume the organic products.

Deeper layers ferment material.

Sulfur-reducing organisms generate sulfide.

Phototrophic or chemotrophic sulfur oxidisers consume it.

Methanogens can operate in strongly reduced zones.

Other microbes use nitrogen or metals.

The exact arrangement changes through a day as light, temperature and chemistry shift.

The mat is a **bioreactor built by ecology**.

This makes microbial mats useful analogues for thinking about early ecosystems.

It also creates a trap.

A modern mat often contains oxygenic cyanobacteria at or near its surface.

We cannot simply remove the animals and plants from a modern mat and call the remainder Archean.

Every lineage has continued evolving for billions of years.

Modern sulfate concentrations differ.

Modern atmospheric oxygen changes redox chemistry.

Modern genomes carry innovations unavailable to the first mats.

Actualism has limits.

What transfers more safely is the physics.

Light comes from one direction.

Reduced chemicals can diffuse from another.

Metabolism modifies the local environment.

Different reactions are favoured at different chemical potentials.

Close packing encourages exchange.

A mat therefore creates niches automatically.

Imagine a pre-oxygenic mat at a shallow Archean shoreline.

The top layer receives sunlight.

Anoxygenic phototrophs could use sulfide, hydrogen or perhaps Fe²⁺ as electron donors.

Organic carbon made near the surface filters downward.

Fermenters and respirers consume it.

Reduced products move upward.

Sulfur compounds cycle.

Methane may escape.

Minerals precipitate where chemical fronts meet.

The exact organisms are speculative.

The **gradient logic** is not.

This helps explain why a simple-looking stromatolite can record a complex ecology.

The lamination is built at the interface between biology and sediment.

But beneath the surface producing that structure, many reactions can occur without each leaving its own visible fossil.

One rock texture therefore cannot tell us the complete membership of the mat.

Isotope chemistry samples a different part of the story.

Minerals sample another.

Modern analogues show which ecological arrangements are physically possible.

Together, they constrain a reconstruction.

Microbial mats also concentrate evolution.

A mutation that changes pigment can alter which light layer a cell occupies.

A new sulfur metabolism opens a chemical niche.

Better adhesion keeps a cell in the community.

A secreted polymer changes sediment trapping.

Cross-feeding rewards proximity.

Competition rewards efficiency.

Environmental fluctuations select for resilience.

Billions of microscopic interactions occur in a physically structured space.

That makes a mat more than a survival strategy.

It is an evolutionary arena.

Some of Earth's earliest convincing biological structures may therefore record communities in which metabolism, ecology and sediment were already inseparable.

The planet's living skin was thin.

Its chemistry was deep.

And every reaction in that thin layer fed back into the ocean, sediment or atmosphere around it.

Scale those communities across shorelines and shallow seas and microbial metabolism begins to influence more than local rock.

It begins to influence Earth.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Modern microbial mats exhibit steep light, pH, redox, O₂/sulfide and nutrient gradients over millimetre scales and contain tightly coupled metabolic guilds.
- Modern mats are functional analogues, not direct phylogenetic reconstructions of Archean mats.
- Pre-GOE mats cannot automatically be assigned modern cyanobacterial/sulfate-rich vertical structures.
- Dresser/MISS/stromatolite evidence demonstrates ancient mat architecture; internal functional zonation is reconstructed from physical/ecological principles with explicit uncertainty.

**Decision:** use modern mats to explain gradient-driven ecosystem structure while avoiding taxonomic or oxygen-cycle back-projection.
