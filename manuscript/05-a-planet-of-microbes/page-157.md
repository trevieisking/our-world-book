# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 157 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### METHANE, SULFUR AND THE INVISIBLE ECOSYSTEM

A microbial mat leaves structure.

A metabolism can leave chemistry.

When organisms process carbon, sulfur or nitrogen, their enzymes often favour one isotope slightly over another.

The effect is tiny in a single reaction.

Across an ecosystem, it can become measurable in rock.

This is one reason ancient isotopes are so valuable.

They can preserve evidence of biological processing even when no recognisable cell survives.

They are also dangerous.

Abiotic reactions fractionate isotopes too.

Metamorphism can modify original signals.

Different metabolisms can produce overlapping ranges.

An isotope value is therefore not a barcode identifying one organism.

It is evidence that has to be interpreted with geology.

The Dresser Formation provides several examples.

Carbonaceous material in ancient hydrothermal veins can be extremely depleted in carbon-13.

Values reported from some organic matter reach roughly **minus 56 per mil**.

Such strong fractionation has been interpreted as evidence for anaerobic chemoautotrophs, including methanogens, whose carbon fixation and methane-related metabolism can generate very light carbon.

For years, one argument supporting that interpretation was environmental.

The veins were thought to represent deep-seafloor hydrothermal conditions where photosynthetic carbon was unlikely.

Later field work changed the setting.

Parts of the Dresser succession are now understood as shallow and locally subaerial within a volcanic caldera.

That does not make the light carbon non-biological.

It broadens the possible biological sources.

Phototrophic carbon fixation becomes harder to exclude simply by invoking darkness.

The lesson is important:

**the isotope did not change. The geological context around it did.**

Sulfur tells another part of the story.

Early Earth contained abundant reduced sulfur species.

Microbes can use sulfur in several ways.

They can oxidise sulfide.

They can reduce sulfate.

They can disproportionate intermediate sulfur compounds, using the same substance as both electron donor and electron acceptor.

Sulfur isotope measurements from Dresser pyrite have been interpreted as evidence of microbial sulfur cycling.

Later work has argued in particular for disproportionation of elemental sulfur in some settings rather than simply projecting modern sulfate-reducing ecosystems backward.

Again, the exact metabolic assignment is less secure than the broader conclusion that biology was interacting with sulfur.

Nitrogen is even more difficult.

Living cells require biologically usable nitrogen.

Atmospheric N₂ is abundant but chemically stubborn.

Modern nitrogen fixation converts it into forms life can use using complex enzymes.

Nitrogen isotope measurements in ancient Dresser organic material have been described as compatible with nitrogen fixation.

But the values are not a unique fingerprint.

Abiotic inputs and later alteration have to be considered.

So we should not build a 3.48-billion-year-old enzyme from one isotope ratio.

What we can say is more interesting at ecosystem scale.

Carbon was being processed.

Sulfur was being processed.

Organic matter accumulated strongly enough to alter minerals and sediment.

Some communities lived in illuminated mats.

Other carbonaceous material occurs in hydrothermal settings.

The biosphere was interacting with several elemental cycles.

That is the beginning of **biogeochemistry**:

life changing the movement of elements through a planet.

At first, the effect may have been local.

A mat.

A pore.

A hydrothermal vein.

A shallow pool.

But each metabolism creates products another metabolism can exploit.

Methane becomes fuel for methane oxidisers.

Sulfur compounds cycle between oxidation states.

Organic carbon becomes food after cells die.

A biosphere becomes more than organisms occupying rock.

It becomes a network of chemical relationships.

To see how far that network had spread, we leave Western Australia.

Across the ancient world, another rock sequence preserves something unusually intimate.

Possible cells living **beneath** the seafloor.

## Research / fact-check notes
- 2025 early-biosphere review: very light Dresser organic-carbon isotope values have been interpreted in terms of anaerobic chemoautotrophy/methanogenesis, but revised shallow/subaerial geological context broadens plausible sources.
- Dresser sulfur-isotope work supports microbial sulfur cycling; specific assignments have included elemental-sulfur disproportionation and sulfate-related metabolism.
- Nitrogen isotope evidence is compatible with early N cycling/fixation but is not uniquely diagnostic.
- Isotope evidence is interpreted as one component of convergent biosignatures, never as a metabolism barcode.

**Decision:** elemental cycling is strongly supported at ecosystem scale; individual Dresser metabolic assignments remain labelled where interpretation depends on context or non-unique isotope fractionation.
