# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 169 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### STROMATOLITES BECOME A LANDSCAPE

The Dresser Formation gives us an early strong snapshot.

A little later, the record becomes richer.

At roughly **3.4 billion years ago**, the Strelley Pool Formation of Western Australia preserves one of the most important windows into early microbial ecosystems.

Its stromatolites are not all the same.

They occur as different morphologies across a carbonate platform.

Some are conical.

Some are domical.

Some are columnar.

Their distribution changes with sediment supply, water depth and hydrothermal influence.

This matters because shape linked systematically to environment is harder to explain as random mineral growth.

A genuine biological structure should respond to its habitat.

Light changes with water depth.

Sediment arrives at different rates.

Currents alter grain movement.

Hydrothermal fluids change chemistry.

A microbial community growing through those gradients can produce different architectures even if the organisms themselves are microscopic.

The 2025 review of Earth's earliest biosphere describes Strelley Pool as preserving the oldest known stromatolites formed on a sediment-starved, peritidal carbonate platform facing the open ocean.

That setting is very different from the volcanic-hydrothermal complexity of Dresser.

Life was not simply repeating one ecological trick.

Microbial communities occupied a broader range of shallow-water environments.

Stromatolites at Strelley Pool also occur with other biosignatures.

Carbonaceous microfossils.

Organic-rich cherts.

Sulfur-bearing minerals.

Isotopic evidence for biological processing.

This convergence is more important than any single spectacular structure.

But even here, not every cone or lamination is automatically biological.

Some siliceous conical structures in the Strelley Pool succession have been interpreted as possible abiotic silica sinters rather than microbial stromatolites.

That disagreement is healthy.

It forces researchers to separate features that genuinely record community growth from structures that only resemble it.

The broader formation still preserves compelling biological evidence.

A disputed structure does not invalidate an entire ecosystem.

Nor should an accepted ecosystem make every nearby structure biological by association.

That is the standard this book will keep.

Stromatolite diversity also cannot be translated directly into species diversity.

Different shapes can be produced by the same microbial community under different physical conditions.

Different organisms can produce similar structures.

A stromatolite is an ecosystem-scale product, not a body fossil with a diagnostic skeleton.

Yet increasing morphological and environmental diversity tells us something important.

Microbial communities were interacting with different sedimentary regimes.

They were persistent enough to build repeated structures.

They occupied broad coastal settings.

And they responded to gradients across a platform.

That is ecology recorded geometrically.

At Dresser, biology writes in sediment.

At Strelley Pool, the handwriting becomes more varied.

The next question is whether any of the writers themselves survived.

For some Strelley Pool rocks, the answer appears to be yes.

Tiny carbonaceous structures preserve something much closer to individual cells.

And that brings us to one of the hardest tests in Precambrian palaeontology:

**how do you tell a fossil cell from a mineral that only looks like one?**

## Research / fact-check notes
- 2025 *Philosophical Transactions B* review: the ~3.4 Ga Strelley Pool Chert preserves diverse stromatolites across environmental gradients on a sediment-starved peritidal carbonate platform.
- Strelley Pool also preserves microfossils and geochemical evidence of microbial activity.
- Some conical siliceous structures have alternative abiotic-sinter interpretations; no formation-wide biological verdict is inferred from one morphology.

**Decision:** stromatolite diversity is treated as evidence of ecological/environmental structure, not a direct count of species.
