# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 159 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### ISUA: OLDER — AND MORE DAMAGED

The Isua Supracrustal Belt in Greenland takes us beyond 3.7 billion years.

It also takes us into more difficult geology.

These rocks began as volcanic and sedimentary material.

Since then they have been buried, heated, deformed and metamorphosed.

Some original sedimentary structures survive.

Many do not.

Organic matter has been converted into graphite.

Minerals recrystallised.

Fluids moved through fractures.

This is exactly where the tests of syngeneity and biogenicity become critical.

The proposed Isua stromatolites made headlines in 2016.

Their shape seemed to offer a spectacularly direct 3.7-billion-year-old fossil.

Detailed three-dimensional work later showed that deformation could explain those structures more convincingly.

The morphological claim weakened.

The chemical evidence did not disappear.

In 2025, Magnus Harding and colleagues analysed graphite preserved inside minerals from metamorphosed detrital sediments and compared it with graphite in later secondary veins.

The two settings behaved differently.

Graphite associated with the sedimentary material showed a relatively narrow carbon-isotope distribution.

Graphite in veins showed a much wider spread consistent with later fluid-related processes.

Some graphite protected inside metamorphic mineral grains retained especially light carbon-isotope values.

Combined with the fact that the graphite follows original sedimentary layers, the researchers interpreted the sediment-associated material as metamorphosed **detrital biological organic matter**.

This is exactly the kind of analysis ancient-life research needs.

Not:

“Graphite means life.”

But:

Where is the graphite?

What rock process put it there?

Does it follow primary sedimentary structure?

Does vein graphite behave differently?

Which grains protected the least-altered signal?

The study estimated peak metamorphic temperatures around 500 degrees Celsius.

The remarkable fact is not that Isua is pristine.

It is that information survives despite not being pristine.

Then, in 2026, another study asked a more ambitious ecological question.

Could the 3.7-billion-year-old sediments preserve evidence not only of organic production, but of organisms **consuming and recycling** that organic matter?

Researchers combined carbon and sulfur isotopes with petrography and iron concentrations from a sedimentary rock core.

They interpreted the patterns as consistent with anaerobic respiration linked to iron reduction and sulfur metabolism.

In their reconstruction, early microbes were not simply producing biomass.

Different metabolic processes interacted within sediment.

If correct, this would push evidence for a structured anaerobic ecosystem deep into the Eoarchaean.

But the caution level must rise with the age.

Each isotope proxy can have abiotic alternatives.

Metamorphism complicates original compositions.

The metabolism is inferred from patterns rather than observed in a fossil cell.

A 2025 review of the earliest biosphere therefore describes the combined Isua evidence as **suggestive**, while noting that more independent evidence strengthens the case.

Our book will keep that hierarchy.

Dresser at 3.48 billion years gives us an exceptionally strong convergent ecosystem record.

Isua may push the biosphere past 3.7 billion years and the 2025–2026 evidence has strengthened that case.

But older does not automatically mean more certain.

The arrow of time and the arrow of evidence point in opposite directions.

As we go farther back, the biosphere may be closer to its origin.

The rocks become less able to tell us what it was.

Even so, put Isua beside Dresser and Barberton and one conclusion becomes difficult to avoid.

By the time the rock record becomes readable, life is already doing more than one thing.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Harding et al. (2025), *Communications Earth & Environment*: sediment-associated graphite in ≥3.7 Ga Isua metasediments has a narrower δ¹³C distribution than vein graphite and is interpreted as preserved detrital biogenic organic matter; porphyroblast-hosted graphite appears less altered.
- Peak metamorphic temperatures around ~500 °C illustrate the severity of overprint.
- 2026 *Communications Earth & Environment*: carbon/sulfur isotope patterns interpreted as anaerobic respiration involving iron and sulfur in 3.7 Ga marine detrital sediments.
- 2025 early-biosphere review retains caution for Isua because individual biosignatures have abiotic alternatives and rocks are strongly altered.

**Decision:** Isua's geochemical biosignatures are treated as substantial and strengthening evidence, but the specific 3.7 Ga ecosystem reconstruction remains an active frontier.
