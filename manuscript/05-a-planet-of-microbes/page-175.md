# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 175 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE PLANET CONSUMES OXYGEN

Imagine oxygenic photosynthesis begins.

Why does the sky not change immediately?

Because every molecule of oxygen enters a world built without it.

The Archean ocean contains abundant Fe²⁺.

Oxygen oxidises it.

Reduced sulfur compounds react.

Volcanic and hydrothermal gases consume oxidants.

Fresh crust contains reduced minerals.

Methane reacts with oxygen-derived chemistry.

Dead organic matter can be re-oxidised.

Every one of these processes is an **oxygen sink**.

For oxygen to accumulate globally, production has to exceed the combined capacity of the planet to remove it over long periods.

That balance depends on more than the number of photosynthetic cells.

Burial matters.

When organic carbon produced by photosynthesis is buried without being re-oxidised, the corresponding oxidising power can remain in the surface system.

When reduced volcanic gases are abundant, they consume more oxygen.

When fresh reduced minerals weather, they consume more.

Nutrient supply controls how much photosynthetic biomass can be produced.

Ocean circulation controls where oxidants and reductants meet.

Atmospheric escape alters the long-term hydrogen budget.

Tectonics changes all of these fluxes.

This is why the long delay between oxygenic photosynthesis and atmospheric oxygenation does not require oxygen production to be biologically insignificant.

The sinks could simply have been larger.

A 2025 Earth-system modelling study explored this explicitly.

Julia Horne, Colin Goldblatt and Lee Kump tested different possible origins for oxygenic photosynthesis and nitrogen fixation.

Counterintuitively, their model found that an **earlier** origin of oxygenic photosynthesis could produce a **longer** delay before the Great Oxidation Event.

One important reason was nutrient limitation.

If oxygenic photosynthesis arose before large surface phosphorus reservoirs developed, productivity could remain constrained even though the water-splitting machinery already existed.

Strong ocean redox stratification could further recycle or bury phosphorus in ways that restricted global oxygen production.

This is a model, not a recovered history.

Other explanations emphasise changing volcanic reductant fluxes, continental growth, organic-carbon burial, hydrogen escape and tectonic evolution.

The point is that there is no contradiction between:

oxygenic photosynthesis existing hundreds of millions of years before the Great Oxidation Event,

and the atmosphere remaining overwhelmingly anoxic through most of that interval.

The biosphere can acquire a new metabolism before the Earth system allows its waste product to accumulate.

This matters beyond oxygen.

Evolution creates innovations locally.

Planetary consequences depend on flux.

A mutation occurs in cells.

A biogeochemical revolution happens only when the new metabolism becomes abundant enough, persistent enough and connected to the right geological conditions.

By the late Archean, oxygen oases show that the balance was beginning to shift in some places.

Manganese oxidation.

Cerium depletion.

Oxidative nitrogen cycling.

Redox-sensitive metals.

The planet was recording oxygen before the atmosphere was dominated by it.

But most life still inhabited a world where anoxia was normal.

Microbial communities retained anaerobic pathways.

Iron-rich waters persisted.

Sulfur and methane remained central currencies.

Oxygen was an expanding ecological disturbance.

Soon it would become an environmental boundary condition for the entire surface Earth.

That transition belongs to the next chapter.

Before we leave this one, we can finally see what microbes accomplished without animals, plants or complex cells.

They built the first enduring biosphere.

They colonised sea, sediment, rock and land.

They created planetary element cycles.

And one microbial metabolism began rewriting the atmosphere.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Oxygen sinks on an anoxic/reduced Earth include Fe²⁺, reduced sulfur, volcanic/hydrothermal reductants, reduced crustal minerals, methane and re-oxidation of organic matter.
- Horne, Goldblatt & Kump (2025), *Philosophical Transactions B*: Earth-system modelling shows that early oxygenic photosynthesis can coexist with a long pre-GOE delay; phosphorus limitation and ocean redox structure can strongly regulate oxygen accumulation.
- Multiple hypotheses remain for the duration/timing of oxygenation; no single model is treated as established history.

**Decision:** explain the production-versus-sink balance without selecting one exclusive cause for the delayed atmospheric transition.
