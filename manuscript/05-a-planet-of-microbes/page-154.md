# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 154 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### HOW MICROBES WRITE IN STONE

A microorganism is fragile.

A microbial community can build architecture.

Many microbes live in **biofilms**.

Cells attach to a surface and surround themselves with a matrix of secreted polymers.

The material is sticky.

It helps cells remain in favourable places.

It traps particles.

It retains water and nutrients.

It changes the chemistry immediately around the community.

When biofilms become laterally extensive and layered, they can form **microbial mats**.

Modern mats may contain many physiological groups stacked within millimetres.

Light is strongest at the top.

Oxygen, if present, can disappear rapidly with depth.

Sulfide can rise from below.

Different organisms occupy different chemical zones.

One organism's waste becomes another organism's fuel.

The mat behaves like an ecosystem built vertically through chemical gradients.

Ancient mats can affect sediment in recognisable ways.

Sticky surfaces trap and bind grains.

Mats stabilise sediment that flowing water would otherwise move.

Growth around buried material produces characteristic relief.

Gas can accumulate beneath cohesive films.

Erosion can tear mat fragments away.

Repeated growth and burial can produce laminations.

Geologists call some of these features **microbially induced sedimentary structures**, or MISS.

Another famous product is the **stromatolite**.

Stromatolites are laminated sedimentary structures whose growth can be associated with microbial communities trapping particles, binding sediment and promoting mineral precipitation.

Modern microbial mats make stromatolites in some environments.

Ancient stromatolites can therefore be powerful evidence for life.

But shape alone is dangerous.

Sediment and minerals can produce laminations without organisms.

Precipitating minerals can build domes.

Deformation can turn layers into structures that resemble biological growth.

The disputed 3.7-billion-year-old Isua “stromatolites” from Chapter Four are a warning: three-dimensional geological analysis later showed that apparently biological shapes could be explained more plausibly by deformation.

A convincing stromatolite must therefore belong to its environment.

Does it grow upward from a sedimentary surface?

Does its lamination respond to topography in a way consistent with accretion?

Do many structures occur together?

Does their shape change predictably with water depth, sediment supply or environmental energy?

Are the surrounding rocks genuinely sedimentary?

Is carbonaceous material associated with the structure?

Can deformation be ruled out?

MISS require the same discipline.

One wrinkle is not a microbial mat.

A repeated association of textures across a mapped depositional environment is much stronger.

This is why detailed field geology matters as much as a microscope.

The biosphere is reconstructed at multiple scales.

A carbon grain micrometres across.

A laminated structure centimetres high.

A sedimentary bed metres thick.

A volcanic basin kilometres wide.

All of them have to tell a compatible story.

In the Dresser Formation, they do.

The rocks preserve associations of stromatolites, MISS, carbonaceous material and hydrothermal deposits within a well-constrained ancient landscape.

The community itself has disappeared.

Its interaction with sediment survived.

That gives us a crucial lesson for the rest of Earth's early fossil record:

**the oldest fossils are often not bodies.**

They are behaviours frozen into geology.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Noffke et al. (2013), *Astrobiology*: MISS in the ca. 3.48 Ga Dresser Formation mapped across distinct sedimentary sub-environments.
- 2025 early-biosphere review: Dresser stromatolites and MISS provide major evidence for Palaeoarchaean microbial mats.
- Allwood et al. (2018), *Nature*: three-dimensional geological reassessment weakened the proposed 3.7 Ga Isua stromatolite interpretation.

**Decision:** stromatolites and MISS are used as contextual biosignatures, not automatically biological shapes.
