# CHAPTER FIVE — A PLANET OF MICROBES
## Page 174 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN BEFORE AN OXYGENATED WORLD

A photosynthetic mat can produce oxygen while the atmosphere above it remains almost completely anoxic.

That sounds contradictory only if we imagine Earth's atmosphere and ocean mixing instantly.

They do not.

A molecule of oxygen released by a microbial cell first enters its local environment.

There it encounters chemicals eager to consume it.

Ferrous iron.

Reduced sulfur.

Organic matter.

Hydrogen.

Methane.

Reduced minerals.

If oxygen production briefly outruns those local sinks, a small **oxygen oasis** can form.

Outside that oasis, the wider ocean and atmosphere can remain reducing.

This gives geologists a way to look for oxygen before the Great Oxidation Event.

They do not search only for ancient bubbles of O₂.

They search for chemical consequences of oxidation.

Iron changes oxidation state.

Manganese oxidation becomes possible.

Redox-sensitive metals such as molybdenum, uranium, rhenium and thallium behave differently.

Cerium is removed from seawater when oxidised.

Nitrogen cycling changes when nitrate becomes stable.

Each proxy records a different part of the system.

No single one should carry the entire argument.

The 2025 cerium study places oxygen production by about 2.87 billion years ago.

Younger Neoarchean rocks make the spatial picture clearer.

In 2025, Xinming Chen and colleagues used thallium isotopes in shales to infer seafloor manganese-oxide burial at about **2.65 billion years ago**.

Manganese oxidation requires substantial oxidising power.

The authors concluded that oxygen penetrated to marine sediments beneath regionally extensive oxygen oases.

The signal was not continuous.

Other intervals lacked the same thallium-isotope evidence.

Oxygenation was dynamic.

A second 2025 synthesis combined nitrogen and thallium isotope records with cyanobacterial evolutionary evidence and argued that oxygenated bottom waters could exist on productive marine shelves roughly 200 million years before the Great Oxidation Event.

In that picture, benthic oxygen-producing mats generated enough local O₂ to stabilise nitrate and manganese oxides near the seafloor.

The atmosphere above could still remain mostly anoxic.

This creates a very different mental image from a single planetary switch.

Early oxygen did not necessarily rise evenly from zero.

It may have appeared as patches.

Daily at first.

Seasonally.

Around productive microbial mats.

In shallow basins.

Then disappeared when reduced chemicals consumed it.

Some oxygen escaped farther.

Some changed minerals.

Some enabled new metabolisms.

Aerobic respiration can harvest far more energy from many organic compounds than common anaerobic alternatives.

Even tiny oxygenated niches therefore create new ecological opportunities.

Organisms that tolerate oxygen gain a refuge.

Organisms that use it gain an energetic advantage.

Others are poisoned or forced into deeper anoxic zones.

A new chemical boundary appears inside microbial communities.

We should not imagine modern oxygenated seawater at 2.8 billion years ago.

The evidence supports local or episodic oxygen production long before persistent atmospheric oxygenation.

The distinction is central.

**Oxygenic photosynthesis is a biological innovation.**

**The Great Oxidation Event is a planetary geochemical transition.**

They are related.

They are not the same event.

Between them lies an interval in which microbes can make oxygen faster than the planet can keep it.

Why did that interval last so long?

Because early Earth contained an enormous appetite for oxygen.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Patry et al. (2025), *Nature*: direct depositional Ce-oxidation evidence at 2.87–2.78 Ga.
- Chen et al. (2025), *Nature Geoscience*: thallium-isotope evidence for transient marine bottom-water oxygenation and manganese-oxide burial by ~2.65 Ga.
- Boden, Ostrander & Stüeken (2025), *Nature Geoscience*: synthesis supporting oxygenated Neoarchean shelf bottom waters generated by benthic microbial mats before the GOE.
- Oxygen-oasis proxies are spatially and temporally variable and do not imply an oxygenated atmosphere.

**Decision:** local oxygen production/accumulation is kept separate from global atmospheric oxygenation.
