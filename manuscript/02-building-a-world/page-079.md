### THE ZIRCON THAT SAW WATER

The Jack Hills zircons give us one of the oldest surviving clues to Earth's surface conditions.

One grain has a U–Pb age of **4.404 ± 0.008 billion years**. Later atom-probe work strengthened confidence that this ancient age is genuine.

The more subtle clue is oxygen.

Some ancient zircons have oxygen-isotope compositions that are difficult to explain using only untouched mantle-derived material. A strong interpretation is that their source material first interacted with liquid water near Earth's surface, was later buried and melted, and then contributed to magma from which zircon crystallised.

That is not the same thing as finding an ancient ocean directly.

It is geochemical inference preserved in a mineral after the original rock disappeared.
