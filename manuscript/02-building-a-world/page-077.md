### WHERE DID THE WATER COME FROM?

The familiar story says that young Earth was dry, comets arrived, and the oceans appeared.

The evidence is more complicated.

Earth accreted from material that already contained hydrogen and other volatile elements. Additional volatile-rich material arrived from farther out in the Solar System. Some water was stored in the mantle, some was released by outgassing, some was lost to space, and later impacts could add more.

Carbonaceous-chondrite-like asteroids are important because their isotopic compositions are broadly compatible with a major contribution to terrestrial water. Samples returned from the carbon-rich asteroid Ryugu strengthen the case that hydrated primitive asteroids carried water-bearing material into the inner Solar System.

But even here, **source** is probably plural.

Deep-mantle krypton indicates that some carbonaceous material reached Earth during relatively early accretion. Other isotope systems indicate later additions from farther out in the Solar System as well.

Comets may have contributed.

They are not required to have supplied the entire ocean.

So the question:

**"Did Earth's water come from asteroids, comets or the mantle?"**

is probably too simple.

The better question is:

**how much came from each reservoir, and when?**

The ocean is not one delivery receipt.

It is the surviving balance of a planetary accounting system that began before Earth finished forming.

**Decision:** Earth's water is treated as a multi-stage inventory, not a one-source comet story.
