### THE BOMBARDMENT THAT WAS NOT ONE SIMPLE EVENT

Look at the Moon.

Its surface is a record of violence.

Basins overlap older craters. Younger craters sit on top of older ones. Some structures are hundreds or thousands of kilometres across.

The broad conclusion is secure:

**the inner Solar System was struck far more intensely during its first billion years than it is today.**

The detailed timing is less secure.

For decades, textbooks often presented a sharp **Late Heavy Bombardment** near 3.9 billion years ago: a sudden cataclysm long after the planets had formed.

The idea grew partly from Apollo impact-melt ages that seemed to cluster around that interval.

Modern work treats that simple picture more cautiously.

Apollo sampled only limited regions of the Moon. One enormous basin can scatter material across huge areas. Later impacts can reset isotopic clocks. Different samples may therefore record the same giant event rather than many independent impacts.

Dynamical models also permit more complicated histories.

Some impactors were leftovers from terrestrial-planet formation.

Others may have been destabilised later as the giant planets changed their orbits.

A 2017 major review concluded that the strongest version of one narrow terminal cataclysm had been substantially weakened and favoured a hybrid history involving more than one impactor population.

A 2024 review of Earth's Precambrian impact record reached the same broad caution: a distinct late-heavy-bombardment phase around 3.85 billion years ago remains debated, and Earth has no confirmed Hadean crater record with which to settle the argument directly.

That does not mean the bombardment disappeared.

It means the curve is uncertain.

Early Earth was unquestionably hit hard.

The open questions are **how hard, how often, and how those impacts were distributed through time**.

The Moon remembers much more than Earth.

Earth's earliest crater record was almost completely erased by tectonics, erosion, volcanism and burial.

So the Moon is our witness.

A very useful witness.

Not an identical twin.
