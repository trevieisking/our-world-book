# CHAPTER TWO — BUILDING A WORLD
## Page 080 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### A SURFACE EMERGES FROM THE FIRE

The Hadean used to be imagined as an almost continuously hellish interval.

The name itself encouraged the picture.

Molten surface.

Relentless impacts.

No stable water.

No persistent crust.

The evidence now supports something more complicated.

Earth certainly began violently. Accretion generated enormous heat. The Moon-forming collision remade the planet. Magma oceans existed. Atmospheres were lost and rebuilt. Early crust was repeatedly recycled.

None of that is in doubt.

But surviving zircons tell us that cooling and chemical differentiation also happened early.

Crust existed by at least 4.4 billion years ago.

Liquid water probably interacted with some of that crust.

An initial hydrosphere may have existed by the same broad interval.

Fresh meteoric water was interacting with crust by around 4.0 billion years ago or earlier.

What we cannot yet do is draw a trustworthy globe.

We do not know the exact fraction of Earth's surface covered by ocean at 4.4 billion years ago.

We do not know the exact salinity.

We do not know the precise atmospheric pressure.

We do not know whether exposed land was widespread or rare.

We do not know whether the earliest crust behaved under plate tectonics exactly as modern crust does.

And the oldest **robust direct rock record of subaqueous environments** is younger than the zircon-based inferences — around **3.7 billion years**.

This is the evidence hierarchy we need to preserve:

**direct ancient rock environments are strongest where they survive;**

**zircons extend the record farther back through geochemical inference;**

**numerical models explore conditions that could connect the surviving clues.**

Together they transform our picture of early Earth.

The planet did not wait until the Archean to begin becoming physically habitable.

At least some prerequisites — crust, liquid water, atmosphere and active water–rock chemistry — may have been present surprisingly early.

That does not mean life existed then.

It means the stage may have been built.

The next question is what happened on it.
