### A SURFACE EMERGES FROM THE FIRE

The Hadean used to be imagined as an almost continuously hellish interval.

The name itself encouraged the picture.

Molten surface.

Relentless impacts.

No stable water.

No persistent crust.

The evidence now supports something more complicated.

Earth certainly began violently. Accretion generated enormous heat. The Moon-forming collision remade the planet. Magma oceans existed. Atmospheres were lost and rebuilt. Early crust was repeatedly recycled.

None of that is in doubt.

But surviving zircons tell us that cooling and chemical differentiation also happened early.

Crust existed by at least 4.4 billion years ago.

Liquid water probably interacted with some of that crust.

An initial hydrosphere may have existed by the same broad interval.

Fresh meteoric water was interacting with crust by around 4.0 billion years ago or earlier.

What we cannot yet do is draw a trustworthy globe.
