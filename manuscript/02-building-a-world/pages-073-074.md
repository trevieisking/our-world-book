## PAGE 073 — COOLING A PLANET THAT REFUSES TO COOL

After the Moon-forming era, Earth had a new problem.

It had to get rid of heat.

Hot objects radiate energy into space. But early Earth was not a bare hot sphere cooling in a vacuum. It was wrapped in gas.

A hot silicate surface releases volatiles dissolved in the melt. Water, carbon-bearing gases, nitrogen-bearing species, sulfur-bearing gases and other components escape from magma as pressure falls and crystallisation proceeds. The atmosphere and magma therefore evolve together.

A thick atmosphere can slow cooling by trapping outgoing infrared radiation. Water vapour is a powerful greenhouse gas. Carbon dioxide is another. If the atmosphere becomes optically thick enough, the surface can remain molten much longer than it would without an atmosphere.

At the same time, the atmosphere is not permanent. Hydrogen-bearing species can escape to space. Impacts can remove gas or add more. Volcanism can replenish it. Condensation can transfer water from atmosphere to surface. Weathering, once liquid water and exposed rock exist, can remove carbon dioxide from the air and store carbon in minerals.

So "Earth cooled" is not one event. It is a coupled problem involving the mantle, the surface, the atmosphere, incoming sunlight and space.

Modern models do not produce one universal cooling time. They depend on the amount of water and carbon dioxide, the initial thermal state, atmospheric radiation, later impacts and the way the magma ocean crystallised.

Some models allow the upper mantle to solidify on geologically short timescales. Others retain molten or partially molten material much longer at depth. Recent work even suggests that crystallisation can preserve a deep basal magma ocean above the core after much of the overlying mantle has solidified.

So we should not picture Earth passing neatly from **MOLTEN** to **SOLID** overnight.

It probably moved through intermediate states.

A surface crust could form, be broken, remelted, buried, resurfaced and formed again.

Cooling was progress.

It was not peace.

## PAGE 074 — THE FIRST CRUST WAS NOT A CONTINENT

The word **crust** can make us imagine continents.

That is a mistake.

Earth can have a crust without having anything resembling modern continental crust.

As a magma ocean cools, solid minerals begin to crystallise. Eventually, a solid outer shell can form. That earliest shell is often called **protocrust**.

What was it made of?

That question is difficult because almost none of the original Hadean crust survives as intact rock.

Most reconstructions favour an early crust dominated by mafic or ultramafic compositions — chemically closer to basaltic or mantle-derived rocks than to modern granite-rich continents. A 2025 study in *Nature* argued that Earth's Hadean protocrust was extracted from the magma ocean and was predominantly mafic, with later melting and reworking producing more evolved crustal compositions.

Continental crust did not need to appear fully formed. Chemical differentiation can happen in stages.

Primitive crust forms. Parts of it melt. Some melts become compositionally evolved. Those new rocks can themselves be buried, melted, altered and recycled.

The result is a planet whose crust grows by repeatedly reprocessing earlier material.

The first crust may have been widespread. Almost all of it may have been destroyed. Small pieces of its chemistry survived inside later materials.

Some zircon grains suggest that evolved, silica-rich melts existed very early. But that does not prove that Earth already possessed broad modern-style continents at 4.4 billion years ago.

A granite-producing process is not automatically a continent.

A zircon is not automatically a mountain range.

A few grains cannot draw us a global map.

What they can establish is that crustal differentiation began astonishingly early.

Earth had already started making chemically distinct surface reservoirs long before the oldest surviving continents took their present form.
