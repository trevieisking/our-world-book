# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 197 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE LOMAGUNDI OVERSHOOT — MAYBE

After the atmosphere first crossed into persistent oxygenation, Earth's carbon cycle entered one of its strangest known states.

From roughly **2.22 to 2.06 billion years ago**, many marine carbonates across multiple continents record unusually positive carbon-isotope values.

The interval is called the **Lomagundi–Jatuli Event**.

Typical positive values are large.

Some reported carbonates reach extraordinarily high carbon-13 enrichments.

For decades, the standard interpretation has been straightforward.

Photosynthesis fixes carbon and preferentially incorporates carbon-12 into organic matter.

If an unusually large fraction of that organic carbon is buried, the remaining dissolved inorganic carbon becomes relatively enriched in carbon-13.

Carbonates record the enriched reservoir.

Burying more reduced organic carbon also leaves more oxidising power behind.

Therefore:

large positive carbon-isotope excursion,

large organic-carbon burial,

large oxygen production.

In this picture, atmospheric O₂ temporarily **overshot** its later background level.

The idea is attractive because it links two enormous changes.

The biggest positive carbonate carbon-isotope excursion in Earth's history occurs during the broad interval of its first major oxygenation.

Other geochemical records indicate expanded surface oxidation and ocean oxygenation during parts of the same interval.

A 2025 study also found that marine phosphate availability covaried with carbon-isotope and redox changes across the GOE.

Its models show how temporary increases in phosphorus could stimulate productivity, increase organic-carbon burial and produce oxygen pulses.

But the Lomagundi interpretation is not a solved equation.

Several problems remain.

Carbonates can record local carbon cycling.

Diagenesis can modify isotope values.

The fraction of global carbon burial required by the simplest mass-balance interpretation can become very large.

Different basins do not preserve identical histories.

The exact atmospheric O₂ concentration associated with the excursion is poorly constrained.

Models span a wide range.

Some studies support oxygen levels still well below modern air.

Other models allow much larger temporary increases.

The 2026 GOE review emphasises that we still cannot robustly translate the Lomagundi carbon-isotope excursion into one atmospheric oxygen number.

Even the idea of a single global oxygen “overshoot” remains under active examination.

The safest conclusion is therefore narrower.

**The Lomagundi–Jatuli interval records an extraordinary reorganisation of the global carbon cycle during a time of enhanced surface oxidation.**

Enhanced burial of reduced carbon probably contributed.

Higher atmospheric and marine oxygen than before the GOE is strongly plausible.

The absolute magnitude remains uncertain.

And one more observation prevents a simple evolutionary story.

If Lomagundi really produced a long-lived oxygen-rich interval, the biological response was not an immediate explosion of visible complexity.

The fossil record does not suddenly fill with large oxygen-hungry organisms.

Complex eukaryotic ecosystems do not obviously radiate in lockstep with the isotope excursion.

Oxygen availability and evolutionary innovation are related.

They are not identical.

The atmosphere can become permissive before evolution takes advantage of it.

And after Lomagundi, oxygen did not continue a smooth march toward modern values.

Several records indicate a return toward lower-oxygen conditions.

The first oxygen revolution may have overshot.

Then Earth partially stepped back.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Lomagundi–Jatuli Event: broadly ~2.22–2.06 Ga, Earth's largest/longest positive marine carbonate δ13C excursion.
- Canonical interpretation links enhanced organic-carbon burial to increased net O₂ production.
- Dodd et al. (2025; correction 2026), *Nature Communications*: phosphorus availability, productivity and oxygen were coupled across GOE intervals.
- Crockford et al. (2026), *Communications Earth & Environment*: absolute O₂ amplitude and even the global carbon-cycle interpretation of Lomagundi remain debated.
- Hodgskiss et al. (2023 review): high Paleoproterozoic oxygen is not matched by a clearly synchronous radiation of high-O₂-demand complexity.

**Decision:** Lomagundi is treated as strong evidence of carbon-cycle/redox reorganisation and probable enhanced oxygenation, not as a precisely quantified atmospheric O₂ overshoot.
