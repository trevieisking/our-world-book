# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 183 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN, METHANE AND ICE

The early Sun was fainter than today's.

Something helped keep liquid water stable on much of the young Earth.

Carbon dioxide contributed.

Methane may have contributed strongly too.

In an anoxic atmosphere, biological methane can survive far longer than it does in modern air.

Methanogens continuously resupply it.

Photochemistry ultimately destroys it, but without abundant oxygen the removal pathways differ.

Now add oxygen.

O₂ does not simply sit beside methane.

Atmospheric chemistry links them.

More oxidising conditions shorten methane's lifetime and convert reduced carbon toward carbon dioxide.

Because methane is an effective greenhouse gas, a large decrease in atmospheric methane can cool the planet.

The timing is intriguing.

The Great Oxidation interval overlaps with multiple Paleoproterozoic glaciations recorded in the Huronian Supergroup and correlative successions.

Some deposits have been interpreted as evidence for very extensive, possibly global ice.

It is therefore tempting to tell a simple story:

cyanobacteria make oxygen,

oxygen destroys methane,

the greenhouse weakens,

Earth freezes.

That mechanism is physically plausible.

It may have contributed.

It is not established as the sole cause of the glaciations.

Climate at the time also depended on carbon dioxide.

Volcanic degassing.

Silicate weathering.

Continental configuration.

Solar luminosity.

Clouds.

Atmospheric haze.

Ocean circulation.

The timing of individual glaciations and oxygen rises is still being refined.

Some oxygen proxy records suggest oscillatory behaviour across the same broad interval.

Cause and effect may run both ways.

Climate changes weathering and nutrient supply.

Glaciation exposes and grinds rock.

Deglaciation delivers pulses of sediment and phosphorus.

Sea level changes where organic matter is buried.

Temperature affects atmospheric photochemistry.

The oxygen cycle can therefore influence climate while climate feeds back into oxygen.

A 2026 review of the GOE stresses how incomplete our quantitative reconstruction remains.

Defensible atmospheric oxygen concentrations across the event still span orders of magnitude.

That makes precise climate forcing difficult to calculate.

We should therefore preserve the strongest statement:

**the rise of oxygen had the capacity to weaken an Archean methane greenhouse, and the GOE coincides broadly with major Paleoproterozoic glaciations.**

The exact size of methane loss and its causal role in each glaciation remain uncertain.

There is another atmospheric consequence that points in the opposite direction from cooling.

As O₂ rises, some is converted photochemically into **ozone**, O₃.

Ozone absorbs damaging ultraviolet radiation.

An ozone layer changes the spectrum reaching the surface.

That alters atmospheric photochemistry.

It changes the environment experienced by microbes on land and in shallow water.

The oxygen revolution therefore changes climate and radiation simultaneously.

One gas becomes harder to maintain.

Another protective layer appears.

The same biological waste product produces both effects.

This is the beginning of a recurring pattern in Earth history.

Life changes the environment.

The altered environment changes which life can thrive.

The GOE was not simply oxygen accumulating around passive organisms.

It was ecological feedback on a planetary scale.

For anaerobes, oxygen could be poison.

For organisms capable of using it, oxygen became an extraordinary energy opportunity.

For rocks, it opened new weathering pathways.

For the atmosphere, it rewrote photochemistry.

The next page follows those consequences out of the air and into the surface Earth.

## Research / fact-check notes
- GOE broadly overlaps Paleoproterozoic/Huronian glaciations.
- Oxidation of atmospheric methane provides a physically plausible cooling mechanism and is widely discussed, but climate causality also involves CO₂, weathering, tectonics, haze, solar forcing and feedbacks.
- 2026 reviews retain large uncertainty in absolute GOE O₂ trajectories, limiting precise methane/climate reconstruction.
- Rising O₂ permits ozone formation and increasing UV shielding.

**Decision:** methane-loss cooling is presented as plausible contribution, not a uniquely established cause of Paleoproterozoic glaciation.
