# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 198 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN FALLS — BUT DOES NOT VANISH

The end of the Lomagundi carbon-isotope excursion is often drawn as another sharp line.

High oxygen.

Then low oxygen.

Reality is less tidy.

Several geochemical records suggest that atmosphere–ocean oxygenation contracted after the Paleoproterozoic high state.

Widespread deep-ocean anoxia returned.

Iron formations reappear around **1.88 billion years ago**, including in settings interpreted as shallow marine.

That implies ferrous iron once again accumulated in waters close to the surface in at least some regions.

Shales through the later Paleoproterozoic and Mesoproterozoic commonly record low-oxygen or anoxic marine conditions.

This broad return toward more reducing oceans is sometimes called **deoxygenation**.

But the atmosphere did not return permanently to the Archean state.

The strong primary atmospheric sulfur MIF regime did not simply restart globally.

Oxidative weathering continued.

Oxygen-sensitive terrestrial mineral relationships remained different from the Archean.

The ozone cycle persisted to some degree.

The baseline had shifted.

What changed was the **amount and distribution** of oxygen.

Even the timing of the post-Lomagundi decline remains difficult.

A 2018 study proposed a two-step deoxygenation at the end of the Lomagundi Event.

Other work has argued that oxygenated marine conditions persisted after the classic carbon-isotope excursion.

The roughly 2.0-billion-year-old Zaonega Formation in Russia contains striking enrichments in redox-sensitive metals and uranium-isotope signals that have been interpreted as evidence for a still substantially oxygenated atmosphere–ocean system after Lomagundi.

A 2020 study therefore warned against assuming an immediate oxygen collapse.

The 2026 GOE review goes further.

It argues that defining an exact **end** to the Great Oxidation interval may be harder than defining its beginning.

Different proxy systems do not converge neatly onto one global exit.

Some apparent deoxygenation signals are regional.

Some sulfur records are altered by multiple generations of pyrite growth.

Some mid-Proterozoic proxies may represent transient oxygenation events.

Others may indicate that baseline oxygen was simply higher than older models assumed.

The correct picture is therefore not:

Archean anoxia -> GOE -> Lomagundi high oxygen -> complete collapse.

It is more like a staircase with uncertain landings.

The atmosphere crosses a threshold and stays fundamentally more oxidised than before.

Surface ocean oxygen expands and contracts.

Deep-ocean redox states remain heterogeneous.

A high-oxygen episode may occur during Lomagundi.

Later oxygen levels decrease relative to that high state, while still remaining above the Archean atmospheric baseline.

This matters for evolution.

Organisms do not evolve against one global O₂ number.

They evolve in local habitats.

A shallow coastal environment can remain oxygenated while the deep sea is anoxic.

A sediment pore can remain anaerobic beneath oxygenated water.

An organism requiring little oxygen may survive conditions that exclude a more active or larger organism.

The post-Lomagundi world therefore still contains ecological oxygen gradients.

For the next billion years, Earth will not resemble the modern planet.

Atmospheric O₂ is present.

Deep oceans are widely anoxic.

Nutrients are constrained.

Redox-sensitive metals are redistributed.

Complex life is possible in some settings but not guaranteed by the chemistry.

This brings us to one of the most tempting causal stories in evolutionary history.

Oxygen rises.

Complex cells appear.

Therefore oxygen caused complex cells.

The timing does not support such a simple conclusion.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Post-Lomagundi records indicate contraction of oxygenated conditions and widespread return of anoxic oceans, while atmospheric oxygen remains above the Archean baseline.
- ~1.88 Ga iron formations are widely cited as evidence for renewed low-O₂/ferruginous marine conditions.
- Mänd et al. (2020), *Nature Geoscience*: Zaonega redox-sensitive metal and U-isotope data indicate substantial oxygenated conditions persisted after Lomagundi.
- Crockford et al. (2026): exact termination of the GOE and global significance of proposed deoxygenation signals remain unresolved.

**Decision:** post-Lomagundi deoxygenation is treated as a relative decline/redistribution, not a permanent return to pre-GOE atmospheric anoxia.
