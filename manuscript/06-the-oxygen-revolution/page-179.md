# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 179 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### A TRANSITION THAT FLICKERS

If the Great Oxidation Event were a switch, every proxy would change together.

They do not.

That is one reason its exact chronology remains an active research problem.

Some sedimentary records indicate that large S-MIF signals weaken or disappear between roughly **2.50 and 2.43 billion years ago**.

Other well-dated successions preserve substantial S-MIF closer to **2.33–2.32 billion years ago**.

Still younger rocks can contain recycled MIF-bearing sulfur inherited from older crust.

The challenge is separating atmospheric behaviour from sedimentary memory.

A 2026 review therefore describes the beginning of the GOE with deliberately broad wording: Earth's first major rise in atmospheric oxygen occurred across a boundary somewhere between about 2.5 and 2.4 billion years ago, while the event as a whole extends through a longer Paleoproterozoic interval.

New marine proxies add another layer.

In 2025, researchers analysed vanadium and thallium behaviour in South African rocks deposited during the GOE.

Their reconstruction suggests that before about **2.32 billion years ago**, atmospheric oxygen remained low enough for S-MIF to persist and the ocean was still dominantly anoxic.

Around 2.32 billion years ago, the proxy system changed.

Atmospheric O₂ rose above the S-MIF-preservation threshold.

Manganese-oxide burial expanded.

Shallow marine oxygenation then became more persistent.

Yet even after that threshold, much of the deeper seafloor remained functionally anoxic.

This gives us a sequence rather than a switch.

First, local oxygen oases appear in the Archean.

Then oxygen production expands.

Atmospheric oxygen begins repeatedly challenging the global reducing sink.

Some proxies record transient oxidation.

Sulfur photochemistry changes.

Shallow ocean waters increasingly acquire oxygen.

Deep waters lag behind.

The final disappearance of anoxic-atmosphere signals is spread across time and geological basins.

The word **persistent** becomes more useful than the word **first**.

When did oxygen first exist?

Very early, biologically.

When did it first accumulate locally?

Before the GOE.

When did the atmosphere first cross a sulfur-photochemistry threshold?

Around the Archean–Proterozoic transition, with chronology still being refined.

When did shallow ocean oxygenation become sustained over broad regions?

Evidence points to the early Paleoproterozoic, with important changes around 2.32 billion years ago.

When did the deep ocean become broadly oxygenated?

Much later.

That last question belongs to later chapters.

The GOE therefore changes the Earth's **baseline**.

Oxygen no longer has to be created anew inside a tiny oasis to exist at the surface.

It becomes an atmospheric reservoir.

Even a small persistent atmospheric reservoir changes weathering everywhere exposed to air.

Rain carries dissolved oxygen.

Rivers transport oxidised products.

Continental minerals encounter a new reactant.

Ocean margins receive oxidants from above.

This is why crossing a low atmospheric threshold can have consequences disproportionate to the absolute oxygen concentration.

The first persistent oxygenated atmosphere was still not modern air.

It did not need to be.

A trace gas can reorganise chemistry if it reacts strongly enough.

The next question is therefore physical.

What changed in the global oxygen budget?

The microbes had been producing oxygen for hundreds of millions of years.

Why did the sinks finally stop consuming all of it?

## Research / fact-check notes
- Crockford et al. (2026), review: GOE onset chronology and O₂ amplitude remain debated; broad transition begins near 2.5–2.4 Ga.
- Izon et al. (2018): MIF-S preservation can be asynchronous and influenced by crustal memory.
- 2025 *Nature Communications*: multi-proxy reconstruction places a major transition around ~2.32 Ga from extremely low atmospheric O₂ toward persistent shallow-ocean oxygenation, while deeper oceans remain largely anoxic.
- Exact chronology varies by proxy and basin.

**Decision:** present the GOE as a sequence of thresholds and persistence changes rather than one globally synchronous date.
