# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 188 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### SULFATE FLOODS THE SULFUR CYCLE

Before widespread atmospheric oxygen, much of Earth's sulfur cycle revolved around volcanic sulfur, elemental sulfur, sulfide and relatively small sulfate reservoirs.

Oxidative weathering changes that.

Expose pyrite to oxygen and water and sulfur is converted toward sulfate.

Rivers transport that sulfate to the ocean.

As continents experience persistent oxidative weathering, the marine sulfate reservoir can expand.

This has major biological consequences.

Sulfate is an electron acceptor.

Microbes can use it to oxidise organic matter or hydrogen while reducing sulfate to sulfide.

Modern marine sediments host enormous communities of sulfate reducers.

The Archean ocean contained much less sulfate, so those ecosystems were constrained.

After the GOE, sulfate availability increases.

That expands the energetic territory available to anaerobic respiration even while oxygen itself expands near the surface.

This is an important reminder that oxygenation does not simply replace anaerobic life.

It can feed new anaerobic pathways indirectly.

More oxidative weathering on land.

More sulfate in seawater.

More sulfate reduction in anoxic sediment.

More sulfide production.

Where that sulfide meets dissolved iron, **pyrite** forms.

Bury the pyrite and reduced sulfur is removed from the surface environment.

That affects the global oxygen budget.

Oxidising sulfide consumes O₂.

But burying reduced sulfur instead of re-oxidising it can preserve oxidising power over geological time.

The sulfur cycle therefore participates in both oxygen consumption and oxygen accumulation depending on which products are recycled and which are buried.

Sulfur isotopes record the expansion.

Before the GOE, atmospheric S-MIF dominates the global signal.

As that atmospheric signature disappears and seawater sulfate grows, mass-dependent biological fractionations become much more prominent.

Microbial sulfate reducers discriminate among sulfur isotopes.

The size of the fractionation depends on sulfate concentration, metabolic rate and the network of sulfur transformations.

A larger sulfate reservoir allows sulfur to be recycled more extensively before final burial.

The isotopic range recorded in sedimentary sulfides expands.

This gives geologists another view of how atmosphere and ocean became coupled.

Atmospheric oxygen oxidises continental sulfur.

Continental weathering delivers sulfate.

Microbes reduce it.

Iron captures some sulfide.

Burial changes the redox budget.

Metabolism has turned atmospheric oxygen into an oceanic anaerobic resource.

But the sulfate ocean after the GOE was not modern seawater.

Concentrations remained far below today's in many reconstructions.

Deep ocean water often remained anoxic.

Where iron was abundant and sulfide scarce, waters were **ferruginous**.

Where sulfate reduction produced enough sulfide to overwhelm available iron, waters became **euxinic**.

The balance between those states controlled more than sulfur.

It controlled trace metals.

Molybdenum binds strongly to sulfide and can be removed from euxinic water.

Iron minerals scavenge phosphorus and trace elements.

Nutrient inventories change.

The post-GOE ocean therefore develops a chemically layered geography.

Oxygen above.

Iron-rich anoxia in some deeper waters.

Sulfidic anoxia in others.

The atmosphere has crossed a threshold.

The deep ocean has not followed uniformly.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Oxidative pyrite weathering after atmospheric O₂ rise increases sulfate delivery to the ocean.
- Expanded marine sulfate enables greater microbial sulfate reduction and sulfide production.
- Pyrite burial affects the long-term redox budget by retaining reduced sulfur.
- Post-GOE marine sulfate remained below modern levels in many reconstructions; ferruginous and euxinic anoxia persisted.

**Decision:** sulfur-cycle expansion is treated as a consequence and feedback of oxygenation, while absolute sulfate concentrations remain model/proxy dependent.
