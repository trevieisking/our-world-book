# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 200 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### THE WORLD AFTER OXYGEN

Chapter Five ended with microbes changing the air.

Chapter Six ends with the air changing the planet.

The Great Oxidation Event was not one morning when Earth woke beneath a blue, oxygen-rich sky.

It was a long threshold crossing.

Oxygenic photosynthesis had evolved earlier.

Local oxygen oases appeared before the atmosphere changed permanently.

Then, across the Archean–Proterozoic transition, biological production finally exceeded Earth's reducing capacity often enough for atmospheric O₂ to persist.

Sulfur photochemistry changed.

The mass-independent sulfur fingerprint of the Archean atmosphere disappeared as a primary global signal.

Reduced minerals stopped surviving ordinary transport across oxygenated landscapes.

Continental iron oxidised.

Sulfate delivery to the oceans increased.

Trace metals moved differently.

Mineral diversity expanded.

Shallow marine environments became more oxygenated.

The deep ocean remained largely anoxic.

The first oxygenated Earth was a chemically divided planet.

Oxygen itself was both crisis and opportunity.

Reactive oxygen species damaged cells.

Antioxidant and repair systems became increasingly valuable.

Respiratory metabolisms learned to exploit O₂ as a powerful terminal electron acceptor.

Aerobic niches expanded.

Anaerobic ecosystems survived below them.

Ozone began changing ultraviolet radiation at the surface.

The carbon cycle entered the extraordinary Lomagundi–Jatuli disturbance.

Oxygen may have temporarily risen well above its earlier Paleoproterozoic baseline.

Then the Earth system moved toward lower oxygen again.

Not back to the Archean.

Forward into a new state.

The lesson of the GOE is therefore not that oxygen rose once.

It is that biology forced the planet across a chemical threshold from which it never permanently returned.

That threshold changed what evolution could explore.

But it did not dictate what evolution would invent.

High oxygen did not automatically produce complex cells.

A cell still needed a radically different architecture.

Modern eukaryotes — animals, plants, fungi, algae, protists and ourselves — share features absent from bacteria and archaea.

A nucleus surrounds the genome.

Chromosomes are organised and copied through elaborate machinery.

A cytoskeleton moves material and reshapes the cell.

Internal membranes create specialised compartments.

Mitochondria carry a second evolutionary history inside the cell.

Many eukaryotes reproduce sexually.

Some form multicellular bodies in which cells specialise and cooperate.

None of that follows automatically from atmospheric oxygen.

Something happened within microbial evolution.

An archaeal lineage acquired bacterial partners.

One partnership became permanent.

Genes moved between genomes.

Membranes, trafficking systems and energy metabolism were reorganised.

A new kind of cell emerged.

The geological record becomes frustrating again.

Microbes rarely preserve their internal structures.

Genes do not fossilise.

The earliest eukaryotic fossils are debated.

Molecular clocks provide ranges rather than a single date.

Living archaea and bacteria preserve clues, but billions of years of evolution have modified them.

The investigation therefore changes methods once more.

To reconstruct the origin of complex cells, we must combine:

fossils,

cell biology,

molecular phylogenies,

genomes,

endosymbiosis,

biochemistry,

and the chemistry of ancient oceans.

The next transition is not a planet inventing oxygen.

It is cells inventing a new way to be cells.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Chapter 6 synthesis retains the evidence hierarchy for the GOE and avoids treating it as a single instant or modern oxygen state.
- Persistent O₂, redox/nutrient reorganisation and oxygen respiration change evolutionary opportunity without uniquely causing eukaryogenesis.
- Chapter 7 scope agrees with the master plan: eukaryotes, endosymbiosis, sex, multicellularity, Snowball Earth and Ediacaran organisms.

**Decision:** Chapter 6 closes with oxygen as a permanent Earth-system threshold and transitions into the cellular mechanisms of eukaryogenesis rather than claiming a direct oxygen-to-complexity causal chain.
