# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 181 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### BURY CARBON, LEAVE OXYGEN

Oxygenic photosynthesis splits water and reduces carbon dioxide into organic matter.

That creates two products with opposite redox roles.

Reduced carbon.

Oxidising power.

If the organic carbon is rapidly respired, fermented and ultimately re-oxidised, the oxygen gain is cancelled.

If some of that carbon is buried before it can react back with oxygen, the cancellation is incomplete.

The atmosphere can gain oxidising power.

This is the foundation of one of the oldest explanations for Earth's oxygenation.

The principle is simple.

The global accounting is not.

Organic carbon burial depends on biological productivity.

Productivity depends on nutrients.

Burial depends on sedimentation rates, basin geometry, mineral protection and whether oxygen or sulfate reaches the sediment.

Tectonics creates and destroys sedimentary basins.

Erosion supplies nutrients and mineral surfaces.

Ocean circulation redistributes organic matter.

Metamorphism can later return reduced carbon to the surface.

So “more carbon burial” is not one switch.

It is the output of an Earth system.

Carbon isotopes help reconstruct that output.

When photosynthesis fixes carbon, it usually favours carbon-12 over carbon-13.

If large amounts of isotopically light organic carbon are buried, the remaining oceanic dissolved inorganic carbon can become relatively enriched in carbon-13.

Carbonate minerals record part of that change.

This logic becomes especially important later in the GOE during the large positive carbon-isotope excursion known as the **Lomagundi Event**, roughly 2.22 to 2.06 billion years ago in many current age models.

Traditionally, that excursion has been interpreted as evidence for unusually high organic-carbon burial and therefore enhanced net oxygen production.

That interpretation remains influential.

It is not uncontested.

Carbonate isotope records can be modified by local environments, diagenesis and changes in carbon-cycle structure.

The magnitude of organic burial required by the simplest models may be difficult to reconcile with other records.

New work therefore combines carbon isotopes with nutrients and redox proxies rather than reading the excursion alone.

A 2025 study measured carbonate-associated phosphate across GOE-age sedimentary successions.

It found that phosphorus availability and carbonate carbon isotopes covaried through several intervals.

Biogeochemical modelling showed that temporary increases in marine phosphorus could increase oxygenic productivity and organic-carbon burial enough to generate larger atmospheric O₂ pulses.

The study argues that nutrient availability was probably an important contributor to rapid oxygen fluctuations during the later GOE.

That does not prove phosphorus caused the entire event.

A model in the same study can produce oxygenation through declining reductant flux even without an extra phosphorus pulse.

The resulting oxygen trajectory is different.

This is useful because it turns competing hypotheses into testable geochemical predictions.

The central point survives every model:

**oxygenic photosynthesis does not oxygenate a planet merely by existing.**

Some reduced products have to escape re-oxidation.

Organic burial is one major escape route.

Pyrite burial provides another.

Hydrogen escape to space can oxidise the planet on very long timescales.

And the size of the oxygen sinks can change independently of biology.

The GOE therefore cannot be understood only from the oxygen source.

We have to ask why the planet's reducing capacity changed.

That question moves below the ocean and atmosphere.

Into mantle gases.

Continental growth.

Weathering.

Tectonics.

The biological revolution was waiting for geology to stop cancelling it.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Net long-term O₂ accumulation requires burial of reduced products such as organic carbon and/or reduced sulfur, or another process that leaves oxidising power behind.
- Dodd et al. (2025), *Nature Communications*: marine phosphate availability covaried with carbon-isotope records across the GOE; modelling supports nutrient-driven productivity/organic-burial pulses as a contributor to O₂ fluctuations.
- Lomagundi carbon-isotope excursion is commonly linked to enhanced organic-carbon burial but competing interpretations remain.
- Declining reductant-flux models can also generate oxygenation.

**Decision:** organic-carbon burial remains a central redox mechanism, not a uniquely established single trigger for the GOE.
