# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 195 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN CREATES NEW ECOLOGY

Before persistent oxygen, redox gradients already organised microbial life.

After the GOE, one gradient becomes more powerful and widespread.

Oxygen above.

Reduced chemistry below.

The boundary between them becomes a biological frontier.

At the oxygenated side, aerobic organisms gain access to a high-energy electron acceptor.

At the anoxic side, methanogens, sulfate reducers, fermenters and iron reducers continue using older metabolic pathways.

Between them live organisms adapted to changing oxygen.

Some tolerate brief exposure.

Some use tiny concentrations.

Some switch metabolism depending on conditions.

Some consume oxygen so efficiently that they help maintain anoxic zones beneath them.

This creates **microoxic** habitats.

Places with oxygen present, but at concentrations far below modern air-saturated water.

Such environments remain common today in sediments, microbial mats and oxygen-minimum zones.

They show how a transition can generate niches rather than merely replace one biosphere with another.

Oxygen also changes competition.

Methanogens can lose substrates to organisms using energetically stronger electron acceptors.

Sulfate reducers gain more sulfate after oxidative weathering but can be excluded from oxygenated layers.

Iron reducers shift deeper as ferric minerals are generated near oxic boundaries.

Sulfur oxidisers thrive where sulfide meets oxygen.

New metabolic partnerships emerge around redox fronts.

The oxygen revolution therefore increases ecological **partitioning**.

A centimetre of sediment can contain oxic, nitrate-reducing, metal-reducing, sulfate-reducing and methanogenic zones stacked by energy yield and reactant availability.

Modern sequences are not literal copies of Paleoproterozoic sediment chemistry.

But the thermodynamic principle is ancient.

Microbes consume the most energetically favourable available electron acceptors first.

As each one is depleted, a different metabolism dominates.

Persistent O₂ adds a new high-energy layer to the top of that hierarchy.

This matters for carbon burial.

Aerobic respiration can degrade organic matter efficiently.

More oxygen can therefore reduce the fraction of organic carbon that survives burial, creating a negative feedback on oxygen accumulation.

At the same time, oxygen promotes weathering that delivers nutrients and sulfate, potentially increasing productivity or reduced-sulfur burial.

Once again, the sign of the feedback depends on context.

This ecological restructuring is difficult to see directly in fossils.

Microbial cells often look similar regardless of metabolism.

The same filament shape can belong to organisms with very different electron-transfer pathways.

Genes do not fossilise.

So geologists infer metabolic geography from minerals, isotopes and sedimentary chemistry.

Where oxygen penetrates.

Where sulfide accumulates.

Where iron remains reduced.

Where organic carbon is preserved.

The changing pattern is the fossil of ecology.

The GOE therefore should not be imagined as an extinction boundary drawn through a microbial family tree.

It is better understood as an expansion of redox space.

Some habitats disappear.

Others grow.

New ones form at their boundaries.

The biosphere becomes chemically more structured.

One environmental effect reaches beyond water and sediment.

Oxygen in the atmosphere absorbs ultraviolet light indirectly by making ozone.

That changes where life can survive near the surface.

And unlike a microbial mat or oxygen oasis, an ozone layer can act on a planetary scale.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Persistent O₂ creates oxic–anoxic and microoxic ecological gradients and reorganises anaerobic metabolisms without eliminating them.
- Aerobic remineralisation can reduce organic-carbon burial, while oxidative weathering and sulfur/nutrient feedbacks can work in other directions.
- Ancient metabolic zonation is inferred geochemically; modern sediment/mat redox sequences are functional analogues, not literal Paleoproterozoic taxonomic reconstructions.

**Decision:** the biological effect of the GOE is presented as ecological repartitioning and niche expansion/contraction, not a simple universal anaerobe extinction.
