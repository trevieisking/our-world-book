# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 191 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### LIFE CREATES NEW MINERALS

A mineral species exists only if chemistry allows its elements to combine in a stable structure.

Change the atmosphere and new structures become possible.

On the early Earth, most near-surface environments were reducing.

Many elements remained in low oxidation states.

Some oxidised minerals either did not form or were rare and local.

Then biology began releasing O₂.

Oxidation opened new chemical pathways.

Iron formed ferric oxides and oxyhydroxides.

Copper accessed higher oxidation states.

Uranium became mobile as U⁶⁺ before being redeposited in new minerals.

Manganese could form high-valence oxides.

Molybdenum and chromium entered new oxidised mineral chemistries.

The mineral kingdom diversified.

Robert Hazen and colleagues developed the idea of **mineral evolution** to describe this historical growth in mineral diversity.

The central insight is that Earth's mineral inventory is not fixed.

Planets create new minerals as they differentiate, circulate water, build continents and change atmosphere.

Life becomes one of those mineral-making processes.

The Great Oxidation Event is therefore not merely recorded by minerals.

It helps generate new ones.

Estimates vary depending on how mineral species and formation modes are counted, but a large fraction of Earth's present mineral diversity is linked directly or indirectly to biologically produced oxygen and the oxidising environments it created.

This gives life a geological signature unlike almost anything before it.

A metabolism inside microscopic cells changes the list of stable minerals across a planet.

The process also runs in reverse.

Minerals affect oxygenation.

Iron oxides adsorb phosphorus.

Sulfides remove trace metals.

Clay minerals protect organic matter.

Weathering minerals influence carbon burial.

A 2023 study proposed that evolving mineral assemblages themselves could change the stability of the oxygen cycle by altering organic-matter preservation and nutrient feedbacks.

That is an intriguing systems hypothesis.

It is not yet established as a principal GOE trigger.

But the reciprocal relationship is real.

Atmosphere changes minerals.

Minerals change biogeochemical cycles.

Those cycles affect atmospheric oxygen.

A 2024 global analysis of redox-sensitive minerals found that high-valence mineral assemblages containing manganese, cerium, molybdenum and chromium changed strongly across Earth's oxygenation history.

The sequence of mineral responses reflects the different chemical potentials required to oxidise each element.

More recent 2026 work continues using large mineral databases and statistical models to reconstruct oxygen evolution.

These methods are promising.

They also depend on preservation and sampling.

The known mineral record is biased toward rocks that survived erosion, metamorphism and tectonic recycling.

Human mineral discovery is uneven geographically and economically.

A database signal is not a direct atmospheric measurement.

Still, the broad qualitative conclusion is exceptionally strong.

An oxygen-poor Earth cannot host the same near-surface mineral diversity as an oxygen-rich Earth.

Life changed Earth's mineralogy by changing atmospheric chemistry.

That means the biosphere has become part of geology in a deeper sense than building stromatolites.

Microbes are now creating entire classes of geochemical environments.

Oxidised soils.

Oxidised sediments.

New evaporite chemistry.

New trace-metal cycles.

New ore-forming pathways.

The rocks after the GOE are not merely younger.

They are chemically different because life altered the planet that made them.

That raises an evolutionary question.

If oxygen has become persistent enough to restructure minerals and nutrients, what does it do to life itself?

For many organisms, the first answer is brutal.

Oxygen is toxic.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Mineral-evolution literature documents major diversification of oxidised near-surface mineral species after biologically driven oxygenation.
- Redox-sensitive Mn, Ce, Mo, Cr, Fe, Cu and U mineral assemblages respond differently to rising O₂.
- Hazen mineral-evolution framework and 2024 redox-sensitive mineral database studies support the qualitative expansion.
- Proposed feedback from mineral evolution to oxygen-cycle stability remains a hypothesis rather than established GOE cause.

**Decision:** oxygen-driven mineral diversification is presented as a strong Earth-system consequence; database-derived quantitative reconstructions retain preservation/sampling caveats.
