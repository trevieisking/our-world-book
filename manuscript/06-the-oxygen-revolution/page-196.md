# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 196 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### BUILDING AN OZONE SHIELD

Oxygen changes sunlight before sunlight reaches the ground.

High in the atmosphere, ultraviolet photons split some O₂ molecules into individual oxygen atoms.

Those atoms can react with O₂ to form **ozone**, O₃.

Ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation strongly.

A sufficiently developed ozone layer therefore shields the surface from wavelengths capable of damaging DNA and breaking organic molecules.

Before oxygenic photosynthesis, early Earth had no modern ozone shield.

Surface organisms faced a harsher ultraviolet environment.

Water provided protection.

Minerals provided shade.

Pigments and repair systems provided biological defence.

Chapter Four showed that ultraviolet radiation could also power useful prebiotic chemistry.

The same energy source can build molecules before life and damage genomes after life evolves.

As atmospheric O₂ rises, the ultraviolet environment changes.

The relationship is nonlinear.

Very small amounts of oxygen can produce some ozone.

More oxygen thickens the shield.

Atmospheric structure, methane and other gases affect the result.

The GOE therefore begins reducing surface UV before O₂ reaches anything like modern abundance.

This has several consequences.

Terrestrial and shallow-water organisms receive less damaging radiation.

That can expand habitable surface area.

Organic molecules survive longer.

Photochemistry changes.

Methane destruction pathways change.

The atmosphere itself becomes a chemical filter shaped by biology.

Ozone also participates in the sulfur story.

The ultraviolet wavelengths that generate atmospheric sulfur MIF are increasingly screened as oxygen and ozone rise.

The loss of the Archean S-MIF signature therefore reflects not simply O₂ as a reactant but a reorganisation of atmospheric photochemistry.

This is one reason the sulfur record gives us such a powerful threshold.

The sky has changed how it processes sunlight.

We should not imagine a modern ozone layer appearing in one step.

GOE oxygen concentrations remain uncertain by orders of magnitude.

Different atmospheric models produce different ozone columns.

The exact surface UV dose during individual stages cannot be reconstructed as a single global number.

Clouds, haze and altitude also matter.

The robust sequence is simpler.

No persistent oxygen reservoir:

weak ozone shielding.

Persistent O₂ rises:

ozone becomes increasingly important.

Surface ultraviolet decreases.

This creates another biosphere–planet feedback.

Life produces oxygen.

Oxygen produces ozone.

Ozone protects life from ultraviolet damage.

That protection can allow more biological activity near the surface.

More surface productivity can influence oxygen production.

But shielding also removes ultraviolet photons from chemical pathways that once operated freely in an anoxic atmosphere.

A biosphere that changes its atmosphere changes the abiotic chemistry surrounding it.

The GOE therefore closes some chemical doors while opening biological ones.

And the oxygen story does not end with a smooth rise.

After the initial transition, carbon isotopes record one of the largest disturbances in Earth's entire carbon cycle.

For more than a hundred million years, many Paleoproterozoic carbonates became extraordinarily enriched in carbon-13.

The event is called Lomagundi–Jatuli.

It has often been interpreted as an oxygen overshoot.

That interpretation is plausible.

It is not as simple as textbooks sometimes make it.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Ozone forms photochemically from O₂ and absorbs damaging UV.
- Ozone shielding grows as O₂ increases but exact GOE ozone columns/surface UV depend on atmospheric models and other gases.
- O₂/O₃ photochemistry contributes to the disappearance of primary Archean S-MIF signals.
- No modern-strength ozone layer is assumed immediately at GOE onset.

**Decision:** ozone is presented as a progressive atmospheric consequence of persistent O₂, not a binary shield with a precise unsupported onset.
