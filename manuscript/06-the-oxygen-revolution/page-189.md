# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 189 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### AN OCEAN WITH LAYERS

An oxygenated atmosphere does not guarantee an oxygenated deep ocean.

The ocean mixes.

It does not mix instantly.

Oxygen enters at the surface from the atmosphere and from photosynthesis.

It is consumed by respiration, reduced metals and sulfur compounds as water sinks or moves into productive regions.

If consumption outpaces resupply, deeper water becomes anoxic.

That basic balance creates redox stratification.

After the Great Oxidation Event, shallow surface waters became increasingly oxygenated.

Below them, large volumes of the ocean remained reducing.

But “anoxic” is not one chemistry.

Two states matter especially.

**Ferruginous** water is anoxic and contains dissolved ferrous iron, Fe²⁺.

**Euxinic** water is anoxic and contains free hydrogen sulfide, H₂S.

Which state develops depends on the supply of iron, sulfate, organic matter and microbial activity.

If sulfate-reducing microbes make sulfide and enough dissolved iron is present, the sulfide reacts with iron and precipitates as iron sulfides.

The water can remain ferruginous.

If sulfide production exceeds the iron supply, free sulfide accumulates.

The water becomes euxinic.

This distinction changes everything from nutrient chemistry to fossil preservation.

For years, the Proterozoic ocean was sometimes described as broadly sulfidic.

Modern geochemical work has refined that picture.

Ferruginous conditions were extensive through much of anoxic deep water.

Euxinia occurred regionally, especially where sulfate supply and organic productivity favoured sulfide accumulation.

Some deeper waters may have been oxygenated at particular times and places.

The ocean became a patchwork.

A 2025 multi-proxy study of South African GOE-age shales captures that heterogeneity directly.

Vanadium, molybdenum, uranium, iron speciation and thallium isotopes indicate that shallow marine oxygenation became persistent while subsets of the same sedimentary succession still record ferruginous or euxinic conditions.

The surface and deep ocean were not moving through the transition in lockstep.

A 2026 review of stagewise oxygenation generalises the lesson.

Atmospheric oxygen rises tend to precede full oceanic responses.

On shorter timescales, atmosphere and ocean can even move in different redox directions.

This matters for biology.

An organism near the surface experiences a different planet from one living hundreds of metres below.

Oxygenic phototrophs occupy light-rich waters.

Aerobic heterotrophs exploit O₂ nearby.

Deeper organisms continue using sulfur, iron, methane and fermentation.

Redox boundaries become ecological boundaries.

Nutrients cross them.

Iron rises from anoxic water and is oxidised at the interface.

Sulfide rises and is consumed by sulfur oxidisers.

Organic matter sinks from the productive surface and feeds anaerobic metabolisms below.

The water column becomes vertically organised by metabolism.

This layered ocean also explains why oxygen-dependent evolution does not happen everywhere at once.

The atmosphere can contain persistent O₂ while enormous habitats remain hostile to aerobic life.

Complex cells evolving in this world would encounter oxygen as a geographically limited resource, not a universal environmental constant.

The Great Oxidation Event therefore creates a new planet without creating the modern ocean.

That distinction will matter for more than a billion years.

And the chemical layers do something else.

They sort trace metals.

Some metals become more soluble when oxidised.

Others are stripped from water by sulfide.

Nutrients can be released in one zone and trapped in another.

The redox map becomes a nutrient map.

Life has changed the ocean's chemistry.

Now the chemistry begins changing which metabolisms life can afford.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Post-GOE surface waters became more oxygenated while much of the deeper ocean remained anoxic.
- Ferruginous = anoxic, Fe²⁺-bearing; euxinic = anoxic, sulfide-bearing.
- 2025 GOE multi-proxy work records persistent shallow-water oxygenation alongside anoxic/euxinic or ferruginous intervals.
- 2026 stagewise-oxygenation review emphasises spatial/temporal heterogeneity and lag between atmospheric and oceanic oxygenation.

**Decision:** the post-GOE ocean is presented as redox-stratified and patchy, not uniformly oxygenated or uniformly euxinic.
