# CHAPTER SIX — THE OXYGEN REVOLUTION
## Page 199 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN DOES NOT EQUAL COMPLEXITY

Complex life needs energy.

Oxygen can provide access to a great deal of it.

That makes an attractive evolutionary equation:

more oxygen -> more energy -> more complex cells.

The equation is incomplete.

The first major rise of atmospheric oxygen happened hundreds of millions of years before the first unambiguous radiation of complex eukaryotic ecosystems.

The Lomagundi interval may have sustained relatively high surface oxygen for more than one hundred million years.

Yet the fossil record does not show an immediate explosion of large, oxygen-demanding organisms.

A 2023 synthesis of Paleoproterozoic oxygenation made this mismatch explicit.

Strong surface oxygenation during and after the GOE appears largely decoupled from obvious increases in biological complexity.

That does not mean oxygen is irrelevant.

It means **permissive conditions are not the same as evolutionary causes**.

A road can be open before anyone travels it.

Complex cells require more than respiration.

They require new cellular architecture.

Dynamic internal membranes.

A nucleus.

A cytoskeleton.

Trafficking systems.

Chromosomes handled in new ways.

Sexual or sex-like recombination.

And, most importantly for almost all living eukaryotes, mitochondria descended from an internalised bacterial partner.

The origin of that cellular organisation is called **eukaryogenesis**.

Its timing is uncertain.

A 2024 review places the broader transition somewhere within approximately **2.7 to 1.8 billion years ago**, with the branch between archaeal ancestors and the last common ancestor of living eukaryotes representing a long interval whose intermediate stages are mostly lost.

Molecular clocks differ.

Fossil interpretations differ.

Different eukaryotic features need not have appeared simultaneously.

The relationship to oxygen is equally complicated.

A major 2022 review compared eukaryotic timing with Earth's oxygen history and found no simple temporal match between eukaryogenesis or mitochondrial origin and either the GOE or later oxygenation events.

Modern work on Asgard archaea and eukaryotic energy metabolism has strengthened models in which an archaeal host and bacterial partner could have interacted under low-oxygen or even locally anoxic conditions.

The earliest stages of complex-cell evolution therefore may not have required modern atmospheric oxygen.

Later eukaryotic diversification certainly benefited from oxygen in many environments.

Mitochondrial respiration gave eukaryotic cells access to powerful bioenergetics.

Sterol biosynthesis and other pathways can require oxygen.

Large active multicellular organisms eventually impose much higher oxygen demands.

But the first eukaryotic cell is a different problem from the first animal.

This distinction protects the history from hindsight.

We know complex aerobic life exists today.

That does not mean every earlier oxygen rise was waiting to produce it.

Evolution depends on contingency.

A symbiosis has to occur.

Partners have to remain together.

Genes have to transfer.

Cellular conflicts have to be resolved.

New molecular systems have to evolve.

Environmental opportunity matters.

Historical accidents matter too.

The GOE therefore belongs in the history of complexity as a **change in possibility**, not a guaranteed cause.

It created persistent oxygenated niches.

It changed nutrient and metal availability.

It altered UV radiation.

It expanded high-energy respiration.

It established new redox boundaries.

Those conditions form part of the world in which eukaryotes later emerged.

But to understand how a microbial cell became something fundamentally different, we have to stop looking only at the atmosphere.

We have to look inside the cell.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Hodgskiss et al. (2023 review): no robust synchrony between Paleoproterozoic high oxygen and immediate rise of high-O₂-demand biological complexity.
- Mills et al. (2022), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: eukaryogenesis and mitochondrial origin are not simply temporally coupled to major planetary oxygenation transitions.
- 2024 *Nature* review: eukaryogenesis occurred broadly ~2.7–1.8 Ga, with timing and intermediate stages poorly constrained.
- Oxygen later matters strongly for eukaryotic ecology and some biosyntheses, but oxygen alone is not treated as the cause of eukaryogenesis.

**Decision:** oxygen is framed as an environmental enabler/constraint, not a sufficient cause of complex-cell origin.
