## PAGE 006 — AUTHOR'S NOTE: WHY THIS BOOK EXISTS

We know an astonishing amount about our world.

We can estimate the age of Earth, reconstruct vanished oceans, recognise the remains of animals that disappeared hundreds of millions of years before any human being existed, follow the movement of continents, read traces of ancient atmospheres from rocks, and recover parts of our own ancestry from fossil bones and DNA.

Yet there are also enormous gaps.

We do not know exactly how the first living system emerged from non-living chemistry. We do not possess a complete record of every species that ever lived. We do not know every migration, every forgotten culture or every event behind the oldest stories. Many questions about consciousness, the earliest moments of the Universe and life beyond Earth remain open.

That combination is what made this book worth writing.

*Our World* is not an attempt to pretend that every mystery has been solved. It is an attempt to place what we **do** know beside what we **do not** know, and to show why the difference matters.

Where evidence is strong, the book will say so.

Where evidence permits more than one serious interpretation, the disagreement will stay on the page.

Where the evidence runs out, we will not replace it with certainty.

The story is remarkable enough without doing that.

## PAGE 007 — WHAT THIS BOOK IS

This is a history of our world told through the evidence that survived it.

No human being watched Earth form. Nobody photographed the first ocean. There was no historian standing beside the earliest life, no witness taking notes when vertebrates first walked onto land, and no camera recording the lives of our earliest ancestors.

So how can we know anything about them?

Because the past leaves evidence.

Atoms can preserve radioactive clocks. Minerals can preserve ancient chemistry. Sedimentary rocks can preserve vanished environments. Fossils can preserve bodies, footprints and behaviour. Isotopes can record changes in oceans and atmospheres. Genomes can preserve ancestry and interbreeding. Artefacts can preserve technology. Buildings and settlements can preserve social organisation. Written records can preserve names, laws, beliefs and events.

None of these witnesses is perfect.

A fossil does not tell us everything about the animal that made it. A manuscript may faithfully record what somebody believed without proving that every event described in it occurred exactly as written. A computer model can show that an event is physically possible without showing that it actually happened.

For that reason, this book separates **observation**, **inference**, **uncertainty** and **speculation**.

The goal is not to produce the final history of existence.

The goal is to construct the strongest history that the surviving evidence presently allows.

## PAGE 008 — THE EVIDENCE KEY

Important claims throughout *Our World* use a common evidence language.

### ESTABLISHED
Several independent lines of evidence converge. Serious disagreement concerns details rather than whether the central event or process occurred.

### STRONGLY SUPPORTED
The evidence strongly favours an explanation, although important inferential steps or unresolved details remain.

### PROBABLE
The evidence favours the claim, but credible alternatives remain possible.

### UNCERTAIN
The available evidence does not yet distinguish securely between serious competing explanations.

### SPECULATIVE
The idea can be discussed coherently, but there is not enough reliable evidence to say that it probably happened.

### UNSUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED
Reliable support has not been established, or the claim conflicts with stronger available evidence.

These labels apply to **claims**, not to people, cultures or religions.

A single subject can contain several different levels of evidence at once. We may know that an ancient city existed, remain uncertain about the date of one event associated with it, and have no historical method capable of proving a supernatural explanation attached to that event.

That distinction will matter throughout this book.

## PAGE 009 — HOW TO READ UNCERTAINTY

There is a temptation to treat uncertainty as weakness.

It is usually the opposite.

A reliable history must be able to distinguish between a conclusion that is secure, one that is merely likely, and one that is still being argued over.

Suppose two laboratories date the same geological event using different methods and arrive at compatible results. Confidence increases because the conclusion does not depend entirely on one measurement.

Now suppose a fossil is securely dated but researchers disagree about which species it belongs to. The age can be well established while the biological interpretation remains uncertain.

Or consider an ancient written account. Historians may be highly confident that the document existed and influenced a community while remaining unable to demonstrate that every event described inside it happened literally.

The correct answer is not always **yes** or **no**.

Sometimes it is:

**We know this part.**

**We probably know this part.**

**This part remains disputed.**

**This part cannot currently be demonstrated.**

Those are not evasions. They are different conclusions.

Throughout this book, uncertainty is therefore preserved rather than edited away.

## PAGE 010 — HOW TO READ THE PICTURES

Many events in this book happened before photography, before art, before humans and, in some chapters, before animals with eyes existed at all.

The illustrations therefore have to obey the same rules as the text.

### Reconstruction
A scene built from evidence: geology, fossils, anatomy, climate indicators, chemical models or archaeological remains. The overall scene can be well supported even when some visual details remain unknown.

### Scientific diagram
An explanatory picture showing a process or relationship. Diagrams may simplify scale, distance or geometry to make the science understandable. Any important simplification should be made clear.

### Map or timeline
An original visualisation based on cited geographical or chronological evidence.

### Artist's impression
Used where substantial appearance details remain uncertain. It is an informed visual possibility, not a claim that the scene looked exactly that way.

A dramatic image will never be used as evidence merely because it looks convincing.

If we do not know an animal's exact colour, the illustration should not pretend that we do. If a prehistoric coastline cannot be reconstructed precisely, the image should not imply metre-by-metre certainty. If an ancient event has several credible interpretations, the artwork should not silently choose one and turn it into fact.

The pictures are here to help us understand the evidence, not replace it.

## PAGE 014 — THE DEEP-TIME KEY

The numbers in this book become difficult to imagine very quickly.

A human lifetime is measured in decades.

Recorded history occupies thousands of years.

Our species reaches back hundreds of thousands of years.

Many familiar animal groups extend back tens or hundreds of millions of years.

Earth itself extends back billions.

For compact scientific dates, the book may occasionally use:

- **ka** — thousand years ago
- **Ma** — million years ago
- **Ga** — billion years ago

These abbreviations are useful, but they can hide the scale of what they mean.

One million seconds is a little under twelve days.

One billion seconds is more than thirty-one years.

The difference between a million years and a billion years is just as enormous.

Whenever possible, the narrative therefore uses ordinary language — *about 66 million years ago*, *roughly 3.5 billion years ago* — and reserves specialised notation for diagrams, tables and places where precision helps rather than distracts.

## PAGE 015 — A NOTE ON "WE DO NOT KNOW"

There will be places in this book where the most accurate answer is:

# WE DO NOT KNOW.

That sentence is important enough to deserve its own page.

It does not mean that every explanation is equally likely.

It does not mean that evidence has failed.

And it does not create a blank space into which any preferred answer can automatically be inserted.

Sometimes we know the boundaries of a mystery extremely well.

We can know that life existed by a certain period while remaining uncertain about exactly when the first living system appeared.

We can demonstrate that two populations interbred while still arguing about how often they met.

We can establish that an ancient community transmitted a miracle story while lacking a method capable of independently demonstrating its supernatural cause.

Knowledge has edges.

Finding those edges is part of discovering the world.

So when this book says **we do not know**, the investigation has not ended.

It has reached the frontier.

## PAGE 016 — TRANSITION: DOWN

To begin the history of our world, we are going in the wrong direction.

Not upward into space.

Not backward through a telescope.

Down.

Past the bright surface of the ocean.

Past the depth where sunlight begins to fail.

Past the familiar shapes of the shallow sea.

Down through kilometres of dark water, toward a landscape no human being could reach unaided.

At the bottom, the planet is still moving.

Rock is being created.

Old crust is travelling toward destruction.

Hot, mineral-rich water escapes from fractures in the seafloor.

And around some of those fractures, life survives without sunlight.

It is a strange place to begin a history of Earth.

That is exactly why we are beginning there.
