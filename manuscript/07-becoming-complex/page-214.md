# CHAPTER SEVEN — BECOMING COMPLEX
## Page 214 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### SEX IS NOT REPRODUCTION

Life reproduced for billions of years before eukaryotes existed.

A bacterium divides.

An archaeon divides.

A cell copies its genome and produces descendants.

That is reproduction.

**Sex** is something different.

Sex combines genetic material from different cells or nuclei.

**Meiosis** then reshuffles and reduces chromosome sets through a specialised division process.

The modern details vary enormously.

Animals.

Plants.

Fungi.

Single-celled eukaryotes.

They do not all mate in the same way.

Some spend most of life haploid.

Some are mostly diploid.

Some fuse equal-sized cells.

Some produce eggs and sperm.

Some can reproduce clonally for many generations and enter sex only under stress.

But beneath that diversity lies a shared molecular core.

Homologous chromosomes recognise one another.

DNA breaks and recombination are controlled.

Chromosome pairs exchange segments.

A diploid or polyploid state is reduced.

New combinations of alleles emerge.

Genes dedicated to meiosis occur across the deepest eukaryotic lineages.

So do homologues of recombination and chromosome-pairing machinery.

The simplest evolutionary interpretation is that **LECA was sexual**.

This does not mean LECA had males and females.

Those are later specialisations.

It does not mean every reproductive cycle was obligately sexual.

LECA may have reproduced clonally most of the time and entered meiosis only under particular conditions.

What it means is that the capacity for cell fusion, recombination and ploidy reduction had already evolved before the major living eukaryotic groups diverged.

Sex therefore belongs among the defining innovations of early eukaryotic complexity.

Its components did not arise from nothing.

Homologous recombination is far older than eukaryotes.

Bacteria and archaea repair broken DNA using related proteins.

Cells already exchange genes horizontally.

DNA pairing and repair machinery already exists.

Meiosis repurposes ancient repair chemistry into a regulated chromosome-scale process.

This is another example of evolutionary assembly.

Take old machinery.

Duplicate genes.

Change timing.

Coordinate it with chromosome segregation.

Add cell fusion.

Build a new life cycle.

The order remains uncertain.

Did cell fusion come first?

Did genome doubling produce pressure for a reduction division?

Did stress-induced DNA repair provide the first meiotic recombination programme?

Did mitochondrial endosymbiosis increase genome instability enough to favour enhanced recombination?

Several models exist.

No fossil records the first meiosis.

Yet the comparative evidence for sexual LECA is much stronger than the evidence for the pathway that produced it.

This distinction matters because sex and multicellularity are often confused historically.

Sex does not require a multicellular body.

Most of its evolutionary history probably occurred among single-celled organisms.

A unicellular eukaryote can fuse with another cell.

Recombine chromosomes.

Undergo meiosis.

Return to clonal growth.

The fundamental innovation is genetic, not anatomical.

Sex creates descendants whose genomes are not simple copies of one parent.

It allows mutations from different lineages to meet.

It can separate harmful mutations from beneficial ones.

It can repair damaged DNA through homologous pairing.

It can also break successful gene combinations.

Sex is costly.

That cost makes its near-universality across eukaryotes one of evolution's most interesting problems.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Comparative genomics and distribution of meiosis-specific genes strongly support a sexual LECA.
- Eukaryotic sex is distinguished from reproduction; male/female anisogamy is not projected onto LECA.
- Meiotic machinery repurposes ancient homologous recombination/DNA-repair systems.
- The sequence among cell fusion, ploidy cycling and meiosis remains uncertain.

**Decision:** LECA sexual capacity is treated as strongly supported; exact original life cycle and selective driver remain unresolved.
