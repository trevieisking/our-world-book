# CHAPTER SEVEN — BECOMING COMPLEX
## Page 208 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### OXYGEN DID NOT BUILD THE EUKARYOTIC CELL

The timing invites a seductive explanation.

Earth oxygenates.

Eukaryotes appear.

Therefore oxygen caused eukaryotes.

Chapter Six showed why that is too simple.

The first persistent atmospheric oxygen appears during the Great Oxidation Event.

The broad molecular and fossil window for eukaryogenesis overlaps the long aftermath.

But overlap is not mechanism.

A 2022 synthesis compared eukaryotic evolution directly with Earth's redox history.

Its conclusion was striking.

Neither the origin of mitochondria nor eukaryogenesis as a whole maps cleanly onto the major planetary oxygenation transitions.

Large regions of the ocean remained anoxic throughout the interval when mitochondria-bearing eukaryotes emerged.

Living eukaryotes also preserve anaerobic mitochondrial metabolisms.

Asgard archaea include lineages adapted to anoxic or low-oxygen habitats.

Several symbiosis models depend on hydrogen transfer rather than oxygen respiration.

The earliest partnership could therefore have formed without an oxygen-rich world.

This does not make the GOE irrelevant.

Persistent surface oxygen changes the environmental menu.

Aerobic respiration becomes available in more habitats.

Sterol biosynthesis and other oxygen-dependent reactions become possible where O₂ is present.

Ozone reduces ultraviolet stress.

Trace-metal availability changes.

Oxic–anoxic boundaries create energetic gradients.

The probability of certain evolutionary routes can change.

But an atmosphere cannot invent a nucleus.

Oxygen cannot by itself evolve a cytoskeleton.

It cannot perform endosymbiosis.

It cannot solve conflicts between two genomes.

Those require biological evolution.

The energetic role of mitochondria is debated in a similar way.

One influential argument proposes that mitochondria removed a major bioenergetic constraint on genome expansion.

Bacteria and archaea produce ATP mainly across their cell membrane.

A larger genome and more internal cellular machinery might therefore become energetically costly.

Mitochondria provide many internal bioenergetic membranes, potentially allowing more energy per host gene.

Under this model, mitochondrial endosymbiosis helped unlock the extraordinary genomic and cellular complexity of eukaryotes.

Other researchers challenge how unique that energetic barrier really is.

Some bacteria reach enormous cell sizes.

Some maintain intracellular membranes.

The cost of individual genes may not scale in the required way.

And the 2026 Asgard ancestry analysis favours substantial host complexity before mitochondrial capture.

The important distinction is between:

**mitochondria being essential to the ancestry of all living eukaryotes**

and

**mitochondria being the sole cause of eukaryotic complexity**.

The first is strongly supported.

The second remains debated.

The same distinction applies to oxygen.

Persistent O₂ became part of the environment in which eukaryotes evolved.

It is not a complete explanation for why eukaryotic cells exist.

So where do we go next?

Inside the architecture.

A cell with mitochondria is not yet enough to explain a nucleus.

A cell with Asgard actin homologues is not yet enough to explain a dynamic eukaryotic cytoskeleton.

A few membrane-remodelling proteins are not yet an endoplasmic reticulum or Golgi apparatus.

The next block follows how familiar eukaryotic systems could have been assembled from older molecular components.

And then we reach another evolutionary invention almost as consequential as endosymbiosis.

Sex.

Not reproduction itself.

Life had reproduced for billions of years.

Sex changes how heredity is shuffled.

It gives complex genomes a new way to evolve.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Mills et al. (2022), *Nature Ecology & Evolution*: eukaryogenesis/mitochondrial origin are not temporally or metabolically locked to a planetary oxygenation event; anoxic syntrophic scenarios remain viable.
- Mitochondrial energetics as an enabler of genomic complexity is an influential but debated hypothesis.
- 2024–2026 Asgard work strengthens evidence for substantial archaeal host complexity before LECA and, in some analyses, before mitochondrial acquisition.
- Fossil and molecular timing remain broad; no exact FECA or mitochondrial-acquisition date is asserted.

**Decision:** oxygen and mitochondrial bioenergetics are treated as important constraints/enablers, not single-cause explanations for eukaryogenesis.

**Transition:** pages 209–216 will examine nucleus/endomembranes, cytoskeleton, phagocytosis, chromosome handling, introns, and the origin/evolutionary consequences of sex.
