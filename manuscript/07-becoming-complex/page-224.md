# CHAPTER SEVEN — BECOMING COMPLEX
## Page 224 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### BEFORE THE CAMBRIAN EXPLOSION

We began this chapter inside a microbial cell.

We end on a seafloor populated by macroscopic organisms.

The distance between those worlds is enormous.

And no single cause bridges it.

Eukaryogenesis joined an archaeal lineage with a bacterial endosymbiont.

Mitochondria became permanent.

Genes moved between genomes.

The cell acquired a nucleus, endomembranes, cytoskeletal machinery, complex chromosome control and sexual reproduction.

By LECA, the major architecture of the modern eukaryotic cell was already assembled.

For hundreds of millions of years, much of eukaryotic life remained microscopic.

The fossil record records benthic eukaryotes in oxygenated habitats by at least the late Palaeoproterozoic and Mesoproterozoic.

Molecular fossils reveal a hidden protosterol-producing biota.

Multicellularity evolved independently again and again.

Some lineages created differentiated bodies more than a billion years ago.

Then the Cryogenian climate reorganised the planet.

The Sturtian glaciation lasted roughly fifty-eight million years.

The Marinoan freeze followed later.

Eukaryotes survived.

Ice did not erase complex life.

Nor does current evidence justify saying that glaciation directly created animals.

Postglacial oceans could remain anoxic and nutrient-limited.

Climate, weathering, nutrient delivery, oxygenation and evolution interacted in ways we are still reconstructing.

After the Marinoan deglaciation, the Ediacaran world emerged.

By about 574 million years ago, seafloors preserved widespread macroscopic communities.

Rangeomorphs used developmental programmes unlike anything visible in older fossils.

Later assemblages include stronger evidence for mobile animals.

Organisms graze microbial surfaces.

Trace fossils record sediment disturbance.

Body plans diversify.

Predators may begin appearing in limited forms.

The ancient microbial mat world is no longer physically undisturbed.

But even at the end of the Ediacaran, the modern marine ecosystem does not yet exist.

Most seafloors are still only weakly churned compared with later periods.

Mineralised skeletons are uncommon.

Predation has not yet driven the widespread armour, spines and burrowing seen later.

Many Ediacaran organisms are sessile.

Some rely on microbial mats that will become less dominant once sediment-disturbing animals spread.

Ecological escalation is beginning.

It has not peaked.

This distinction is why the **Cambrian Explosion** should not be introduced as the “start of animals.”

Animals are older.

Nor is it one instantaneous evolutionary explosion.

The major animal lineages have deeper roots.

Molecular clocks commonly place divergences before their first obvious body fossils.

Soft-bodied organisms preserve poorly.

Small early animals leave weak traces.

The Cambrian fossil record is also improved by widespread mineralised hard parts and exceptional fossil deposits.

Part of the apparent explosion is therefore a change in visibility.

But only part.

Something genuinely changes across the Ediacaran–Cambrian transition.

Animals become more abundant.

More mobile.

More deeply burrowing.

More heavily armoured.

Predation intensifies.

Eyes become ecologically important.

Food webs acquire more trophic levels.

Sediment is mixed.

Reefs change.

Skeletons appear repeatedly in unrelated groups.

Ecological engineering accelerates.

These interactions create evolutionary feedback.

A predator favours armour.

Armour favours stronger feeding structures.

Burrowing creates new habitats and destroys old microbial surfaces.

Eyes improve detection.

Better detection favours camouflage, speed and defence.

Ecology begins driving morphology faster and more visibly.

The Cambrian radiation is therefore not the moment complexity appears.

It is the moment **complex animal ecology becomes impossible to miss**.

The threshold is recorded around **538.8 million years ago**, the current base of the Cambrian Period.

On one side lies the Ediacaran world of microbial mats, enigmatic soft-bodied communities and early animals.

On the other lies an accelerating contest among moving, feeding, sensing, burrowing and increasingly mineralised organisms.

The next chapter follows that contest.

Not as a miracle of sudden creation.

Not as a single mutation.

Not as a fossil illusion.

But as an evolutionary radiation whose causes include developmental innovation, environmental change, preservation and — increasingly — organisms changing the selective pressures on one another.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Animals predate the Cambrian; widespread Ediacaran animal communities are established by ~574 Ma in current interpretations.
- Current Ediacaran–Cambrian boundary is ~538.8 Ma.
- Cambrian diversification combines genuine biological/ecological radiation with preservational and biomineralisation effects.
- No single trigger — oxygen, Snowball Earth, developmental genes, predation or preservation — is treated as a complete explanation.
- Chapter 8 scope agrees with the master plan: Cambrian diversification, ecological escalation, eyes, armour, predation, burrowing and complex food webs.

**Decision:** Chapter 7 closes with the Cambrian radiation framed as an ecological/evolutionary intensification built on Ediacaran ancestry, not the first appearance of animals.
