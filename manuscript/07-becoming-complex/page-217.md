### WHEN EUKARYOTES ENTER THE FOSSIL RECORD

Genes can reconstruct an ancestor we never see.

Fossils tell us when recognisable cells had definitely appeared.

The two records do not line up perfectly.

That is expected.

The first eukaryotes were microscopic.

Their internal membranes rarely fossilised.

A nucleus usually leaves no direct mineral imprint.

Mitochondria are far too small to identify in ordinary Precambrian fossils.

So palaeontologists need indirect cellular clues.

Size helps.

It is not enough.

Some bacteria are enormous.

A complex wall ornament helps.

It is not enough.

Minerals and prokaryotes can make elaborate shapes.

The strongest eukaryotic microfossils combine several features:

large cell size,

complex wall architecture,

processes or protrusions,

evidence of cytoskeletal control,

repeated populations,

life-cycle structures,

and secure sedimentary context.

Widely accepted fossil eukaryotes occur by roughly **1.75 to 1.4 billion years ago**.

Forms such as *Tappania*, *Valeria*, *Dictyosphaera* and *Shuiyousphaeridium* possess sizes and wall morphologies difficult to explain with typical prokaryotic cells.

Some fossils show processes extending from the cell wall in patterns interpreted as requiring a dynamic cytoskeleton.

A 2026 *Nature* study added something new.

Instead of asking only what these organisms looked like, Maxwell Lechte and colleagues asked **where they lived**.

They combined fossil distributions with sedimentology and geochemical redox indicators from rocks containing the oldest known eukaryotic assemblages.

The fossils occurred across coastal and offshore settings.

But they were overwhelmingly associated with sediments deposited beneath **oxygenated bottom waters**.

Comparable fossil-rich anoxic samples contained few or none of the same eukaryotes.

The authors therefore inferred that these early fossil eukaryotes were likely aerobic, facultatively aerobic or microaerophilic.

They also argued that the organisms were mainly **benthic**.

If they had been abundant plankton drifting through the water column, their remains should have appeared more equally in oxic and anoxic sediments.

Instead, they seem to have lived on or near oxygenated seafloors.

This is a major ecological insight.

The first fossil eukaryotes do not simply appear after atmospheric oxygen rises.

They occupy particular oxygenated habitats inside a still largely redox-stratified ocean.

That fits the world described at the end of Chapter Six.

Atmospheric O₂ exists.

The deep ocean is often anoxic.

Shallow seafloor oases provide the right chemistry.

Eukaryotic ecology is therefore restricted by **where oxygen reaches**, not by one global atmospheric number.

The study also helps explain a long puzzle.

Molecular clocks suggest eukaryotic history began earlier than the richest eukaryotic fossil and biomarker records.

If early eukaryotes lived mainly as benthic organisms in limited oxic habitats, they could exist for hundreds of millions of years without dominating the plankton or leaving abundant global biomarkers.

Their ecological expansion may have lagged behind their cellular origin.

Once again:

origin is not expansion.

A lineage can exist long before it becomes abundant.

The oldest fossils therefore give us a minimum.

By about 1.75–1.4 billion years ago, recognisably eukaryotic cells inhabited oxygenated benthic ecosystems.

The lineage itself must be older.

Another record reaches into that missing history.

Not cell walls.

Molecules.

Steroids preserved in ancient rocks.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Lechte et al. (2026), *Nature*: oldest known fossil eukaryotes (~1.75–1.4 Ga) are strongly associated with oxygenated bottom-water settings and interpreted as mainly benthic aerobes/facultative aerobes/microaerophiles.
- Widely accepted early eukaryotic taxa include *Tappania*, *Valeria*, *Dictyosphaera* and *Shuiyousphaeridium* in late Palaeoproterozoic–Mesoproterozoic rocks.
- Size alone is not diagnostic; context and morphological complexity matter.
- Fossil appearance is a minimum age, not the date of eukaryogenesis.

**Decision:** the 2026 habitat result is used to explain ecological restriction without equating oxygenated habitat preference with oxygen causing eukaryogenesis.
