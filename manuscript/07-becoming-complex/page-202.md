### THE FAMILY TREE BENDS INSIDE ARCHAEA

For much of the twentieth century, textbooks divided life mainly by appearance.

Cells with nuclei.

Cells without nuclei.

Then molecular biology changed the tree.

Carl Woese and colleagues compared ribosomal RNA sequences.

They found that organisms traditionally lumped together as “prokaryotes” actually contained two deeply distinct groups.

Bacteria.

Archaea.

Eukaryotes formed another major lineage.

For years, this was commonly drawn as **three domains** branching separately from an ancient common ancestor.

Genomic data later complicated the picture.

When researchers compared large sets of conserved genes using improved evolutionary models, many analyses placed eukaryotes not as a completely independent primary branch but **within the archaeal radiation**.

This produces what is often called a **two-domain tree**:

Bacteria on one side.

Archaea — including the lineage that gave rise to eukaryotes — on the other.

The exact placement is difficult.

Genes evolve at different rates.

Ancient branches are long.

Horizontal gene transfer moves sequences among microorganisms.

Genome sampling is extremely uneven.

Some methods recover different positions depending on which organisms or genes are included.

So one should not draw a single modern archaeal species at the base of the eukaryotic branch and call the problem solved.

The strongest broad conclusion is more stable than the exact twig.

**The host lineage of eukaryotes was archaeal.**

Then came the discovery that transformed the search for that host.

Environmental DNA recovered from deep marine sediments revealed previously unknown archaeal genomes containing genes that looked unexpectedly eukaryotic.

The first famous lineage was named **Lokiarchaeota**, after Loki's Castle, a hydrothermal field in the North Atlantic region where related environmental sequences had been studied.

Subsequent genomic surveys uncovered Thorarchaeota, Odinarchaeota, Heimdallarchaeia and many other related lineages.

Together they became the **Asgard archaea**.

The name is mythological.

The evolutionary significance is biological.

Asgard genomes contain homologues of proteins involved in processes once thought to be uniquely eukaryotic.

Actin-related cytoskeleton proteins.

ESCRT membrane-remodelling proteins.

Small GTPases.

Ubiquitin-related systems.

Proteins associated with membrane trafficking and cell-shape control.

This does not mean Asgard cells possess little nuclei, Golgi bodies or mitochondria.

They do not.

It means some of the molecular toolkit later elaborated into eukaryotic cellular machinery already had archaeal precursors.

Phylogenomic studies place eukaryotes particularly close to branches within the broader Asgard radiation.

Different studies disagree over the exact nearest lineage.

A 2023 *Nature* analysis supported a sister relationship with Hodarchaeales within Heimdallarchaeia.

Other analyses recover deeper placements.

A 2025 study argued for a eukaryotic origin outside Heimdallarchaeia but still within Asgard.

The 2026 review literature therefore treats Asgard's central role as compelling while leaving the exact ancestral branch unresolved.

This is an important distinction.

A scientific tree can be highly informative even when the final branching order is not fixed.

We know where to search.

Inside archaeal biology.

Among lineages with unexpectedly eukaryote-like molecular systems.

But genomes alone can make an organism seem more familiar than it really is.

To understand what Asgard archaea are like as cells, researchers had to do something far more difficult.

Grow one.

It took more than a decade.

The result did not look like a tiny eukaryote.

It looked stranger.

And it changed the kinds of symbiosis researchers could imagine.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Williams et al. (2020) and subsequent phylogenomics strongly support archaeal ancestry/two-domain models for eukaryotes.
- Asgard archaea were identified by environmental genomics and carry numerous eukaryotic-signature proteins.
- Eme et al. (2023), *Nature*, placed eukaryotes close to Hodarchaeales; other analyses differ.
- 2025–2026 studies/reviews retain Asgard centrality while exact eukaryote–Asgard branching remains unresolved.

**Decision:** distinguish strong archaeal/Asgard ancestry from the still-active problem of the exact sister lineage.
