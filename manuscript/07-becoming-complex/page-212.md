# CHAPTER SEVEN — BECOMING COMPLEX
## Page 212 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### DIVIDING A GENOME INTO DAUGHTERS

Bacteria can copy and segregate chromosomes with extraordinary reliability.

Eukaryotes face a different version of the problem.

Their nuclear genomes are usually divided among multiple **linear chromosomes**.

Each chromosome must be copied once.

The copies must remain identifiable.

Pairs of sister chromatids must attach to a segregation apparatus.

Each daughter cell must receive the correct set.

Failure can delete genes, duplicate chromosomes or kill the cell.

Modern eukaryotes solve this with **mitosis**.

Chromosomes condense.

Microtubules form a spindle.

Protein complexes connect chromosomes to spindle fibres at centromeres.

Sister chromatids separate.

Nuclei reform or remain reorganised depending on the lineage.

Cytokinesis divides the cell.

This system is so elaborate that it can appear irreducibly eukaryotic.

Its components have deeper ancestry.

Tubulin relatives occur in bacteria and archaea.

SMC proteins organise chromosomes across all domains of life.

Archaeal histones package DNA.

ATPases and DNA-repair proteins were inherited and duplicated.

Eukaryogenesis remodelled those systems into a new chromosome architecture.

LECA already possessed the major machinery for mitosis.

Comparative reconstruction also indicates:

linear chromosomes,

centromeres,

telomeres,

nucleosomal chromatin,

chromosome-remodelling enzymes,

and complex cell-cycle control.

The origin of linear chromosomes creates a problem circular bacterial chromosomes largely avoid.

A DNA polymerase cannot fully copy the very end of a conventional linear DNA molecule.

Eukaryotes solve this with **telomeres** and telomerase or alternative mechanisms.

Chromosome ends become specialised structures rather than broken DNA.

Centromeres solve a different problem.

They provide regions where segregation machinery can assemble.

Their DNA sequences vary dramatically among eukaryotes, but the functional need is ancient.

The duplication of chromatin proteins adds another layer of control.

Archaeal histones provide a plausible ancestral starting point.

Eukaryotic histones diversified into H2A, H2B, H3 and H4 families.

Nucleosomes became substrates for chemical modifications that alter chromatin accessibility.

Genome packaging became gene regulation.

This is a pattern we have seen repeatedly.

An old molecular function expands.

Duplication creates specialised copies.

New interactions integrate them.

The resulting system becomes qualitatively different without requiring every component to appear at once.

A larger, intron-rich genome also creates a timing problem.

In bacteria and many archaea, transcription and translation can be tightly coupled.

A ribosome can begin translating an RNA while it is still being transcribed.

Eukaryotic messenger RNAs often need extensive processing first.

Introns have to be removed.

Ends are modified.

Quality-control systems inspect the transcript.

The nucleus physically separates transcription from translation.

That separation may have allowed increasingly complex RNA processing to evolve without ribosomes attacking unfinished transcripts.

Whether introns helped drive nuclear compartmentalisation or merely benefited from it remains debated.

But by LECA, the two systems were deeply integrated.

The eukaryotic genome was not merely larger.

It had become **genes in pieces**, managed by one of the most intricate molecular machines in the cell.

The spliceosome.

## Research / fact-check notes
- LECA reconstructions support a nucleus, linear chromosomes, centromeres/telomeres, nucleosomal chromatin, mitosis and complex cytoskeletal segregation machinery.
- Archaeal histones, SMC proteins and prokaryotic tubulin/actin relatives provide ancestral components; eukaryotic systems underwent major duplication/specialisation.
- PLOS Biology (2024) LECA reconstruction synthesises these features.
- The causal relationship among introns, nuclear compartmentalisation and translation separation remains debated.

**Decision:** LECA's chromosome-control machinery is established by comparative genomics; detailed FECA-to-LECA assembly order remains uncertain.
