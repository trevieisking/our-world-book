# CHAPTER SEVEN — BECOMING COMPLEX
## Page 219 — Controlled manuscript draft v0.1

### MULTICELLULARITY IS NOT ONE INVENTION

A cell divides.

The daughters separate.

That is the ordinary unicellular outcome.

Now change one step.

The daughters remain attached.

A filament forms.

A sheet.

A cluster.

A branching thallus.

Multicellularity can begin with something as simple as **not separating**.

Or it can begin differently.

Independent cells can aggregate after division.

A multinucleate cell can compartmentalise itself.

Cells can live embedded in a shared extracellular matrix.

Because there are several physical routes, multicellularity has evolved repeatedly.

A 2023 survey identified at least **45 independently evolved multicellular eukaryotic lineages** under explicit criteria and argued that the true number may be considerably higher.

Most are relatively simple.

Complex multicellularity — bodies with persistent developmental programmes and specialised cell types — evolved far fewer times.

Animals.

Land plants.

Several algal lineages.

Fungi.

Each solved the problem differently.

Recent work continues to reinforce convergence.

A 2026 review of photosynthetic eukaryotes compares red algae, brown algae and plants whose complex multicellular bodies evolved independently from unicellular ancestors.

They often face the same physical problems.

Adhesion.

Cell polarity.

Long-distance transport.

Developmental patterning.

Yet the underlying molecular solutions can differ.

This matters because multicellularity should not be described as a single evolutionary rung.

There was no moment when “eukaryotes became multicellular.”

Different eukaryotes became multicellular at different times for different reasons.

Some later returned toward simpler forms.

The fossil record preserves some early experiments.

**Qingshania**, from rocks around 1.63 billion years old in North China, has been interpreted as a multicellular photosynthetic eukaryote and possibly an archaeplastid.

If that interpretation is correct, multicellularity reaches deep into the Palaeoproterozoic.

Its exact affinity remains debated.

A much stronger later landmark is **Bangiomorpha pubescens**.

This fossil consists of multicellular filaments with features interpreted as cell differentiation and sexual reproduction in a red-algal lineage.

Precise dating places it at about **1.047 billion years ago**.

Because Bangiomorpha belongs close to crown red algae, it demonstrates that a recognisably modern form of complex algal multicellularity and eukaryotic photosynthesis existed by that time.

But algae are not ancestors of animals.

Their multicellularity is an independent experiment.

This distinction is essential.

A multicellular red alga does not show that animal-style development had already evolved.

It shows that eukaryotic cells repeatedly possess enough regulatory and cytoskeletal capacity to construct bodies.

Why does multicellularity keep reappearing?

Size can protect cells from grazers.

A larger body can reach resources unavailable to one cell.

Cells can specialise.

One layer captures light.

Another anchors.

One cell digests.

Another reproduces.

A colony can create internal chemical gradients.

Cells can share extracellular structures too expensive for each to make alone.

But cooperation creates a cheating problem.

A cell that takes resources without contributing can reproduce faster inside the group.

Complex multicellularity therefore requires mechanisms that align cell fitness with organism fitness.

Clonal development provides one powerful solution.

If a body begins from one cell, most cells are close genetic relatives.

Cheating becomes less favourable.

Development can impose reproductive bottlenecks.

Some cells give up direct reproduction entirely.

That is the conceptual leap.

A multicellular organism is not simply many cells touching.

It is a new evolutionary individual.

And long before animals become abundant, eukaryotes experiment repeatedly with how to build one.

## Research / fact-check notes
- Lamża (2023), *Biological Reviews*: at least 45 independently evolved multicellular eukaryotic lineages under explicit criteria; number depends on definition.
- 2026 *Current Biology* review: complex multicellularity in major photosynthetic eukaryote clades evolved convergently.
- *Bangiomorpha pubescens* is dated to 1.047 +0.013/−0.017 Ga and is a strong crown red-algal/multicellular calibration.
- *Qingshania* (~1.63 Ga) has been interpreted as multicellular archaeplastid/eukaryote, but affinity is less secure.

**Decision:** multicellularity is treated as recurrent/convergent, with fossil claims assigned different confidence levels.
