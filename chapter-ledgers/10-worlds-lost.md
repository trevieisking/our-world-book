## Evidence boundaries
- A local last occurrence is not automatically a global extinction or an exact extinction time.
- Stratigraphic changes, incomplete sampling and biological losses require separate tests.
- Establishing extinction, estimating its tempo and identifying its cause are distinct tasks.
- Models demonstrate conditional mechanisms; they do not directly observe ancient events.
- Regional or taxon-specific findings must not be silently globalised.
- Proxy-based temperature and oxygen reconstructions do not describe every habitat identically.
- Newly published age-depth estimates retain uncertainties and do not silently replace the reference timescale.
- Kellwasser and Hangenberg represent distinct intervals; proposed drivers cannot be transferred between them without evidence.
- Spore abnormalities do not directly measure ancient ozone; phosphorus trends do not uniquely identify their source.
- Meishan marine chronology and Sydney Basin floral chronology cannot be collapsed into a single global extinction instant.
- A dated intrusion is not a direct measurement of emitted gas; temporal association and specific killing mechanisms require distinct evidence.
- Boron-based acidification reconstructions differ in archives and timing; conditional physiological or erosion models do not prove one universal cause of death.
- Local ecological complexity, species diversity and environmental stability are different recovery measures; modelled internal oscillations do not exclude every external forcing.
