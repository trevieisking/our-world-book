## Sources and access
- **S11-45:** Haile-Selassie, Y. (2001), *Late Miocene hominids from the Middle Awash, Ethiopia*. Nature 412, 178–181. https://doi.org/10.1038/35084063 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/35084063 . Publisher abstract checked 2026-10-04. It reports material dated 5.2–5.8 Ma, wooded palaeoenvironment association, derived dental characters with primitive dental/postcranial features, and the original interpretation of the pedal phalanx. The paper's then-current “earliest” language is treated historically, not as a current absolute.
- **S11-46:** WoldeGabriel, G. et al. (2001), *Geology and palaeontology of the Late Miocene Middle Awash valley, Afar rift, Ethiopia*. Nature 412, 175–178. https://doi.org/10.1038/35084058 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/35084058 . Publisher abstract checked 2026-10-04. It dates the principal older finds to 5.54–5.77 Ma and reconstructs relatively wet, wooded environments.
- **S11-47:** Haile-Selassie, Y., Suwa, G. & White, T.D. (2004), *Late Miocene teeth from Middle Awash, Ethiopia, and early hominid dental evolution*. Science 303, 1503–1505. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1092978 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15001775/ . Indexed original abstract checked 2026-10-04. The authors distinguish *Ar. kadabba* from *Ar. ramidus* on primitive dental morphology/wear and caution against inferring extensive late-Miocene diversity from available samples.
- **S11-48:** DeSilva, J.M. et al. (2019), *One small step: A review of Plio-Pleistocene hominin foot evolution*. American Journal of Physical Anthropology 168(S67), 63–140. https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.23750 ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajpa.23750 . Review text checked 2026-10-04. It notes the 5.2 Ma fourth proximal pedal phalanx and cautions that dorsal canting also occurs in non-bipedal *Ekembo*, so more postcranial material is needed to assess *kadabba* bipedality.

## Checks
The page separates taxonomic naming, dental functional inference, palaeoenvironment and locomotor inference. The toe is not presented as a uniquely diagnostic bipedal feature in isolation, and wooded association is not converted into a single-cause model for the origin of bipedality.
