# Chapter 4 support register — pages 121–128

This register is authoritative for pages 121–128 until the large book-wide ledgers are batch-reconciled.

## Evidence checkpoints
- **121 — why RNA matters:** RNA can carry sequence information and can catalyse chemical reactions; the ribosomal peptidyl-transferase centre is fundamentally RNA-catalysed. These facts strongly motivate an ancient RNA-rich evolutionary phase but do not directly prove a historically pure RNA-only world. **Status: ESTABLISHED functions / STRONGLY SUPPORTED framework.**
- **122 — sequence to catalytic function:** RNA sequence determines folding and can generate selectable catalytic function. Natural and laboratory ribozymes demonstrate broad catalytic capacity; in vitro evolution explores chemical possibility but uses modern experimental selection/amplification. **Status: ESTABLISHED capacity / laboratory pathway not historical evidence.**
- **123 — prebiotic nucleotide synthesis:** experimentally plausible routes exist to activated pyrimidine ribonucleotides and to common precursor chemistry feeding nucleotide, amino-acid and lipid building blocks. A continuous natural Hadean production network from feedstocks to an evolving RNA population remains unestablished. **Status: ESTABLISHED partial routes / UNCERTAIN integrated history.**
- **124 — non-enzymatic copying:** activated nucleotides and oligonucleotides can undergo template-directed RNA copying without protein enzymes; activation, sequence biases, degradation, and integration of compatible conditions remain active constraints. **Status: ESTABLISHED proof of principle / incomplete system.**
- **125 — strand separation:** stable RNA duplexes create product inhibition. Attwater et al. (2025) showed that activated trinucleotide substrates plus coupled pH/freeze–thaw cycles can inhibit reannealing and enable repeated/exponential RNA replication by an artificial polymerase ribozyme, including partial self-replication and open-ended sequence-pool amplification. **Status: ESTABLISHED experimental mechanism / not autonomous abiogenesis.**
- **126 — fidelity and evolution:** Papastavrou, Horning & Joyce (2024) showed that a sufficiently high-fidelity RNA polymerase ribozyme can propagate and evolve a functional hammerhead ribozyme, while lower fidelity loses function. The workflow used laboratory selection and modern amplification steps. **Status: ESTABLISHED RNA-catalysed Darwinian evolution in a laboratory system.**
- **127 — QT45:** Gianni et al. (2026) reported a 45-nt RNA polymerase ribozyme that catalyses general RNA-templated synthesis with activated trinucleotide substrates in mildly alkaline eutectic ice. It produced its complementary sequence at 94.1% per-nucleotide fidelity using a random triplet pool and a copy of itself using defined substrates; both reactions reached about 0.2% yield after 72 days. The two component reactions required for self-replication are therefore experimentally demonstrated in one small ribozyme, but a sustained autonomous self-replication/evolution cycle from simple prebiotic feedstocks was not. **Status: MAJOR ESTABLISHED 2026 proof of principle / complete autonomous cycle UNRESOLVED.**
- **128 — RNA-world boundary:** RNA solves several coupled origin problems more parsimoniously than a simultaneous modern DNA/protein system, but prebiotic supply, activation, polymerisation, energy, compartmentalisation, parasites, and the transition to RNA–peptide/protein/DNA systems remain unresolved. Hybrid/co-evolution scenarios remain viable. **Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED framework / historical sequence UNCERTAIN.**
