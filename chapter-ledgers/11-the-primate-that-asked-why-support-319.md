## Sources and access
- **S11-49:** Lovejoy, C.O., Suwa, G., Spurlock, L., Asfaw, B. & White, T.D. (2009), *The pelvis and femur of Ardipithecus ramidus: the emergence of upright walking*. Science 326, 71e1–71e6. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19810197/ . Original indexed abstract checked 2026-10-04. It reports pelvic/femoral characters for upright bipedal walking and movement in trees and describes bipedality as more primitive than in later *Australopithecus*.
- **S11-50:** Lovejoy, C.O., Latimer, B., Suwa, G., Asfaw, B. & White, T.D. (2009), *Combining prehension and propulsion: the foot of Ardipithecus ramidus*. Science 326, 72e1–72e8. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19810198/ . Original indexed abstract checked 2026-10-04. It documents the widely abducted hallux and the original authors' interpretation of plantar rigidity and climbing mechanics. Their broader common-ancestor model is treated as an interpretation, not a fact.
- **S11-51:** Simpson, S.W. et al. (2019), *Ardipithecus ramidus postcrania from the Gona Project area, Afar Regional State, Ethiopia*. Journal of Human Evolution 129, 1–45. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhevol.2018.12.005 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30904038/ . Original abstract and publisher summary checked 2026-10-04. It reinforces mixed terrestrial bipedality/arboreal clambering and reports greater ankle/hallux bipedal adaptation than previously recognised.
- **S11-52:** Prang, T.C. et al. (2025), *Ardipithecus ramidus ankle provides evidence for African ape-like vertical climbing in the earliest hominins*. Communications Biology 8, 1454. https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-025-08711-7 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-08711-7 . Open publisher article checked 2026-10-04. The authors find talar similarities to chimpanzees/gorillas consistent with vertical climbing and plantigrade quadrupedalism, plus derived push-off features, and argue against the earlier generalized-arboreal common-ancestor model.

## Checks
