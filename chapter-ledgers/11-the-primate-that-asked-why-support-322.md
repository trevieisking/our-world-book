## Sources and access
- **S11-61:** Leakey, M.D. & Hay, R.L. (1979), *Pliocene footprints in the Laetolil Beds at Laetoli, northern Tanzania*. Nature 278, 317–323. https://doi.org/10.1038/278317a0 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/278317a0 . Original publisher abstract and article metadata checked 2026-10-04. It records the Pliocene footprint discovery and geological setting; later studies provide the more specific modern locomotor/taxonomic interpretations.
- **S11-62:** Bennett, M.R., Reynolds, S.C., Morse, S.A. & Budka, M. (2016), *Laetoli's lost tracks: 3D generated mean shape and missing footprints*. Scientific Reports 6, 21916. https://doi.org/10.1038/srep21916 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26902912/ . Original indexed abstract checked 2026-10-04. The study computationally separates information from the composite G2/G3 trail and restores G3 footprint morphology for comparison.
- **S11-63:** Masao, F.T. et al. (2016), *New footprints from Laetoli (Tanzania) provide evidence for marked body size variation in early hominins*. eLife 5, e19568. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.19568 ; https://elifesciences.org/articles/19568 . Open primary article checked 2026-10-04. Site S tracks are on the 3.66 Ma palaeosurface and interpreted as additional bipedal individuals; large size and sexual-dimorphism/social implications remain conditional on taxonomic and estimation assumptions.
- **S11-64:** McNutt, E.J. et al. (2021), *Footprint evidence of early hominin locomotor diversity at Laetoli, Tanzania*. Nature 600, 468–471. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04187-7 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04187-7 . Open primary article checked 2026-10-04. Re-excavation and 3D comparison support a bipedal hominin Site A trackmaker distinct in foot proportions/gait from Site G and lead the authors to infer a minimum of two hominin taxa.

## Checks
The page distinguishes trace behaviour from anatomical capacity and taxonomic attribution. Site G/S are not made into individually identified *A. afarensis* persons, and Site A is not assigned an invented species. The same palaeosurface does not by itself prove social interaction among the trackmakers. Research preceded prose; read back before page 323 research.
