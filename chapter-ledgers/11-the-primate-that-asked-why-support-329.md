## Sources and access
- **S11-86:** McPherron, S.P. et al. (2010), *Evidence for stone-tool-assisted consumption of animal tissues before 3.39 million years ago at Dikika, Ethiopia*. Reports the original cut/percussion interpretation and stratigraphic age constraint.
- **S11-87:** Domínguez-Rodrigo, M., Pickering, T.R. & Bunn, H.T. (2010), *Configurational approach to identifying the earliest hominin butchers*. PNAS 107, 20929–20934. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3000273/ . Open full text checked 2026-10-04. Argues that trampling damage cannot be excluded and therefore rejects the marks as secure butchery evidence.
- **S11-88:** Thompson, J.C. et al. (2015), *Taphonomy of fossils from the hominin-bearing deposits at Dikika, Ethiopia*. Journal of Human Evolution 86, 112–135. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhevol.2015.06.013 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26277305/ . Indexed original abstract checked 2026-10-04. Finds common local trampling damage generally smaller and characteristically different from DIK-55 and concludes trampling is not the most parsimonious explanation.
- **S11-89:** Courtenay, L.A. et al. (2020), *Artificial intelligence provides greater accuracy in the classification of modern and ancient bone surface modifications*. Scientific Reports 10. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7606445/ . Open article checked 2026-10-04. Several illustrated Dikika marks were classified as trampling or tooth modifications; used only to demonstrate continuing method dependence, not as an unquestioned final diagnosis.
