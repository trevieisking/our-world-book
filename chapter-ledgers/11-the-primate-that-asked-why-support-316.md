## Sources and access
- **S11-36:** Brunet, M. et al. (2002), *A new hominid from the Upper Miocene of Chad, Central Africa*. Nature 418, 145–151. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature00879 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/nature00879 . Original publisher abstract and article metadata checked 2026-10-04. The paper reports the cranium, jaws and teeth and the original mosaic diagnosis. Nature published a narrow dental-description erratum on 15 August 2002; no affected wording is used here.
- **S11-37:** Zollikofer, C.P.E. et al. (2005), *Virtual cranial reconstruction of Sahelanthropus tchadensis*. Nature 434, 755–759. https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03397 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/nature03397 . Publisher abstract checked 2026-10-04. The basicranial inference is presented as compatibility with upright bipedality, not as a preserved stride.
- **S11-38:** Lebatard, A.-E. et al. (2008), *Cosmogenic nuclide dating of Sahelanthropus tchadensis and Australopithecus bahrelghazali: Mio-Pliocene hominids from Chad*. The manuscript uses “close to seven million years” rather than treating a sediment measurement as an exact birth date.

## Checks
The page deliberately separates taxonomic diagnosis, locomotor inference, geological age and direct ancestry. The current 2026 analysis is included without rewriting the 2022 interpretation or the 2024/2025 reply as though either never existed. “Earliest” is used only when attached to a source's interpretation, not as an immutable ranking.
