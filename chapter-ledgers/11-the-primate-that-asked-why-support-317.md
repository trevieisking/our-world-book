## Sources and access
- **S11-42:** Senut, B., Pickford, M., Gommery, D., Mein, P., Cheboi, K. & Coppens, Y. (2001), *First hominid from the Miocene (Lukeino Formation, Kenya)*. Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences - Series IIA - Earth and Planetary Science 332, 137–144. https://doi.org/10.1016/S1251-8050(01)01529-4 ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1251805001015294 . Original article abstract checked 2026-10-04. It reports 13 fossils from at least five individuals, sediments at about 6 Ma, bipedal interpretation of femora and arboreal indications from humerus/manual phalanx. The page does not inherit the paper's stronger phylogenetic claims as settled fact.
- **S11-43:** Richmond, B.G. & Jungers, W.L. (2008), *Orrorin tugenensis femoral morphology and the evolution of hominin bipedalism*. Science 319, 1662–1665. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1154197 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18356526/ . Indexed original abstract checked 2026-10-04. The authors infer bipedality and report strongest similarity to *Australopithecus*/*Paranthropus*, not special affinity to *Homo*.
- **S11-44:** Almécija, S. et al. (2013), *The femur of Orrorin tugenensis exhibits morphometric affinities with both Miocene apes and later hominins*. Nature Communications 4, 2888. https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms3888 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms3888 . Publisher article and abstract checked 2026-10-04. The analysis places *Orrorin* intermediate in shape space and reconstructs both living great-ape and hominin femoral evolution away from a more primitive morphology represented by some fossil apes.

## Checks
The manuscript avoids a ladder model: “intermediate” refers to comparative morphology, not a claim that *Orrorin* is literally halfway between a modern ape and a human. The age, bipedal inference, arboreal evidence and proposed lineage position remain distinct. Research preceded prose; read back before page 318 research.
