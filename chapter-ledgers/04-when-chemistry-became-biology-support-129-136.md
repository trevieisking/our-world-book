## Evidence checkpoints
- **129 — why compartments matter:** spatial localisation keeps catalytic benefits associated with the molecular systems that produce them, limits access by parasitic replicators and permits selection among molecular collectives. Lipid membranes are one route; pores, surfaces, ice and phase-separated droplets are alternatives/complements. **Status: ESTABLISHED evolutionary/physical principle; first historical compartment UNKNOWN.**
- **130 — spontaneous membrane assembly:** suitable amphiphiles spontaneously form bilayers/vesicles through ordinary physical chemistry. Fatty acids have multiple plausible abiotic sources but local Hadean concentrations are uncertain. Mineral surfaces and mixed amphiphiles can promote or stabilise assembly. 2025 cysteine/thioester chemistry and 2026 organocatalytic chemistry provide additional routes from simpler precursors toward protocell-like membranes. **Status: ESTABLISHED self-assembly / STRONGLY SUPPORTED chemical plausibility / historical route UNCERTAIN.**
- **131 — permeability trade-off:** fatty-acid membranes can admit activated nucleotides and short oligomers while retaining longer genetic polymers, allowing internal copying without modern transport proteins. Permeability depends on composition, temperature, ions and solute identity. **Status: ESTABLISHED in model systems.**
- **132 — RNA/membrane compatibility:** free Mg²⁺ can destabilise fatty-acid membranes and degrade RNA while promoting copying chemistry. Citrate chelation experimentally mitigates both problems while permitting non-enzymatic RNA copying inside vesicles. Clay-associated RNA can be encapsulated during vesicle formation. **Status: ESTABLISHED experimental workaround / historical citrate-clay route UNCERTAIN.**
- **133 — protocell competition:** encapsulated nucleic acids can create osmotic effects that drive preferential fatty-acid uptake; encapsulated catalytic chemistry can also generate membrane-active products that increase vesicle growth. **Status: ESTABLISHED physical/catalytic fitness coupling in model protocells.**
- **134 — division and environmental machinery:** fatty-acid vesicle growth can produce thread-like morphologies that divide under modest shear while retaining/distributing RNA. Freeze–thaw cycles can enable RNA exchange and persistence of self-replicating ribozymes across vesicle populations. **Status: ESTABLISHED laboratory mechanisms / exact early-Earth implementation UNKNOWN.**
- **135 — sequence-to-compartment fitness:** Baba et al. (2026) demonstrated that defined peptide sequences alter fatty-acid-vesicle growth and generate a sequence–fitness landscape with epistatic effects. Peptides were supplied experimentally and were not genetically encoded inside the vesicles. **Status: MAJOR ESTABLISHED 2026 coupling / hereditary encoding step UNRESOLVED.**
- **136 — protocell boundary:** 2026 work shows new bottom-up routes to protocell assembly, de novo membrane generation and multi-module synthetic cells. Systems that use evolved enzymes, recombinant translation machinery or supplied catalysts are not prebiotic reconstructions. No complete autonomous transition from simple plausible geochemical feedstocks to an open-ended evolving cellular lineage has been demonstrated. **Status: ESTABLISHED current experimental frontier.**
