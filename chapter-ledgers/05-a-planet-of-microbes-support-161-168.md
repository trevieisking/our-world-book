# Chapter 5 support register — pages 161–168

This register is authoritative for pages 161–168 until the large book-wide ledgers are batch-reconciled.

## Evidence checkpoints
- **161 — primary production/carbon fixation:** early ecosystems required conversion of inorganic carbon into biomass. Carbon-isotope fractionation supports biological carbon processing but usually does not uniquely identify one fixation pathway. **Status: ESTABLISHED ecosystem function / pathway-specific reconstruction UNCERTAIN.**
- **162 — methane:** very 13C-depleted methane in Dresser fluid inclusions has been interpreted as microbial methanogenesis before ~3.46 Ga, but abiotic hydrothermal methane and environmental-context revisions prevent treating the assignment as uniquely proven. Methanogenesis is more securely indicated later in the Archean. **Status: PLAUSIBLE/STRONGLY ARGUED at Dresser / stronger later record.**
- **163 — sulfur:** Dresser sulfur-isotope evidence strongly supports biological sulfur cycling; sulfate reduction, elemental-sulfur reduction/disproportionation and related pathways remain partly non-unique. 2026 Isua results strengthen an older sulfur-respiration interpretation. **Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED broad cycling / pathway details ACTIVE FRONTIER.**
- **164 — iron:** Fe2+-rich anoxic Archean oceans made biological iron oxidation/reduction energetically accessible. Iron-isotope and sedimentary records are consistent with early biological iron cycling, while timing and ancestry of photoferrotrophy remain debated. **Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED availability/cycling / specific early pathway UNCERTAIN.**
- **165 — nitrogen:** secure nitrogen-isotope evidence for biological N2 fixation reaches to about 3.2 Ga. Older Palaeoarchaean nitrogen in carbonaceous material supports biological nitrogen burial but does not securely identify the fixation pathway. **Status: ESTABLISHED by ~3.2 Ga / older pathway evidence UNCERTAIN.**
- **166 — syntrophy:** modern anaerobic ecosystems demonstrate thermodynamically coupled cross-feeding. Palaeoarchaean rocks support ecosystem recycling but do not directly fossilise named syntrophic partnerships. **Status: ESTABLISHED ecological mechanism / ancient partnership INFERENTIAL.**
- **167 — mat zonation:** modern microbial mats show steep light, pH and redox gradients over millimetres and provide a robust physical analogue for structured ancient communities. Modern taxa and oxygen/sulfate regimes cannot be back-projected directly. **Status: ESTABLISHED physical ecology / ancient membership UNKNOWN.**
- **168 — planetary microbial feedbacks:** combined evidence supports biological participation in Archean carbon, sulfur, iron, methane and eventually nitrogen cycles before atmospheric oxygenation. Global flux magnitudes remain uncertain. **Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED qualitative biogeochemical role.**

## Best sources
- Ueno et al. (2006), *Nature*, very 13C-depleted methane in Dresser fluid inclusions interpreted as microbial methanogenesis.
- Archean-life/biosignature reviews through 2020–2025 for methane, sulfur and iron evidence and uncertainty.
- Dresser multiple-sulfur-isotope work and later environmental syntheses.
- Stüeken et al. (2015), *Nature*, nitrogen-isotope evidence for biological N2 fixation by ~3.2 Ga.
- Garcia et al. (2020), *Geobiology*, nitrogenase evolutionary history and metal-specificity uncertainty.
- 2026 Archean nitrogen-cycle review, biological nitrogen burial versus pathway-specific evidence.
- 2026 Isua carbon/sulfur isotope work, interpreted anaerobic iron- and sulfur-linked respiration.
- Modern syntrophy and microbial-mat literature used only as functional analogues.
