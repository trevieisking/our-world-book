# Chapter 3 support register — pages 093–100

This register is authoritative for pages 093–100 until the large book-wide ledgers are batch-reconciled.

## Evidence checkpoints
- **093 — Earth–Moon dynamics:** tidal exchange transfers angular momentum from Earth's rotation to the Moon's orbit, slowing Earth's rotation and increasing lunar distance over geological time. Exact Hadean day length is model-dependent; the oldest proposed Archean constraints are ambiguous.
- **094 — tide amplitude:** stronger lunar forcing does not translate directly into one global tide height. Basin geometry, water depth, resonance, coastline shape and rotation rate strongly control actual tides. Early-ocean modelling does not require universal supertides.
- **095 — basins and sea level:** hotter Archean mantle likely produced thicker oceanic crust and different basin capacities, while continental extent and elevation were also changing. No unique Hadean global coastline or sea-level curve is accepted.
- **096 — shore zones:** direct tidal/estuarine sedimentary systems are preserved by ~3.2 Ga in the Moodies Group. Hadean shorelines are physically expected where land met ocean but are not directly preserved.
- **097 — concentration versus dilution:** evaporation, sediment pores, mineral adsorption, freezing, wet–dry cycles and confined flow can concentrate solutes relative to the open ocean. Relevance to prebiotic chemistry is plausible but does not identify life's birthplace.
- **098 — hydrothermal/redox gradients:** early hydrothermal systems generated temperature, pH, redox and compositional gradients, but global early oceans were anoxic and should not be drawn as modern oxygenated vent systems.
- **099 — interfaces:** river mouths, shorelines, pores, hydrothermal mixing zones and other boundaries create disequilibria and cycling that can accelerate or redirect chemistry.
- **100 — synthesis:** the early ocean is treated as a network of distinct environments rather than one homogeneous chemical soup.
