# Chapter 3 support register — pages 101–108

This register is authoritative for pages 101–108 until the large book-wide ledgers are batch-reconciled.

## Evidence checkpoints
- **101 — carbon:** early carbon cycled among atmosphere, ocean, rock and reduced organic forms; exact Hadean pCO₂ and ocean pH remain uncertain.
- **102 — nitrogen:** N₂ can be abundant while reactive nitrogen is scarce. Lightning, impacts, hydrothermal chemistry and mineral-mediated reactions provide plausible abiotic fixation pathways, but global Hadean fluxes remain uncertain.
- **103 — phosphorus:** phosphate availability is environment-dependent. Recent experiments/modeling indicate Fe²⁺-rich anoxic seawater may dissolve far more phosphate than older models predicted; reduced meteoritic phosphorus also supplies reactive forms.
- **104 — sulfur:** Archean marine sulfate was far below modern levels; reduced sulfur and sulfur-isotope systematics are major redox tracers.
- **105 — iron:** anoxic seawater could contain far more dissolved Fe²⁺ than modern surface oceans; oxidation and sulfide formation remove it into minerals.
- **106 — silica:** Archean seawater was substantially more silica-rich than modern seawater because of intense water–rock interaction and the absence of modern biological silica sinks.
- **107 — sediment archive:** chemical sediments preserve transformed records of seawater chemistry, but diagenesis and metamorphism can alter primary signals.
- **108 — availability:** elemental inventory and chemical availability are explicitly separated.
